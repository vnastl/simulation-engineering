## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): knowledg, family, gooddeg, mother, careers, school*

**Interviewer —** So when and why do you think that you decided to design and technology?

**Subject —** Erm, well when I was at school it was just the easiest subject for me, and so it was sort of the natural thing to go and do it at university. It was a bit of a hard decision because a lot of the design and technology courses, you have to have an art foundation, but erm this one you didn’t so.

**Interviewer —** Did you have much careers advice or anything like that when you was at school?

**Subject —** Erm, not – you could get careers advice but it was absolutely useless, so I didn’t really bother.

**Interviewer —** Ok, did you do much career research yourself, like looking into what’s involved in design and technology?

**Subject —** Not at all, I probably should have done some. I wasn’t sure what I wanted to study and basically my mum was looking at courses for me really because I couldn’t be bothered, which is awful. But erm, she was looking. And I didn’t, because I had to come to an interview here, I found that a bit more (encouraging?) because I didn’t know before I came what it was going to be like so if I hadn’t had an interview I probably wouldn’t have much of an idea.

**Interviewer —** What do you think it is that appeals to you about design and technology then?

**Subject —** Erm, its just that its not just sitting around learning. You’re making things as well, its much more of a sociable course than other ones. Like we, I know everyone on the course because we’re always making things, we’re never just sat in a lecture theatre, we’re always doing something, which makes it much more interesting.

**Interviewer —** When you decided to do design and technology, did you realise that it was so male dominated?

**Subject —** Erm, yes, I knew it was. Well all of the courses I had interviews, it was always an issue, when I went to the interview I could tell, like a lot of the people interviewing me, particularly at other universities, they were desperate to get me on the course because they needed to balance out their numbers. I’ve always felt like I don’t know if I would have got on this course if I’d been a bloke. My interview wasn’t particularly design orientated, they weren’t really looking at my work, we were just chatting about my CV and what I did out of time, and I wasn’t really, I sort of thought I wasn’t going to be accepted whatever because, to make up their numbers.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that girls are given enough opportunity and enough information about the subject to make a reasonable decision not to take it?

**Subject —** Yes erm, I don’t know. Once I’d decided on a few courses, I got sent a load of literature from the courses about girls doing engineering and stuff and about grants that are available and. So once I’d applied there was a lot of information, but you had to apply first to get it. It depends as well how designs conducted at your school, we only did like technology, so engineering, I don’t think anyone knew much about engineering really, it just depends like how you’re taught really. I just didn’t even think about engineering, I didn’t know what it was so it wasn’t really an option.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): pleased, Mconfid, Fhelp, interper, supppers, groupwk, coursewk, practwk*

**Interviewer —** You’ve said a bit about this already, but what do you think are the advantages of being a woman in a male dominated environment?

**Subject —** I think you do get preferential treatment, erm we have like, our technicians are more inclined to help the girls in workshops and stuff. But on the downside you’re in a minority, being a minority, if you have like group projects or somethings, you’re likely to get shouted down or you’ve got to be quite strong-minded person to get your views across and stuff, but the main advantage is extra help and people, and like I think there probably is more chance of getting a job afterwards. Like coming to university they were trying to balance out their numbers and…

**Interviewer —** Has the course then been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Erm, I didn’t know much about it to be honest before I came, but it was harder than I thought it would be, I did struggle at first doing my work, but I’m settled into it now and its good. I’m mean I like working hard, I’m used to it now and I get bored if I don’t have designs to do and stuff, so yeah its alright.

**Interviewer —** What would you say are the best and the worst things so far?

**Subject —** Er, I like the making, making things, but I’m not so keen, just the amount of coursework that we get is phenomenal (lost words) it’s like we’ve got to hand in 6 pieces of course work and an exam in 2 days during, at the end of July, is going to be a bit of a nightmare, but you’ve just got to work out how you’re going to deal with them.

**Interviewer —** Ok are you happy with all the career choices you’ve made so far? Or would you do anything differently if you went through it all again?

**Subject —** I probably, I don’t know if I would’ve took my course. I found it really hard in the first few months, but I’m not the kind of person who sort of drops out. I think I’m a bit of a perseverer. So erm, I sort of decided that this is something, I’ll feel really prod of myself once I finished it because it is hard work. It’d be easy just to say oh I’d rather do something a bit easier, but erm yes. I don’t actually, I don’t regret it at all really. I think its good for me, and whatever I’m meant to do I’ll do sort of thing.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, experien, money, educatio, jobdecid, industry, finalyr*

**Interviewer —** Do you feel prepared and ready to go on the placement now?

**Subject —** Yes, I went to see my manager last week, so I’ve seen where I’ll be and all the offices, and met her, so it kind of put my mind at rest because it was a bit of an unknown. And next week we’ve got like a welcome day, where we meet all the other placement students, and that, so I’m quite happy about it really. I think they’ve done quite a lot to make sure we feel happy before we go there sort of thing, so.

**Interviewer —** Why did you really decide to go on placement then?

**Subject —** Because, it’ll be nice to work for a year rather than studying. I think I’m too, I don’t feel old enough to do my final year or prepared enough. I’m just not ready to take on all the work really, I want to stall for a year. Its quite nice because we’ve done 2 years of work and the final year’s going to be quite a lot of work, so its quite nice to have a sort of break in the middle. Its interesting to see what it’ll be like in the workplace.

**Interviewer —** Do you think when you come back to uni, you’ll bring anything back from the workplace to help you?

**Subject —** I hope so, I don’t know whether it will or not, but its worth a go. I hope it will, its supposed to. Often we get told we need to do a placement because it’ll help you do your final year and hopefully get some ideas for my final project and stuff, but as I said, I don’t really know what I’ll be doing.

**Interviewer —** What are you most looking forward to about the placement?

**Subject —** Just the independence really. Mostly the fact that I’ll be able to buy a car. Just, I’ve always done little odd jobs at different places, but it’ll be nice to do like a proper job for a bit.

**Interviewer —** And what are you most apprehensive about?

**Subject —** Being able to do the work, that you have the ability to do the work. I don’t know what its going to be, and I don’t want to feel like I can’t do it, but they have, (I felt like it was the same with this course?), and I feel like they’ve found out about me enough and they should know whether I’d be capable, so hopefully I’ll be ok.

**Interviewer —** Did you look at anything like location, training opportunities, and what prospects you might have when you leave?

**Subject —** Erm, I knew it would be quite hard to get a job, so I wasn’t trying to think about location. I sort of said to myself I’d go anywhere, but luckily its just down the road from here so its ideal really, although I said I’d go anywhere, its much nicer to be near here and be where all my friends will be next year. Erm, and I didn’t really look at training and stuff, but since I’ve spoken to the manager, there is quite a lot on offer, and that’s definitely, I mean I’m definitely going to accept the job, like they’ve impressed me that they’ve told me what, there are a lot of opportunities for me to further myself and things so.

**Interviewer —** In terms of your career then, where do you see yourself in 5 years time?

**Subject —** Its probably going to be quite a telling year this year, whether I like it or not. Erm, I’ll wait and see how much I enjoy next year.
