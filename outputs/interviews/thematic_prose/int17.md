## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (12): interest, special, mathsci, knowledg, family, hobbies, gooddeg, mother, father, teacher, nobody, discoura*

**Interviewer —** OK, so we’re going to talk about why you wanted to do engineering in the first place. So why do you think that you decided to study engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, it was basically that I was doing maths and physics at A level, and I was trying to find a degree that would have substantial proportions of that in it (yeah) and basically my physics teacher at A level was really good and I went spoke to him and said, look, these are the bits I’ve enjoyed, what do you suggest? And he suggested civil engineering and I looked into it a bit more and just…

**Interviewer —** Why then did you do maths and physics A levels and not…?

**Subject —** They were just the subjects that at GCSE I enjoyed – I was good at, and I knew I could cope with doing them at a higher level. There were other GCSE subjects that I might have enjoyed but I couldn’t see myself actually managing to complete an A level course. (yes) So, it was a combination of being good and enjoying it.

**Interviewer —** OK, what about your family – were they supportive of your choices?

**Subject —** Yes, they – it’s always – not been expected, but always been a highly favourable option, to go off to university and do something, like, both my older sisters did and yeah, I think.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you do any career research and did you know any – any other engineers to talk to, to speak to – what it was like?

**Subject —** Not really, basically, I knew that having an engineering degree wouldn’t – didn’t just lead you to doing engineering. When, I knew that if I came out with an engineering degree, I could go off and do all sorts of stuff. So to me, the fact of getting a degree was more important than the necessary career afterwards.

**Interviewer —** OK. Is there anything in particular that attracted you to civil engineering rather than say mechanical or electrical engineering?

**Subject —** Civil engineering just sounded more practical to me, and like it made more sense to me, so having something – and I’ve found in some modules its not quite this way, but having something you can visualise and something big like a building, you can really see it’s there, whereas things like mechanical – it all gets a bit technical.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (10): practica, curricul, pleased, relevanc, interper, supplect, supppers, groupwk, friends, practwk*

**Interviewer —** Was there?

**Subject —** So I think, out of the arranged courses – there were like 28, there were 5 girls, which to me, arriving at that, seemed quite a large number. As far as what we’ve been taught, I knew that it was going to be a mix, between practical modules and management modules and that’s what’s come out of it. Some of the practical ones I didn’t foresee as us actually doing, like very much the hydraulics and fluids ones, I hadn’t really expected that so much and I think that’s shown ‘cos those are the ones I struggled more with. Those are the bits of physics I’m not very good with. But apart from that, no, it’s pretty much as I expected.

**Interviewer —** What do you think have been the best and the worst things about your course, so far?

**Subject —** Er, the best is, for me, coming into course without much knowledge about the whole industry, I knew the general – the general aspects, but probably just finding out more about the industry and learning about it as you, as you progress. The worst things – I don’t actually - I don’t think there are any particular aspects that I would stand like and say, oh no those have been awful.

**Interviewer —** OK, yeah – I was going to ask you about personal tutors as well – are you pleased that you’ve got a female personal tutor?

**Subject —** I’m pleased that I’ve got the personal tutor I have – particularly last year, when I was going through all the having a baby stuff, she was – she was quite good at that and I can see how some of the male personal tutors just wouldn’t have really known what to do. So I’m quite glad to have her, but I could see how actually some of the male personal tutors would have been perfectly good at it, but at the end of the day er I was quite glad to have her.

**Interviewer —** Have you had any particularly good or bad experiences of group work?

**Subject —** Erm, there was a bit of a bad exp – well, not a bad experience, but last year when they were starting to mix the groups between courses – so you’d have MENG people, BENG people and ANDN and you’d end up with all – all the ranges of abilities and levels of commitment and that was quite hard trying to get – and it ends up that I think one of the guys, who was in my group for that has – he’s since left, but I think he failed last year and it was very hard getting somebody who had a much lower level of commitment to the course, to – to work effectively in the group (Yeah). You were running around after them, hassling them to do some work but apart from that things have all just gone on quite nicely. The only problem comes when one person doesn’t do the work.

**Interviewer —** OK. Do you think that the university could do anything to help students prepare more for the workplace?

**Subject —** There is CIVSOC, which they organise site visits and that would be very useful. Unfortunately, they go on those site visits on Wednesdays, which is when sport is, so I can’t go on those site visits. Erm, I don’t – I think the department’s set up quite well, apart from that, just to help you. There’s all sorts of people who’ve got practical experience, who you can go and talk to if you want to, er so the only thing to do to help more is to tell CIVSOC to do their visits another time.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that the educational experience and the way you’re taught, reflects the reality of what it’s like in the workplace?

**Subject —** Not hugely. I mean there’s a lot of stuff we’re doing now, like you’re sitting in a lecture hall, you’re writing notes and you can see how the stuff you’re being taught will help you but the – the day to day, what we do, I don’t think it’s anything like what you’d actually be doing in a job.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (5): experien, jobdecid, industry, placloca, noappeal*

**Interviewer —** So, the indecision about the sandwich bit at the minute, is that your choice or the company?

**Subject —** That’s complicated. Er, actually in May last year, I had a baby and so it’s a case of whether I can find a job for a placement year which will allow me to still look after her because I know that if I went onto a site, I’d be expected to be on site for stupid long hours and I wouldn’t be able to look after my daughter. So it’s mostly just that if they can – if my sponsors can offer me a job where I can either be in an office, where I work regular hours, hopefully, live at home so I’ve got a support network, then I’m going into industry. If not – if they can’t offer me that then I’ll stay – just go straight on through. But that – that should be decided in the next couple of weeks.

**Interviewer —** What appeals to you about doing the sandwich course?

**Subject —** It’s mostly to help me have an idea of whether I actually want to go off and work in er the industry when I graduate, or whether I realise, during the year in industry, that actually it’s not for me. I’d rather realise that in one year out, than apply for a job, get a job and then realise after I’ve graduated, that actually it’s not for me. So it’s just to help me make my mind up.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you feel ready and prepared to go on placement at the moment – this stage in the course?

**Subject —** I think it all depends on what job I got sent out to do. Er, I would be extremely nervous and feel slightly under prepared because a lot of people who will be going on their industrial placement worked last summer in industry, so they’ve got 3 months or whatever of experience, and I haven’t got that. I’ve never actually been onto a site but I can’t see myself ending up in a job, next year, if I do go into industry next year, that will require me to go on site all the time. I think it would be more that if I do take a placement year then it will be a placement in an office er I don’t really know what doing – design work or something like that.

**Interviewer —** Were you happy with that choice then?

**Subject —** At that point in time, yes. I’ve come to realise that it was possibly not the best company I could be sponsored by, because they are a very construction based (right) company and for me now that isn’t really what I need to be doing and I can’t see myself actually managing to work on site for them. So, it’s all a bit up in the air and not necessarily the best sort of thing.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you do much research into the organisation?

**Subject —** I tried. But you go onto their web site and it’s a mix. Some companies have an extremely small web site and it doesn’t really tell you much. It tends to highlight the projects that they’ve done and not much else about the company, so it was – it was quite difficult unless you had contacts in the industry, to find anything else out about the company apart from. Eve said that she hoped the placement would give her an opportunity to use instruments, for example in surveying. She didn’t expect group work to be a big deal, as she was used good at this anyway from doing so much team sports. Eve felt that the best thing about going on placement would be the chance to get a feel for the industry and learning things that are impossible to learn in the classroom. She expected the worse thing to be the long hours, giving up student life, and having less time to spend with her daughter. She expected work to be different from being at university because you would have to act professional all the time. She also expected that she would get treated differently to being at university because of her lack of experience. She also thought the fact that she didn’t have a ‘burning desire’ to be an engineer, compared to some other students, might effect how she is treated. In terms of career, Eve had no idea what she would be doing in the future. She had all sorts of ideas, but could only see herself in the engineering industry if she could get a 9-5 job. One of the options she is considering is doing a PGCE in Maths teaching, but she wasn’t sure whether this was a job she would like. She didn’t know what her ideal career would be, and felt like she needed help making decisions. A lot of ideas she has had, have been excluded because they don’t pay enough. Her major concern at the moment is making a decision about the placement year. She felt that she wasn’t in a position for people to help her make decisions at the moment because she literally has no idea about what to do. While, she has enjoyed the degree, she doesn’t think she will enjoy the engineering job at the end. Although she did think that the career prospects in engineering were nothing but good, with lots of different options both in and out of the industry. She felt that her conflict of interests were limiting her choice and career options. If she went through it all again, she wouldn’t get sponsorship, as now when she’s trying to make decisions she has to think about them as well as herself, and that their interests and hers were different. She didn’t know what would happen to the money she had received from the sponsoring company, but was planning to get the advice of her personal tutor and family. Eve described the engineering culture as intense and work-based. That it fits in with non-family oriented people, and that as an employee you are expected to be flexible to the company’s needs. Felt that family life just doesn’t fit with site work, so would have to compromise if staying in industry for office job with regular hours. Also thought that the industry was based on problem-solving. When asked if the conflict between family and work could be resolved in the industry, she thought it would be difficult to overcome the problem because this is how site work is, and couldn’t see how else it could be organised. End.

So, obviously they wanted to know that I’d passed my exams before they did anything, but I haven’t heard back from them yet. I’m gonna ring them up.
