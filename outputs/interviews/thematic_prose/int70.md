## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (0): none*

**Interviewer —** Ok. What sort of proportion of women do they employ here?

**Subject —** I don't know obviously actual statistics, but I know that certainly in my department, there's very few women. The financial department and that sort of thing, which have more women go into it. Certainly the engineering side women are a minority.

**Interviewer —** Do you think it matters that there aren’t more women in engineering?

**Subject —** No, I guess I’ve always known when I chose an engineering career that that's how it would be probably for most of my career and that's not really an issue.

**Interviewer —** Why do you think that women aren't interested then?

**Subject —** I guess that engineering's kind of portrayed us in cars and engines and motors. If you ask most women they’ll see it as that and I guess that what goes alongside that is the A level choices you make are again very male dominated. You need your maths and physics and they just generally don't appeal, in the same way that less men do English as a degree. I think that that will probably always be there, but women that have probably chosen more scientific based A levels will be aware that engineering isn't all about motors and cars and stuff and that, you know, its much more broader than that and that would be the best thing to promote that it is actually broader than that, that there's more available than what people initially think I guess.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (3): pleased, interper, groupwk*

**Interviewer —** The questions that I've got are mainly about how your placement relates to your course and how you're getting on with your placement and stuff. So how useful do you think that your course has been to the work that you’re doing?

**Subject —** I guess whilst I haven’t really used much of my course the things I have used have obviously been very beneficial, and I’ve used in quite a lot of depth. I think the main difference is the course is very … you're just taught it, whereas once you're at work you obviously get to implement it and then you get real life situations where things don't always go according to plan and then you need to adjust and look broader than just the things that you’re taught.

**Interviewer —** Are there any particular skills that you think have been useful?

**Subject —** The stuff that I've done has been mainly design work and I know that some of the things I've been taught at uni have been useful in that and applying that. Erm, mechanics particularly and (inaudible) diagrams.

**Interviewer —** What about communication skills and more generic stuff – has that been useful?

**Subject —** No, to be honest I don't really think that there's been anything from that side of my course that's really benefited me here, but that may be quite a personal thing. Since I've been at work here I've done presentations and been to meetings and suchlike, which I guess some people having done presentations at uni may benefit from that.

**Interviewer —** Is there anything you wish you had learnt before you started the placement?

**Subject —** No I don't think so, I think the course uni is quite well balanced in that we do get to do some practical application of what we're learning, all be it in a controlled environment, so you know what the outcomes gonna be before you do it, but no I felt quite happy that I haven't really been out of my depth.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the group work you do at uni is worthwhile then?

**Subject —** Yeah, you certainly learn a lot from group work at uni but on the unfairness side you don't always get recognition that perhaps you deserve or, you know, you’re all marked on a level as a team whereas I'd say in real life, and certainly from my experiences here, when you're working in a team it's obvious who's doing what because you have your own role outlined from the beginning of what you're in charge of and if you don't do that then, then obviously that's noticed when it's reviewed, whereas if you've done the work, then you'll get the recognition for it.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, experien, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, apptheor*

**Interviewer —** How would you summarise your placement experience so far then?

**Subject —** I'd say it's been a suitable balance of challenges and the work I've done has been interesting, so that’s good, I'd say mostly generally yeah really happy with my development since I’ve started and the opportunities I’ve been given.

**Interviewer —** What would you say are the main benefits of doing the placement? Or do you think that there are any drawbacks to doing a placement?

**Subject —** I wouldn't say there's any drawbacks, initially when I thought about getting a placement obviously I realised it would make my degree longer and that was kind of a negative point, but certainly having been here, I think the benefits far outweigh that, because you have a year’s experience. You’ll go back to university more focused because I know really what I want now. And also going back to university with the experience of having worked and dealt with problems is going to make any final year projects or any setbacks and group work far more easy to deal with.

**Interviewer —** Do you think the experience will help you get a job, but also cope in a new work environment?

**Subject —** Yes, yes. I think it will. It'll certainly help me get a job even elsewhere because you do have one year’s experience and that’s obviously on your CV and that’s quite a sought after thing in that you’ve got some sort of idea of engineering skills in real like.
