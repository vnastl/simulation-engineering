And because, once you've got an engineering degree, you don't have to be an engineer (OK) so I'm keeping it open.

**Interviewer —** Oh good, do you think anything else influenced your decision to study engineering, like your interests or the fact that your dad was in industry or any careers advice that you've had?

**Subject —** My dad tried to put me off it, if anything, because he really hates his job. Erm, careers advice was – well, non-existent really. There was nothing to it. I suppose it was my own decision but I really don't know on what influenced it.

**Interviewer —** OK, when you decided you did want to do engineering, did you do much research into it, or did you speak to any other engineers or anything like that?

**Subject —** Erm, I kind of got in touch with a guy, who got me my placement and basically what he said was, you know, get an engineering degree then you've got so many options that you're not tying yourself down – so go for it.

**Interviewer —** OK, What do you think it is that appeals to you about engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, probably the hands-on ishness. It's not just sat in a lab, you do get to do other things as well.

**Interviewer —** OK, why do you think you decided to do mechanical engineering and not civil engineering or something in one of the other disciplines? Did you ever consider doing any of the others?

**Subject —** I considered electrical, cos I kind of want to do mechatronic, but I couldn't find a uni that did a good mechatronic course, whereas mechanical you get mechatronic elements in it anyway. So it's like if you select those modules, you can get mechatronics basically.

**Interviewer —** Does it ever bother you at all?

**Subject —** Nah, at school like my maths class I was the only girl. In physics there was two of us, you get used to it. I did electronics at GCSE, I was the only girl on that – it really didn't make a difference.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, apptheor, finalyr*

**Interviewer —** OK, Why do you think you wanted to go on placement?

**Subject —** So much fun - Just to get out and do something practical and learn something real rather than sit down and do – I mean the theory is like – it's got to be useful otherwise we wouldn't be learning it, I guess but it does seem a bit irrelevant and our projects, like well, yes, I 'm setting you this project, but we know what the answer is because we did it last year and the year before that and the year before that, whereas in industry, you know, it's something new that you're doing and it's actually got some value to someone.

**Interviewer —** OK, has anyone particularly encouraged or discouraged you from going on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, my tutor told me it'd be good. My dad told me – I think my dad told me it'd be good. Erm, but yeah it was basically people who'd been on it before – they were like you know, it's such a good experience you know. You don't come back with any money but you know so much more, you know, and you're ready to get a job.

**Interviewer —** OK, and what attracted you to them?

**Subject —** Er, well originally and honestly I applied to anyone that looked like it could be fun. I didn't bother on the whole, I thought yeah that'd be a good place and then I went that'll be – you know – it would look good on my CV that, I want to have had an interesting year. And they have so much variety and you know they send you round the different departments – all engineering, so you weren't like spending hours in management or anything, so you got to see different parts of what they did, so – so I wasn't really sure what I want to do when I graduate, so I thought if I go there, get a good experience and then it might – at least it narrows it down like I don't want to do that or I do want to do that.

**Interviewer —** That's really good. What sorts of things are you hoping to get out of your placement then?

**Subject —** Erm, the whole working experience, basically. I really don't know what to expect, because I was like do you know what I'll be doing like – they were like we don't know what projects will be in when you start, so you have to wait and see, but the actual experience of work and real life.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think will be the worst and best things or what are you most looking forward to and most worried about on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, I'm looking forward to just getting in there and actually doing something, and having responsibility and stuff. I'm not really worried about anything, at the moment. I'm sure when I get closer I will be, but at the moment, it's a couple of months away and it's sort of like (??)
