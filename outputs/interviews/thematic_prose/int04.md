## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (12): highsal, interest, special, mathsci, knowledg, family, gooddeg, varied, mother, careers, nobody, discoura*

**Interviewer —** So when and why do you think that you decided to study engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, I was originally interested in geo physics, erm sort of working with volcanoes or earthquakes but because geo physics has a very narrow qualification I decided that civil engineering would be better. Because – although it would allow me to do geo physics if I wanted to – you know I could specialise in that, it would also enable me to take on other jobs if, you know, over the 4 years, I decided that I wanted to do something else.

**Interviewer —** OK did anything else influence your decision to study engineering, like parents or anything in the media, or careers advice – anything like that?

**Subject —** Probably my mum because my uncle studied civil engineering and so I think my mum originally suggested it, because I hadn't really heard much about engineering. It was sort of something that existed in the sub-conscious somewhere – you don't really actively think about it.

**Interviewer —** Yeah – did you do much career research then?

**Subject —** Oh my goodness, I did an awful lot but I can't say engineering ever came up.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you ever consider doing any of the other types of engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, yes, I applied for both aero and civil but I decided to go with civil because I dec – well, aero has an element of electronics in it and I cannot do electronics. And in the end I decided that civils was probably more diverse and that I would find it more interesting in the long run.

**Interviewer —** OK, that's great, how did you decide to come to [university]?

**Subject —** Erm, well my maths teacher came here and that was the first I'd heard about it. Mm, it – my choice was really between [this university] or [university 2], because they both have very good civil engineering and aero eng departments. And, in the end I decided [this university], because I came here for a visit and it was friendly – [university]. And yeah, [university 2] actually has got a big hill, so you've got the halls of residence on the top and the civil engineering department at the bottom and I just really didn't fancy that.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that more women should be encouraged to be engineers?

**Subject —** Yes. I think – well, I felt that – you know – it wasn't really given to me as an option when I was at school. I knew virtually nothing about it. And I don't know what the case was for the boys – you know, maybe they knew about it – just sort of intuitively or something, er but certainly you know, it wasn't flagged up as something I could do.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): pleased, relevanc, interper, quality, supppers, friends, coursewk, variety*

**Interviewer —** What about in terms of the gender proportions or the type of teaching that you've had?

**Subject —** The teaching's been very good – actually with probably one or two exceptions. The teaching has been excellent. Gender proportions are fine – it just doesn't fuss me at all.

**Interviewer —** OK. What has been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Erm, I'm not sure really. The best thing is getting course work in on time – in civil engineering we don't have any choice – we have to hand it in otherwise it doesn't get accepted.. The worst thing has got to be the sheer volume of work at times, and the fact that you sort of start a week and finish a week without having actually seen daylight, because you're glued to your desk.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that, that helps you – although it's not nice at times, compared to other students doing other subjects, that, that might prepare you for when you do go into the industry?

**Subject —** Erm, possibly. I was speaking to civil engineers who are out in industry. Very few of them work in the same way they did when they were students. In fact, hardly any. You know, mostly they're doing a range different things but you're not – you're not working as hard as you were – I mean at university – so in a way, we are prepared. You know we'll go out and suddenly work will be easy but at the same time there's a chance that you sort of feel that – you know – why do we work so hard – what's the point if in actual fact, you're never going to touch hydraulics again! Why did you spend2 years of your life learning it. It's just – it's just one of those things because civil engineering' so broad.

**Interviewer —** OK, Are you pleased that you chose to do this course?

**Subject —** I think so. I didn't want something that was all very samey. I wanted something that was quite diverse. Something that would interest me as much as possible.

**Interviewer —** Does that bother you at all? Would you rather have a female personal tutor?

**Subject —** Not really. No it doesn't bother me at all that I've got a male personal tutor. I get on really well with him and so on. You know, he sort of keeps up to date with what I'm doing, which is great. But no, it hadn't even occurred to me that I might want a female personal tutor.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, or even … ?

**Subject —** Erm, in academic terms I don't know – I think friends who are interested in the same thing, who are doing the same piece of work – has been very motivating, because when you get stuck there's always some people to talk to.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): placemen, placloca, finuni, noplaoth*

**Interviewer —** OK, how have you decided not to go on a placement – because you were considering … ?

**Subject —** Yes, I was going to go on a placement. Originally when I started university I was sponsored. (OK) But, I got ill and I had to take 7 months leave of absence in my original 2nd year. So I did 3 months of my 2nd year and then I had to leave because I was ill. And at which point, they terminated my sponsorship – erm, all well and good. Erm, so then when I came back, I had to apply to other companies – which has been fine but I'm restricted because of my husband's – he's doing a PhD and I can't – well I don't really want to live outside of the country – I want to commute every week and I just haven't been able to find a job in the area. So I've decided that I'm better off going straight through and then just getting out into industry one year earlier.

**Interviewer —** Yeah.

**Subject —** Erm, I think the advantages are that you see what you've been learning, sort of being employed and you – you sort of see how – the sort of work when you leave university, because they basically treat you like a graduate and you assume responsibilities in line with your capability. And er that's a good thing because it makes you motivated to come back, learn the extra bit and then get out there and you know, if that's what you want to do. Or, you know it might show you that actually you don't want to do engineering and that you'd be better off finishing off and you know, doing something else. So it's just like a form of taster of what life will be like after Uni.

**Interviewer —** Do you feel like the 3 months experience that you have had went some way in doing that?

**Subject —** Er, it helped discourage me.

**Interviewer —** Did it? Why?

**Subject —** Erm, well I was placed between 2 teams – geotechnics and transportation and erm the guy in charge of transportation, for whatever reason, just didn't like me and he made my life a misery – quite literally. And in the end he accused of me lying and I was rather upset by that shall we say and then they moved me to geotechnics and then everything was fine – you know, I really enjoyed that. But, I think the few weeks that I had with transportation was an absolute misery.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that there's anything else that they could have done or were you satisfied with what they did?

**Subject —** Well, short of going and getting a job for me, they've done everything they could do.
