## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): highsal, special, family, hobbies, varied, father, careers, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** Ok, what type of school did you go to?

**Subject —** Erm, I don’t know. It was an all girl’s school but it was just a er – its not private, whatever that is.

**Interviewer —** Are either of your parents engineers at all?

**Subject —** No, my Dad works for [company] and my Mum’s a dental hygeniest.

**Interviewer —** So when and why do you think you decided to do engineering?

**Subject —** Well it was between – well my ‘A’ levels were either Maths, Business or Graphics, and I could do any of them really. But I messed up my Graphic design, and I didn’t want to do straight business studies, ‘cos I’d get bored I thought. And the idea of straight Maths didn’t appeal to me to much either. Cars was always just an interest I had, so it just seems like a thing where I could do a bit of everything.

**Interviewer —** Do you think anything else influenced you? Did you have any careers advice?

**Subject —** I went to careers advice but it was pretty much I could do whatever I wanted to do. But graphics – you had to be good at what you did to get a good salary, whereas engineering is more, you’re gonna get this starting. So that probably swayed me a little bit more, but …

**Interviewer —** Were you’re family supportive of you wanting to do engineering?

**Subject —** Yes, they just – well whatever I wanted to do – they’re fine with.

**Interviewer —** What is it then that appeals to you about automotive engineering?

**Subject —** It was the – I’ve always been interested in – followed Formula 1, so it was the idea that I could maybe have some sort of career related to that. But it was just – I don’t really know, I’ve always liked cars – I think it might have been my Dad’s fault. It’s always been there, so it made sense.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): practica, pleased, Fhelp, interper, supplect, supppers, groupwk, friends, theory*

**Interviewer —** Do you think that there are any advantages or disadvantages to being a woman engineer?

**Subject —** Well we’ve found that there are definitely advantages because the boys like to help you a lot, which is all good. Erm, I don’t know, you could argue maybe that from a social point of view it’s a bit different but …

**Interviewer —** What about the gender proportions? How many girls are there on your course?

**Subject —** Two, me and [name].

**Interviewer —** What do you think are the best and the worst things about the course?

**Subject —** Erm, I’m not sure. I know in the first year I had nearly swopped courses, mainly because I decided it wasn’t for me, but I some how managed to pass my exams, and then they were like you may as well just see how you do in the summer, and I passed them again. And this year’s been a lot more enjoyable. I think the satisfaction when you’ve got something that works, that’s really the best bits. But the worse is just, you can spend hours trying to draw a graph, that’s really not that hard, but it’s so much easier when you know what the answer is, but getting to that point, its just – drives you up the wall.

**Interviewer —** Is that the type of thing that was putting you off last year then?

**Subject —** I think last year I just, I wasn’t sure if I should have done graphics or not. I think it was just the change in going to uni as well. I got home at Christmas and was just like I don’t want to do this anymore. But erm, I’m glad I didn’t change I think, yes.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the male lecturers treat you the same as the male students?

**Subject —** I think so. Erm, we have one lecturer that just jokes with us, but other than that, they all pretty much treat us the same way. I think, when I tried to give up they were certainly – I think even getting here because we didn’t have physics, we both found that it was, oh girls, do anything we can to get girls on the course. But otherwise its all, we don’t have a problem at all.

**Interviewer —** Ok that’s fine. Is there anyone or anything that you would say has been the biggest influence on you since you started the degree?

**Subject —** Erm, I think the group of friends that I have on the course, probably help me through it, but short of that nothing really.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, jobdecid, industry, placloca, noappeal, noaccept, noplaoth*

**Interviewer —** Why did you decide not to go on a placement then?

**Subject —** Well initially I was on the sandwich course and I applied to a couple of places and one I went to an interview for, which – it was a good experience, but I didn’t actually really want it because it was North Wales, and ideally I want to get a placement at home, which I just couldn’t find anything, and it got to the point where there wasn’t really anything I wanted to do and I thought I’d just be doing it for the sake of doing it. And the group of people on my course who I’m happy with aren’t doing it, so I’d lose that, so its just – makes more sense to stay here. I think what I’ll do is switch to the Masters instead, so I’ll still do 4 years. And try and get some summer placements, so I’ll get the best of both worlds.

**Interviewer —** Ok, do you think that there are any particular advantages or disadvantages to going on the placement?

**Subject —** I’d say the experience helps a lot, just – I think half of it with me is working out if that’s what I want to do – and I’m still not sure, I might just do my Masters in Business Studies or something. But that’s the nice thing about engineering degrees – you can do that. But I think certainly from a boy’s point of view having the experience is a good thing.
