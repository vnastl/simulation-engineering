## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): interest, special, knowledg, family, careers, nobody, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** OK when and why do you think that you decided to do product design then?

**Subject —** Er, well when I was younger I liked all the sort of options available, it was sort of the only one that would incorporate all 3 of my A levels, and I didn't want to drop any one in particular, so it seemed liked the best option.

**Interviewer —** Did you just choose the subjects on the basis of the ones you enjoyed then?

**Subject —** Yeah. Basically just because I enjoyed it and I didn't want to like throw away any one of them, so…

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think there's any other factors that influenced your decision to do an engineering based subject - did you have any careers advice when you were at school, anything like that?

**Subject —** Er, yeah, our careers were quite good but I think they were a bit shocked when I decided to come here and do a sort of engineering – well it's quite renowned for its engineering, but I suppose a lot of the girls from my school have gone into sort of engineering or maths and that kind of field, anyway, so it wasn't really that big a surprise I think, 'cos there was the direction a lot of us go.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you know any engineers to talk to, or anyone that had gone into similar fields?

**Subject —** Not really, no. Well my grandfather sort of did engineering, in a sense. He was sort of really quite technical minded but that was really about it.

**Interviewer —** OK, What is it then, about the subject that you think does appeal to you?

**Subject —** I suppose it's 'cos I have the subjects that I enjoyed at school, but that was really about it – I suppose I didn't really know an awful lot about it till I started it so it was just a fluke really, I think.

**Interviewer —** OK. How did you decide to come to [this university] then?

**Subject —** Erm, probably because of its reputation mainly and because I'm not actually from England it was just a matter of almost picking from a hat really, what courses did the sort of subjects that I liked and then, you know, what universities did those at (mock?) courses, so it wasn't really a – well not like planned.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): pleased, relevanc, Fhelp, interper, supplect, supppers, groupwk, friends*

**Interviewer —** Did it ever bother you at all?

**Subject —** Erm, I think a little bit, just to begin with, just at the getting to know everybody and like, just getting to know what the guys are like, as to whether they were gonna sort of like be, oh you're a girl kind of thing. But they're all really good so it doesn't bother me now, 'cos at least they see me as somebody else, they don't see you as a girl as such, so that's quite good.

**Interviewer —** OK, has the course been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** No, it's a lot more – they sort of – I got the impression, from the prospectus, it was going to be a lot more design, but there's a lot more manufacture, which is – well is a shock, but I enjoy it, so it's OK.

**Interviewer —** OK. What do you think have been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Oh, erm, the worst probably is some of the – well some of the modules that I have to do, I don't quite see the relevance of them, so that – I don't really enjoy them though, I suppose. And the best is probably – I dunno, apart from the people.

**Interviewer —** When you say that you don't see the relevance of some of the modules, do you mean as in you don't think that you'll use them when you leave?

**Subject —** Yeah, it's more like the integrated studies kind of modules, in that you're doing things that you're only doing for maybe a few weeks, and you're not actually being – you haven't actually done any lectures on them. So you don't have enough knowledge to fully understand what you're doing, and it's not carried on so you don't – you don't feel you get anything from it.

**Interviewer —** Yes, OK, do you think that the male lecturers treat the female students the same as the male students?

**Subject —** Er, the majority of them do, yeah. The odd one or two, either exclude you or not really consider you as part of the group, or will pick on you one or the other.

**Interviewer —** OK, have you had any particularly good or bad experiences of group work?

**Subject —** Yes, bad ones have probably been – well we're working in a group at the minute actually that's not particularly enjoyable because – well one of the guys is – I feel that he has the impression that you're just a girl, which I find quite annoying, so we don't really see eye to eye on a lot of things and I find him quite sort of domineering, that he's just came and took the role of leader – it's sort of like you will do this, this, and this. And there's no sort of leverage for different opinions in the group so, that's been a bit of a battle.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): placemen, experien, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel*

**Interviewer —** And what do you think are the advantages of doing that?

**Subject —** Basically just getting experience, because everywhere they all – everybody just wants experience and if you don't have it then you know, it's a bit difficult to get a bit further, so I think that was the main reason.

**Interviewer —** Do you think it'll help you make your mind up what you want to do, more long-term as well?

**Subject —** Hopefully – yeah. It'll tell me whether I absolutely hate engineering or love it, hopefully.

**Interviewer —** OK, did anyone particularly encourage you or discourage you from going on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, my parents probably – they really encouraged me. I don't think anybody's discouraged me, really much. Erm, probably some of the employers have discouraged me, in the sense that they've been quite uncooperative or rude, in a sense.

**Interviewer —** Is that when you've been to interviews or (?)

**Subject —** Not too much interviews, but when I've been applying in that it's like I've been waiting to hear from one for about 4 weeks now, and I was told that I would hear, like 4 weeks ago and I felt encouraged and so – and when I call them, my calls just get re-directed and stuff, so I think that's quite rude. So that's kind of put me off a little bit – things like that but I think my parents have encouraged me quite a bit and like I'd say the staff have been encouraging quite a bit they say, like keep trying.

**Interviewer —** OK, what have you been looking for in a company and what has attracted you to the companies that you have applied for?

**Subject —** Erm, probably more – a bit more of the design aspect rather than full on engineering, but a mixture of both – companies that will actually do the design work and then carry out right the way through. So that kind of thing rather than just going into solid engineering or something.

**Interviewer —** OK, what sorts of things are you hoping to get out of your placement?

**Subject —** Erm, confidence and the ability to like work for a company and to not have it all go wrong hopefully.

*Coded variables informed by this theme (5): carpath, workrole, salary, location, promot*

**Interviewer —** Have you looked at other things like pay or training opportunities and career prospects, or location – have you looked at anything like that?

**Subject —** Erm, location mainly – has been probably the strongest one, because initially I wanted to stay fairly local to here, but that's changed now and I'm sort of looking wider for something because I'm not finding anything. Erm, pay – a little bit but it's probably not as much as erm other things like where it actually is and what it does.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, OK, in terms of your career then, what do you think you'll be doing in say 5 years time?

**Subject —** Oh I have no idea – really no idea. Erm, hopefully something that I enjoy and that will use what I'm actually learning at the minute 'cos I don't think I'd like to just throw it all away and do something completely different.

**Interviewer —** So what would be your ideal career then?

**Subject —** I'd probably really like to work for some sort of consultancy – engineering design consultancy that type of thing.

**Interviewer —** What is it that appeals to you about doing that?

**Subject —** Erm, probably the same as what I'm looking for in a placement – they do their own sort of designs, and they work with sort of big companies and it – you see it through to manufacture but it's not just only the engineering of one thing.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that they're good for getting in, in the first place and to climb up the career ladder once you're in?

**Subject —** I think probably getting into it, is probably a little more difficult because there doesn't seem to be that many opportunities at the minute, and there's probably more and more people starting to look at these things so I suppose there's more people coming out than there are jobs available so – and you know that probably you're gonna have to compete against people who've done a more streamlined form of engineering, which could be more difficult.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that more long-term you'd like to go into management within the field that you're in?

**Subject —** Yeah, probably because I'm doing a management module at the minute, which I've really enjoyed so possibly.
