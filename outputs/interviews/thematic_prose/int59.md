## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (2): interest, school*

**Interviewer —** OK do you think that it is an issue that there are say fewer women than men there?

**Subject —** I think its become an issue because people have suddenly noticed, hmm in my mind I’ve never seen it as when people have said but you’re a girl, it’s a bit of a strange degree. Its like, I wanted to take the degree, I was interested in it so I’m becoming an engineer. It’s never been kind of, what can I do to, cos’ it will be strange, sort of, I’ll go and be an engineer. I’ve never seen that, its kind of like, yes I’ve done physics and all the rest of it, so I’m doing this, the same way my friends did this and decided they wanted to be doctors or chemistry students, there’s, I just don’t see why its become so (inaudible), its only become a big deal because they’ve looked at their statistics and gone, do you realise that only like 2% of them are women, its like, does it matter as long as the jobs get done and no ones been turned away on purpose because of it. If there’s like actual discrimination, to say no, we only employ blokes, that’s fine, there’s an issue there, but with the whole industry as an employer of women, why does it need to employ women, it needs to employ engineers. (Yeah) So I’m not really into the getting more women into engineering, I’ve always been, if I’ve done stuff with schools and stuff, I don’t mind helping them getting into science and maths and sort of take that kind of route, but in the end I’m not gonna say, oh you really like physics, your good at maths well, come and do engineering, you don’t want to do physics, you don’t want to do anything else, you don’t want to go off and be a doctor, come and do engineering cos’ its great. I’d much rather say well if you like it, get on with it and have a look at what you can do because I don’t see the point in suddenly going, you’ve kind of got the right things, you do want to do this because we need to boost the numbers for whatever reasons.

**Interviewer —** OK that’s great. Just one last thing, you mentioned that another team was much younger than, do you think when people aren’t used to maybe having women to work with, that there is an age issue there?

**Subject —** Hmm I don’t think its so much an age issue, its just what people are used to hmm I mean at lancing, where I did my first placement while I was at A levels hmm although I wasn’t working on the shop floor, I spent a lot of time around fitters, and when they haven’t been around hmm working with women and stuff, they found it quite hard to adjust, but they were kind of more asking lots of questions about why you were there rather than making it any harder for you or anything and stuff. (Yeah) Hmm but then again here, where people from, where people have graduated recently with more women in classes, their fine with that, their used to working with it or at the other end of the scale within my group where there’s people, they’ve got their kids which are as old as I am and know that sort of we’re not going to be (inaudible) and all the rest of it and we don’t need treating with kitten gloves, we can just get on with it, and then there’s people whose sort of have worked a lot with people in HR where it’s the complete reverse, where there’s hundreds of women, not many blokes and their fine with it. But you’ve got people who have come up various companies and they’ve never had any women it’s a bit of a culture shock some of them. Its, its just getting used to things, if you suddenly sort of, I mean I know going from an all girls school in sixth form we were suddenly joined with [school] and it was odd having classes with blokes but after the first month or so you got used to it. (Yeah) It’s just sort of…

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): placemen, experien, educatio, jobdecid, industry, indchart*

**Interviewer —** OK is there anything that you thought you would achieve but haven’t so far?

**Subject —** Hmm, not really but that’s more because I didn’t really set out to achieve anything, it was a case of I’m doing this placement, we’ll go and we’ll see what its like cos’ if its bad I know where I don’t wanna go, if its good, great. Hmm, especially with other placements I’ve found over the summer when I did hmm things like [company] where they took me on because they wanted to advertise to graduates rather than they had work for me to do and they had some (inaudible) where I just got really bored with it all and really didn’t want to be there. So I sort of did that myself, sort of set myself thinking just in case it turned out to be as bad as previous times so hmm yeah.

**Interviewer —** OK what would you now say are the benefits of doing a placement?

**Subject —** Hmm well the experience hmm with engineering in particular it’s a year off chartership if you want to do that. Hmm it’s a break from uni instead of being sat with books, going to lectures, having to sit there for two hours and concentrate, its being able to actually do work. If you aren’t happy with something you can go and say I don’t know or I can’t do this and be given something else if things are too much and just actually work through things.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that there are any drawbacks to doing a placement?

**Subject —** Hmm the only kind of drawback I’ve had is hmm ringing up my friends at school and uni, cos’ all of them are doing three year courses. They’ll have graduated by the time I come back. Hmmm which is kind of rough, hmm trying to come back here and find places to live and stuff, but apart from that hmm, no, if you’ve got a placement you enjoy its fine. From previous experience, if your in somewhere you really don’t like, your kind of stuck there till the end of the year, even if you do say, sort of I don’t like this, you end up going around everywhere and finding out that you really don’t like the company or you don’t like these people, you do have to stay there for a year. Hmm and its very disheartening going to a placement and finding out you really don’t like them and you sit there thinking, I’ve just spent two years doing this degree, I’ve done a job of it and I really hated it, what should I be doing now, and again that can be a positive, if you find out, it’s better to find out after two years that you really should be doing something else than it is to do four years, then you come out and find that you cant try something else or see what else is going on.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the placement experience will help you when you start work when you finished university?

**Subject —** Hmm yeah, it will give me some idea of how I need to operate in a working environment rather than hmm than what I do at uni and just be sort of used to the, that routine and knowing that its all right to know that you need these equations but you’ve got to be able to apply them for this and while your applying it to that, that maybe the optimal answer for that, you need to build it which you can’t do with this and things like that. So just knowing the whole process and I mean if I do end up going back to the same company, it gives me a head start, cos’ I’ll know people there, I already know that if I need help with this, I can go and talk to Mike, or I can talk to John if I need things so.
