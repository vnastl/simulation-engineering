## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them

**Interviewer —** OK, what factors do you think influenced your decision – like family and education and your strengths? Like why did you decide to do the Maths and Physics and maybe not Sociology or something like that?

**Subject —** Erm, I’ve always been very practical. And, er, mechanical was just – it was varied enough for you to get as much out of it as you wanted to, regarding – to varying a module and everything and not knowing much about it, within the (?) of doing it. ## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): pleased, interper, quality, supppers, groupwk, designwk*

**Interviewer —** OK, let’s talk a bit about your course and your experiences on the course so far. What did you expect from your degree, in terms of like the skills that you’ve learnt and maybe the gender proportions and the type of teaching and things like that?

**Subject —** Er, gender proportions I was quite surprised how few girls there were, on a course of 140, because I think there’s maybe 10/15 us possibly in our year, er which was a bit of a surprise at how low it was – ‘cos I knew it was low. I don’t know – the course itself is – I don’t know, I expected the course to be more intense than it was, kind of, because it’s always been built up as this really hard course which nobody wants to do, and all the rest of it, and it’s a lot of work. But, it’s what I thought, but it’s not as time consuming as I thought (yeah) because it’s a lot of in lectures stuff, rather than a lot of courses 6 hours, but then you’ve got to do 3 weeks worth of reading for next week and things like that.

**Interviewer —** OK what do you think have been the best and worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Erm, the worst thing is the group projects possibly, because we don’t get enough information to do a lot of the stuff, so we’re always getting cross (?) because they’re usually run by more than one of the lecturers. So it’s very confusing and your – your goals aren’t set (yeah) and then when people start getting irritated and it doesn’t work well. The best bit is I’m finally understanding a lot of stuff, because last year was a lot of learning new things and now it’s generally more or less apply what you’ve learnt last year and I can (so its all starting to make sense?) yeah its all starting coming together and we’re looking at more interesting things rather than basic examples.

**Interviewer —** OK, have you been taught by male and female lecturers?

**Subject —** We’ve had one female lecturer so far and she wasn’t very good (laughs). Erm, no there’s been a general consensus on our course that the one female lecturer we’ve had is possible the worst role model we could’ve ever asked for and we prefer – we much prefer just having whoever.

**Interviewer —** Have you been to them or are they approachable if you needed to go to them?

**Subject —** They’re approachable erm yeah – Colin’s quite good.

**Interviewer —** Ok, do you think that men or women are better to work with or is it…?

**Subject —** Not really mixed groups tend to be the best – well I find – well because if there are a couple of guys on the course who – where they’ve actually done – specially design and make projects, where they’ve actually done some machining or things, they tend to try to take over. And if you’ve got a mixed group, usually you can get some back up for everything you’re going to say. Even if it’s just quieting everyone down so they’ll actually listen. Er, it’s also because we do actually look at things a slightly different way, and it just brings up a few more alternatives (yeah) to look at, and things to take into account.

*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): placemen, experien, educatio, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, apptheor, finuni*

**Interviewer —** Right, OK. What do you think it was that made you want to do a Sandwich course?

**Subject —** Erm, well firstly because 4 week – 4 years, straight through University seemed like a lot to take on really (yes) because I’ve always wanted to do things like placement stuff, even if it was just to see which bits of engineering I didn’t want to do. (Yeah) So, it’s kind of knowing where I want to go when I’m finished. Gain the experience and just seeing how it does work in the real world, as opposed to design this and you have a lathe and this. And knowing what’s out there.

**Interviewer —** Is there a particular reason for that?

**Subject —** I don’t like the idea of it being put through – that these are [university] students – they are meant to be the best and being put in for companies who always take in [university] students, which seem to be what most of those ones are. I’d rather have sent in my CV, covering letter or do the on-line thing, and just say, this is me, this is what I’ve done, do you want to employ me or not?

**Interviewer —** OK, do you feel prepared and ready to go on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, mostly. I’m still a bit worried about not knowing enough, especially because I don’t know exactly where I’m going or what I’ll be expected to do at that company.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, what sort of companies have you been applying to?

**Subject —** Everything from er [company] to [company], erm [organisation] – anything which sounds like it could be more than just being sat at an office doing one thing time and time again, so…

**Interviewer —** Kind of putting the theory into practice (yeah). OK, do you think that it will improve your career prospects at the end being able to say that you have been on a placement?

**Subject —** Yeah, ‘cos it shows that you have got some idea of erm being grounded in industry rather than saying I know all this theory like everyone else does. You can apply it, knowing what’s needed for what and…

**Interviewer —** What do you think could be the worst and the best things about being on a placement?

**Subject —** Er, the best thing will be meeting – making new contacts (yeah) because a lot of the companies will help you – like find me a project and things and helping you out with information. The worst is probably moving to wherever the placement is and being somewhere completely new and having to live with total strangers (yeah) for a whole year where you can’t just turn round and then say no, I’m not going to have this any more.
