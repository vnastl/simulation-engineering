## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (10): interest, challeng, mathsci, knowledg, family, hobbies, varied, father, careers, school*

**Interviewer —** OK, What is it that made you want to do engineering, in the first place, then do you think?

**Subject —** Erm, well it sounds stupid my dad’s an engineer at the moment. I think because I’ve got an interest in maths and physics and stuff and it just seemed the logical thing to go and do. Erm, I quite like knowing how things are made and how things work and things like that so - it’s quite a big area, it’s not just like being a teacher, it’s like vast and stuff.

**Interviewer —** Yes. You said that your dad was an engineer, did he have much influence on your decision then?

**Subject —** I think he influenced me in the way that he had a – he has a passion for engineering, so it kind of like encourages you to like think about engineering and kind of with dad, there’s a lot of stuff in the garage and he always let me go and play with it, but not in the sense that you’re going to do engineering at university – you know what I mean? He wasn’t like that.

**Interviewer —** Did you have much careers advice or anything like that?

**Subject —** Erm, I don’t know. I think at school we had the whole – you know those big forms where they tell you you’re gonna be like a gardener or something. They had those. Erm, I had quite a good careers advisor at my sixth form college – but that was more to do with like what I was gonna do at university and which universities to go to, rather than like what do you want to do with your life? Kind of thing.

**Interviewer —** How did you decide to do the type of engineering that you are doing then?

**Subject —** Erm, I actually started doing mechanical engineering and then I swapped so. I started doing mechanical engineering because it was just the maths and physics that I liked and that was the most obvious one to go into. Er, kind of like – I don’t know, got sick of doing maths and physics and realised that I actually wanted to do more like manufacturing (design?). Like I wanted to know how stuff was made more than the forces or anything like that.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): practica, pleased, relevanc, quality, groupwk, practwk*

**Interviewer —** OK. I’m going to ask you a bit about the course now – has the course been what you expected in terms of things like the skills you’ve been taught and the content of the course and gender proportions and anything like that?

**Subject —** OK. Erm, yeah I think so. It’s a bit hard because we’ve learnt a lot about manufacturing and we do quite a lot of hours of that a week. We haven’t done as much kinds of proper hands on stuff, like being in a lab and using a lathe. We did that in our first year. We had like the labs and went generally in the workshops but – I had a fair amount of experience before, but like other people haven’t and for them to just use it for 2 hours wasn’t really developing their skills, it was just giving them an insight and stuff. So I was expecting it to be more, like actual make stuff, which we haven’t really done. Erm, comparing it to what I used to do in mech eng, the science has been a lot easier and it’s a lot more basic than what I was expecting. Erm, we’re not doing as much maths as what I thought we do because we only do maths in the first year. Erm, we’re doing like stats this year. I don’t know – I think it’s met my expectations, in what I’ve learnt, anyway.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think have been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Erm, the best thing has been the manufacturing lectures and there’s just – we’re just doing one on like prototyping which is really good and we got shown round the labs and shown how all the machines work – which is the best thing. Erm, the worst thing is going to 9 O’clock lectures. (Laughs) I don’t know – I don’t think we’ve had that many.

**Interviewer —** Why is that?

**Subject —** Because the women on our course are all quite dictating. It’s like – it is quite – I do find it hard working with them because I think, in general, you tend to have similar qualities and it just gets quite difficult and it’s easy when it’s just one girl and a couple of men, in my sort of class, so…

**Interviewer —** OK have you had any particularly good or bad experiences of group work?

**Subject —** Erm, generally bad ones – because we normally do them like last minute and stuff and it’s always get quite stressful, right at the end. Erm, yeah, we just – we don’t always have good time management, so obviously it’s a bit stressful at the end of the project, really.

**Interviewer —** Are you happy with all the career choices that you’ve made so far? Is there anything that you would you do differently if you went through it all again?

**Subject —** Erm, no I think I am happy with how I’ve ended up. I think the only thing I would have liked to have done, is perhaps, in my gap year got sponsored and that would’ve like taken the stress off money and stuff but I think – no, I can’t imagine not doing what I’ve done – so.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (3): placemen, wkexp, finuni*

**Interviewer —** Why did you decide not to go on the placement this time?

**Subject —** Erm, because I’m too old. (laughs). Because I’m like 22 now, so it’s just gonna be a year out, I’d be like 24 by the time I graduated and I decided that I could just wait like another year and then probably get paid better than what I’d get paid next year. Yeah, I just wanted to graduate really.

**Interviewer —** If you hadn’t already done a year out, might you have been more interested in it?

**Subject —** Yes, definitely. I think it’s a really good thing to do and I think people should do it compulsorily – I think they should, before they have a proper job, have a …

**Interviewer —** What type of work were you doing when you were on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, well all the crap work, so – I used to erm, yes, cos on my gap year I did like 3 months on quality, 3 months in engineering and then I did about 6 months in purchasing. So in – in quality I had to like go down on the shop floor and – let me think – we had – yeah, we were doing like a reject analysis so we were trying to examine like whether, determine where the rejects were coming from so we could like lower those. So I had to like do loads of sheets and I was going get the sheets and then put all the information on the computers and stuff like that and pull up some graphs about what (lost words). In eng – in the engineering it - cos [name] industries is a – is a contractor, [company], contractor. They used to make – I’m not sure – like field hands – you know the big [diggers] and stuff (yeah) they used to make those. Erm, and [company] were wanting to go into like a different tyre. They wanted something cheaper and lighter, so I had to redesign the field tank and make some such, that was quite good – the prototype stuff. Erm general odds and sods jobs and stuff and erm the purchasing manager left – he got kind of sacked – and it was quite a small company, there’s only about 20 like engineers, like proper engineers there, so I got put in there to work with my other friend and we had to like manage that department for about 6 months. So that was pretty good.

**Interviewer —** Were you given quite a lot of opportunities?

**Subject —** Yeah, we were given quite a lot of responsibilities, quite early on and stuff which was good. Erm, yeah, that’s all I did there. And then I did a different place when I left Imperial, I worked in a plastics manufacturing company as a quality engineer, for about 4 months so I had my own kind of clients and I had some new projects coming into the factory, so I had to set up like and organise that the (tills?) were there and do this a bit like presentations and that kind of thing. And then dealing with all the quality issues that come in with that.

**Interviewer —** Or the experience generally?

**Subject —** I think the best thing was just kind of like erm increasing my confidence – kind of like having a good understanding how everything works. ‘Cos I always used to think that like, any product that comes out of a factory is going to be spot on and there’ll be no problems with it and it really opened my eyes as to actually what goes on in a factory and stuff like that. Erm, the worst things – it was a really mundane job, and sometimes you’d just sit there all day and being like measurements and I used to hate doing that, because you keep the same thing (lost words).

**Interviewer —** Are you pleased that you did the placement before you started university?

**Subject —** I think it helped me in the respect that I grew up quite a lot in that year, and I think if I’d gone to university straight after my A levels, I wouldn’t have got involved in as many things and stuff like that. I think I just grew up quite a lot. If I hadn’t – like probably if I hadn’t done like mech eng in the first year, I probably would have done one now because I’d just have that extra year, so – no, I’m glad I did it then.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): carpath, workrole, location, people, travel, training*

**Interviewer —** Do you know what type of company you’d like to work for when you’ve finished?

**Subject —** No, I don’t know. I don’t want a job that relies on production and stuff. I don’t want people coming to me, like at 9 O’clock in the morning and saying right, you can get this stuff out the door by 2 O’clock. I don’t want anything like that. Erm, I’d just want something that kind of like stretches your brain and stuff and I’m quite interested in looking at and developing erm like manufacturing processes and finding better and more efficient and cheaper ways to do stuff. That kind of thing. I’m looking at kind of doing like more like research or R & D kind of stuff, rather than kind of working close to the shop floor.

**Interviewer —** OK, what sort of things do you think will attract you to a company when you are looking for a job? Will it be pay or training opportunities?

**Subject —** Yeah, things like training opportunities. Erm, yeah like what I’m gonna be able to do – like opportunities like within the companies whether I’m going to be able to travel with it and things like that.

**Interviewer —** Would you like to travel?

**Subject —** Yeah, I’d like to like be exposed to manufacturing in different companies, in different countries and see how it’s different and see if you can send that back to the UK or anything. Erm, location I suppose, money erm, and the general company ethos as well and what the kind of like the attitude of the company is as well, and like whether it’s kind of a bit backwards or a bit forwards and that kind of thing, I think.

**Interviewer —** What about more long term, say in 5 or10 years time?

**Subject —** Erm, I don’t know really. Ooh, I don’t know because it all depends on whether – because I think women have the whole like husband and children stuff and that sort of breaks your career up as well, so – I don’t know ‘cos – I don’t really – I really don’t know.

**Interviewer —** Are you interested in managerial type side of things?

**Subject —** Er, to some extent yeah, I quite enjoy project management. I do enjoy like when we have like projects on the course, kind of like telling the boys what to do. (laughter) So I would like to do like manager – I wouldn’t have any problems with managing like a team of colleagues or anything like that so…
