## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (12): highsal, interest, challeng, mathsci, knowledg, family, gooddeg, varied, careers, teacher, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** What do your parents do? Are either of them engineers at all?

**Subject —** Erm, no, my dad’s an accountant, and my mum’s a child play worker. Erm but my er all my grandparents were engineers and their parents were engineers as well and my brother is as well.

**Interviewer —** That’s quite interesting. When and why then do you think that you decided to do engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, well my granddad, he did aeronautical engineering, became an airline pilot, erm and or whatever aeronautical engineering was, back then. Erm, and then I decided I wanted to be an airline pilot as well, but then after the terrorist attack on September 11th a lot of the airlines couldn’t afford to take on any pilots that didn’t have licences already. It costs a lot of money for them to fund a pilot to be trained – it’s about £100,000 or something, so erm, I had to – I couldn’t go straight from ‘A’ levels, to pilot training, so I decided to go to university and do something to do with aeroplanes, and then, hopefully, when I left I could like go back onto the original life plan that I had.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you have much careers advice when you were at school?

**Subject —** Er, yeah we – er my physics teacher was an aeronautical engineer so she’s the one that suggested doing it and pushed it, maybe a bit more. Erm, but like I’d looked into pilot training, couldn’t fund it myself and you all had to be sponsored and when the opportunity for sponsorship wasn’t around any more I thought that maybe aeronautical engineering was a good idea, anyway. Erm, but I didn’t – I wasn’t told of any university open days or anything like that. I found out about the one at [this university] from my physics teacher, but the careers like department, at school, they didn’t tell us about open days or careers fairs or anything that were at universities – we had to do that ourselves really. But if you know where you want to go already it’s a bit easier I suppose.

**Interviewer —** Did you ever look at the air transport management course here?

**Subject —** Erm, I did, but you don’t do it, do you? (No) It feels too easy. Only people who usually get like really ‘A’ level grades, I don’t think – I think they tell you to do aeronautical instead, because – I don’t know – I got erm 2 A’s and 2 B’s and like I’ve got friends that got like B’s, that did air transport management, so I thought I don’t want to – I want to keep challenging myself and get the best degree that I can, instead of doing something that I know will be easier but probably won’t get me as far as I want to get (yeah) but I did think about it. Erm, but then I think what they can do with air transport management; I could probably do with aeronautical engineering as well but still have other choices. So I decided to go with that.

**Interviewer —** You don’t regret choosing this course at all then?

**Subject —** No, if I was gonna do any course apart from this, it would be pure maths. So, and that’d be just boring. I’m only good at maths and numbers really, so it was either engineering or maths. I suppose I could have done French but I just – I don’t like – the good thing about engineering is, when you get work, you know whether you’ve done it right or wrong, and with a subject like French, or whatever, you write an essay and they check it for grammar and things – I don’t like that kind of things.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that goes back to the perception of engineering being dirty and …?

**Subject —** Yeah because people – I’ve got to admit I thought when I first started, I thought it would be as bad. I’ve said a lot more dirty, a lot more hands-on, like saws and drilling and rivets and things. The bits that we have done like that are cool, and I guess, I don’t know – I think once you’re into it, then you know it’s not like that but before you get into it, you don’t think that. And that’s why before I got into it, I kind of already knew I wasn’t going to do it for the rest of my life, ‘cos I thought it could be cutting up metal for the rest of my life. I just did it ‘cos it had high entry grades and it said if you’re an engineer you’re very versatile and employable, so I thought that’s what I want to be – employable.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): practica, pleased, quality, supppers, groupwk, theory, practwk, designwk*

**Interviewer —** OK. Has the course been what you expected it to be ?

**Subject —** Erm, not really. Erm, I thought it would be a bit more design; a bit more erm contact with aeroplanes and a bit more lab work but I mean with all the recent strikes and things I know that they are really under funded and they can’t afford to let us do a lot of practical work and run wind tunnels and things all the time but I think there’s a lot more theory than I expected. I thought there’d be a bit more practice but I can handle that. Some – a lot of people have left because they – they can’t do maths day in and day out, but they wanted to see like cars and planes and things but I’m OK with it, even though it wasn’t what I expected, it’s still not that different.

**Interviewer —** OK, are you happy with the personal tutor that you’ve got – do you find them approachable?

**Subject —** Erm, I’ve only ever spoken to her face to face twice and once was – they were both in my first year, one was after my summer you know – yeah, one was after my winter exam results and one was when I first started university but like I don’t – there was a death in my family recently, and I told my personal tutor and she told my lecturers and people that my course work was supposed to be handed to, that it wouldn’t all be in on time, that kind of thing. And she was really good with that. And, she’s also quite – she’ll talk to you on your own level because she’s got her children at university, or they’ve just graduated or something, so she knows the score, she doesn’t expect you to have 150% attendance for every single lecture – and 98% in all your course work and she doesn’t expect you to not go to the Union, so she’s quite cool, because she knows what it’s like. If anything like – if someone tries to stop you from doing something, you want to do it even more I suppose. But erm my first set of exam results – first ever, weren’t that good because I was used to the ‘A’ level way of doing things and she just asked me about it and then, after my second set of results, last year, that were a lot better she er – she was like, oh nice to see you on your feet now and that was it really. So…

**Interviewer —** No that’s good. OK, have you had to do much group work as part of your course?

**Subject —** Not that much like Samantha and Natalie, they do civil and architectural engineering and they – it seems like they do it in groups all the time and I think I’ve erm 3 or 4 since I’ve been here – 3 or 4 pieces of group work and I absolutely hate it. And they had some pieces of course work, where it was optional, you could do it on your own or do it in a group and share the marks, and I was like I’m doing it on my own. I hate group work.

**Interviewer —** Why?

**Subject —** Because boys are just useless and the chances of me being in a group, with other girls is very slim. I just think let me work on my own. They’re not useless but the last group I was in, they – they actually said from the start, we’ll do an all nighter the night before it’s supposed to be handed in and I was like er no, I’m an 8 hours a night girl. I’m going to sleep. So I did all my work, all my part of the course – the work – gave it to them, like over email and then they really resented the fact that that night they were doing an all nighter, I was out with my friends. But that’s why I don’t like it, and it’s just too much running after people, trying to organise things. And there’s one guy who’s always in my group because he’s like behind in the alphabet but he’s also in the RAF and he’s – he uses it as an excuse not to go to group meetings, every single time, so it just – it’s annoying, because being a girl as well, they kind of expect you to organise meetings and things. Erm, if anything, like the way sometimes girls play up to the fact that they’re female, the guys will do exactly the same and they’ll say, come on, you do it. You’re a girl. You know you’re better at this than us, so you end up in a position where you’re in charge of everyone and they just don’t do what you wan them to do.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): placemen, wkexp, nogain, noplaoth*

**Interviewer —** OK, we are interviewing women from all the different engineering disciplines – people in their 2nd year. We’re mainly interviewing people that are going on placement, because we want to know why they’re going on placement and what they expect to find out and we’re going back to them, when they’re on placement, again – but you’re not going on placement?

**Subject —** No, I was on the placement programme but then I changed to the actual one without the industrial placement.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the experience of group work will help you when you get into the industry as well?

**Subject —** Erm, well I’m not doing a placement and that’s because I don’t think I want to work in the industry when I’m finished. So, I can’t really comment on that I suppose. If I did go into the industry I would probably still avoid group work.

**Interviewer —** OK, why did you decide not to go on placement then?

**Subject —** Well yeah. Well I started the course and I, at the end of the first year, I just – I started to think I like this for now, but I thought can I do this till I’m 65? And I thought, I don’t think I can. And because my teacher’s had engineering degrees, I thought maybe teaching was something to go into. But, like I said before, one of the reasons I did the course was because it was to push to myself to the absolute limit, like it was – BEng degrees were higher than my final A level group. So I got in, luckily, and I think – I just wanted to get the best degree and know that you can do anything really afterwards – with an aeronautical engineering degree. Obviously you can’t be a lawyer or a doctor but you could go into business or finance or teaching or even the police force or something, so I think I just did it to give myself a lot of options and the possibility of working with aeroplanes as well. But I don’t know. I’ve got some work experience at [company] over the summer. Because I’m not doing a placement anymore, I thought I’ll try and get experience anyway. Erm, so I worked at [company], all of last summer and I’ve got some experience at [company] this summer and I’ll do something else next summer. So I’ll see how it goes at [company] because if it’s good then I might think OK, it could be for me after all.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that there would have been any advantages in doing a placement?

**Subject —** Erm, yeah, obviously you’d have friends within the industry – you’d have a foot in the door, whatever company you do your placement with. But, er like after working at [company], they said they’ve have me back any time I want and this thing with [company] – I’m sure that a month there will be the same – like I’ll still make as much of an impression, as I could if I were there for longer. So I think it’s – I can still get myself to the same position where I’ve got opportunities in the industry when I leave.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): carpath, workrole, furstudy, studarea, salary, location, workenv*

**Interviewer —** That’s interesting because you said that you thought that the course would have more hands on stuff.

**Subject —** Yeah, I thought it would have more hands on stuff like – not like, obviously engines and oil, that kind of thing – like designing and making erm – I don’t mind – I could do that with metal things, but I wouldn’t want to have to wear overalls to work every day. Er, I think I dunno, because at physics ‘A’ level, you did a lot of experiments and things and it was fun writing them up and stuff to get an idea of what’s going on. I think that’s why it was important for me to do the same thing for the degree – to – just to understand what’s going on really, ‘cos when you see stuff on paper, and you see more and more on paper, and it’s all based on other stuff you’ve seen on paper, you start to lose track of what stuff actually is and you know all these names of things and components but you don’t actually know where they are in real life, and it starts to get a bit confusing. But it would really help er my understanding while I’m here but then, when I leave, I don’t want to – I want to – I think design is more my kind of thing – er more maths and numbers than like cutting up sheets of metal and things. But then essentially I have – you know, they say when you finish people do the cutting up of metal for you. You’re the one that – you do all the top level stuff and then they’re the one that finishes off and actually manufactures things. You’ve got someone else doing it – labour that kind of thing.

**Interviewer —** What sorts of things do you think will attract you to a company? Will you look at things like location and money? Or will the type of work that they do be more important do you think?

**Subject —** I’d rather get paid less but be able to get out of bed and go to work everyday than get paid more and absolutely hate what I do like for 5 days a week. So money – obviously I don’t want to get a degree here to earn the same as someone that has walked straight out of school erm but I – I also, I’m not obsessed with it. And location doesn’t bother me at all. Anywhere in the world really erm so long as they’re English or French speaking. Erm, so that’s not anywhere in the world, is it? No, like America, Australia, Canada – anywhere like that. That’d be fine cos I mean my family are dotted all over the world anyway, so I’d just be another addition for the holiday possibilities.

**Interviewer —** OK, in terms of your career then, where do you see yourself in say 5 years time?

**Subject —** In 5 years? Er dunno. Erm, in 5 years time I definitely want to have a job and a house – I don’t want to be living at home – and working – I would like to work in aerospace because with an aeronautical engineering degree, you’d get more money in aerospace because you’re qualified for what they want you for and because there’s not that many of us, they have to offer you more money to do it, I suppose. Erm, I would – preferably, to make me feel like the degree was worthwhile, I would like to be working in aerospace, but it’s not completely 100% necessary. I have actually been looking into a PGCE to look at teacher training so I don’t really know.

**Interviewer —** Ok, what about in say 10 years time? Do you think you might go into management or anything like that?

**Subject —** Yeah, like I said before like all the – all the hands on stuff and all that kind of thing, would be really good for the degree and I understand fully everything that’s going on, and get good exam grades and just leave with full knowledge of the subject but then, like I don’t want to – I think management would be good, sort of something – you feel like if you were gonna work 4 years for a masters and then maybe do extra, after that, like a PhD or something, you feel like you deserve to not actually erm you know, drive a white van or whatever. You know the – you want to – you don’t want to have to settle for anything – when you’ve worked so hard you want to – you want to be – I don’t know what the word is – head hunted almost. You think that you deserve that kind of appreciation for what you’ve done, but obviously there’s thousands and thousands of students that all want the same. They all want everyone to think they’re the best thing in the world, so. Erm, but management would be good – something – I want to progress in whatever I do. If I became a teacher, I wouldn’t want to teach primary school kids for the rest of my life. I’d want to be Head of Department or Deputy Head of school, that kind of thing. I wouldn’t – you want to progress in whatever you do really.
