## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (1): pleased*

**Interviewer —** Okay. How useful do you think that your degree has been so far to the work that you’re currently doing?

**Subject —** Certain modules very useful. Stuff like the materials module we did was very useful because the work I’ve done has mostly been reformulating, like, rubbers and things and modules like Thermo have been useful to the work that I’ve been doing but that’s because of the projects that I’ve been working on so the other guys here have found different things useful.

**Interviewer —** Yeah. Okay. Is there anything particular that you wish that you had learnt before you started the placement that would have been useful?

**Subject —** A more in-depth knowledge of material would have helped but we’ve been taught it. They basically looked at what we already knew and built on it. So it would have helped just because we wouldn’t have had to learn so much in the first couple of months. I guess wherever you go you’re going to be thrown in at the deep end and you learn a lot to start off with.

**Interviewer —** Okay. Do you think that you’ll use what you’re learning on placement in your final year?

**Subject —** Yes. Definitely things like report writing has become like second nature because everything you do, you have to report back. So there’s that. You used to get a piece of like coursework and be ‘oh god I’ve got to write a report’ and now it’ll be do that in an hour sort of thing. You know the style and you learn a lot of tricks to make things easier.

**Interviewer —** Do you think you’ll use anything from work for that?

**Subject —** Bits yes, but it depends what final year project I get, whether it’s related to this. The skills I’ve learned I’ll be able to transfer but the knowledge, it will depend what project I get.

**Interviewer —** Yes. Your experience of engineering in the workplace?

**Subject —** Fun. Material-based. Interesting. Challenging. Rewarding.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry*

**Interviewer —** Okay. How would you summarise your placement experience so far then?

**Subject —** Really useful I guess.

**Interviewer —** What do you think have been the best and worst things about the placement?

**Subject —** The best thing is generally seeing something you’ve made go - you know people are actually using that in the field now. I joined right at the end of one project and they had a last minute panic, and something I did, basically is now being used. So that’s really good. The worst things is that - I don’t know - it can get a bit repetitive just because of the nature of the job, you tend - if you’re testing something you tend to do two or three rounds of testing on the same thing, because you know you tweak it and you test it again. So it can get a bit boring but only for sort of two or three days. You don’t do the same thing constantly.

**Interviewer —** What things do you think that you’ve gained from doing the placement?

**Subject —** Just understanding work because it is so different from Uni. I think it’ll help me prepare for getting a job as a graduate as well. Things that I’ve seen - I know, like, how companies work, I’ve also seen, like, interviews, so - and I’ve interviewed people so I know more what people are looking for in an interview. That’s been really good actually, really enjoyed that. And also it’s quite specific here so I’ve gained a lot of knowledge about materials and I enjoy it, so I guess I’ve gained knowledge of what I’d like to do when I graduate.

**Interviewer —** Okay. Do you think that there are any drawbacks to doing a placement, at all or do you think it’s all positive?

**Subject —** It takes a year, so it’s a year that you’re not - you’re earning money but it’s not a graduate pay, whereas if you didn’t obviously you’d be earning more earlier. That’s about it really.
