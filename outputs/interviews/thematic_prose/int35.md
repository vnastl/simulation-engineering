# Interview INT35 — thematic prose

*The full answer to each recurring interview question, after probing — shown as the verbatim exchange and linked to the variables it informs.*

## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (11): highsal, interest, family, hobbies, varied, mother, father, careers, teacher, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** OK, what do your parents do? Are either of them in the industry at all?

**Subject —** No, my dad’s just a business man. I don’t really know what he does. Erm, my mum she works in a big cash and carry – she does administration.

**Interviewer —** OK, when and why then do you think that you decided to do Design and Technology?

**Subject —** Well, you do like a bit of Design Technology like – at my school you do it in like – from Year 7, till like up to GCSE’s – it’s compulsory, like everyone does a bit of it. So I thought it was – that was like a bit of a taster and I liked it, like making things – I thought that was quite interesting, so I thought I’d carry on. If I’m enjoying it I might as well carry on.

**Interviewer —** Yeah. OK, do you think anything influenced you to do it? Did you have any careers advice before you decided to go to university?

**Subject —** Erm, well we had these like – we were able to do these little tests things where we’d try and find out what careers we’ve get. Mine was a little bit creative, so they were saying like you could be a designer. That was on the list and I was thinking, oh that sounds quite nice.

**Interviewer —** OK, so what about your family – have they been supportive?

**Subject —** Yeah, they’ve been supportive but I wouldn’t say they’ve influenced me as such as. My sister – she’s physiotherapist so she hadn’t done design, she didn’t really like design and my mum doesn’t either.

**Interviewer —** What do you think it is about Design & Technology that appeals to you?

**Subject —** I like the creative side definitely more than the technology bit. Erm, I like the sort of – it’s nice to know I’ve got a course where I can do a bit of drawing and it’s not just like all essays all the time, so I like that side of it.

**Interviewer —** Ok, do you think that coming from a girls’ school you’re encouraged more than maybe other girls are to do Design & Technology.

**Subject —** Maybe. I think because erm when you choose choices – you are in very small sets, er just doing design and girls and then I think only a few of us went on to do ‘A’ levels. The teacher kind of encouraged it. They said that’s what I want to do and they said, don’t be discouraged because it might be a lot of boys and things like that, so…

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): curricul, pleased, Fhelp, interper, supplect, supppers, groupwk, designwk, variety*

**Interviewer —** OK, talking a bit about the course then, has it been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Not quite. (laughs) it’s been a lot of harder than I thought it would be. It’s a lot – they put emphasis on the technology cos when we first came they were saying it’s industrial design and technology, it’s not like just industrial design and then the technology bit. It’s like, you know, design & technology together. And it’s like they – they see you drawing and then they sort of criticise your drawing and say you can’t draw right, so you start to feel a bit like hmm, maybe I’m not quite as good on this course. You see other people doing it and it comes really naturally (yeah) because they tell us to sketch things and then the way of sketching that they like – like really fast but accurate and I had a bit of a problem – a few problems with that and that was done and it’s not gone quite exactly as I thought It’s different to my ‘A’ levels (yeah) – definitely.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think have been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** The worst thing I would say was doing computer design – CAD, because I’ve never done it before – ever and they start you off, some of – in the first IT lesson we had I think we were learning how to send an email. So it really was a good start, cos I know how to do that – you know, carry on from there and it might be quite, quite OK, you know. But then like they’d completely skipped onto this new erm programme like for engineers, that we use mostly, and we can design things on that, but I’d never used it before and the – the menus are like really weird and hard to get hold of (yeah) so I had like really – a really hard time like getting the grasp of that, but you do get the help that – like, the lecturer that we had for that he wasn’t particularly that helpful and a lot of people complained about it, so like trying to do an assignment, you’d just kind of help each other out and there’s me trying to get the hang of it and each time you have a bit more work you get, like a better grasp of it but I found that quite hard – I still do (yeah). But I don’t know what the best thing – the best thing I enjoy. I do like erm a module I’m doing now, it’s called Product Analysis and Artefacts and that’s a really creative one. Like they don’t ask for technology, all like you design something - they don’t ask like how would it work? They don’t ask precisely what electronics have you got? And it’s just kind of like – it’s, it’s – I think it’s more fun that way because you get to be like creative and think like innovative things, and we look at like, like shops and we look at like brands and products and it’s that side of design that I prefer like the more creative, thinking side instead of – yeah, cos like I like drawing but I don’t say that I’m the best and I wouldn’t like to draw all the time either (yeah) cos there’s another module called Styling and they do a lot of like rendering and things like that and I’m thinking hmm, I don’t like that too much.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, you don’t think that they treat the female and male students differently at all?

**Subject —** There’s this one assistant in our department and he is known for helping girls out more than boys (OK) and we were doing an electronics project last year and I had a friend, a girl and a boy and the boy went up to ask him sort of questions, for some help and he said, look in this book. And the girl asked the same questions and he did all the stuff for her like looked up all these things, off these sheets and things and helped her out and went to check on her and stuff. So I think he was a bit more helpful obviously to the girls than the boys – and the boys got a bit angry.

**Interviewer —** Do you like doing group work?

**Subject —** I do actually. I’ve been quite lucky because erm the people either that you’ve been put with, or that you pick yourself, when I’ve been put in the groups, my people have been like we’ve all been helpful, and friendly, and willing to work – like the boys and girls and that’s been fine but I’ve heard a few people who’ve said their groups have been not good to help them work with, because some people just aren’t willing to help other people – they just do their bit and they just leave other people, even if they find it hard they won’t help them out. I don’t know if that works quite so well.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (10): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, placloca, breaked, noappeal*

**Interviewer —** Do you think that you’ll use the technical skills that you’ve learnt as much as the generic skills like the group work?

**Subject —** Yeah, I think all the – depending on what type of placement you choose because you’re choosing yourselves (yeah). Erm, I’ve sort of leaned away from all the ones that ask for technical things just cos I don’t want to be stuck, for like a year, doing something I’d be bored with or not think I’d be able to manage too well. So, but obviously you’re doing placements that are more creative than technical but you do get – mostly, they would ask for a bit of both – for design placements especially, because like being modern times then it’s like there are – people are using more computers, so to have computer skills and maybe not quite so much computer designing on programmes, just sort of basic computer skills like Word and programmes like that, it’s helpful, I think. So you can’t just like be pen and paper and drawing, you’ve got to know how to do things on computers.

**Interviewer —** Yeah. OK. Why do you want to go on placement then?

**Subject —** I thought it would be a really good way of getting work experience, because people say you can do these things in the summer but often they’re not paying things, so I like to work in the summer and get a bit of money for the year, so I tend to not apply for like design placements on my summer, so I tend to have a bit of like time to relax as well. And so, if you did like a placement, you get to like see how design works in industry, like it’s completely different to designing on a course, like getting a project set. I mean you might get projects set like in your job, but working differently you know – they leave everything to yourself, like you have to do everything, organise everything by yourself, and if you’re in a group you’ve got to do everything together, you’re not gonna have like a lecturer coming in and making sure you’ve done things (yeah). So I think it’s gonna be very different and strange.

**Interviewer —** Has anyone particularly encouraged or discouraged you from going on placement at all?

**Subject —** I’ve spoken to a few 3rd years – I’ve been asking why they haven’t gone on a placement (right) erm and they’ve basically said they want to get like the final year over with – they want to get University over with, so they can carry – like go out into the world and see what they want to do. Erm, and I was – I’m kind of like – I’ve applied for – to go on a placement course, but I’ve been thinking like at the end of last year, thinking I don’t know if I want one – if I want one, cos like if you get like a year’s experience that would be really good but I’ve always been like scared that like you’d lose some skills, like if your placement didn’t have emphasis on like computer design as such, by the time you came back you might forget everything – yeah. Or, if you don’t like sketch all the time, when you come back and you’ve got to do some sketching, it might be like really rusty (yeah) and also like if you just get a placement and it’s not quite what you expected, then I’d be like wasting time as such, like you wouldn’t be enjoying it, whereas you just want to get your course finished with, so…

**Interviewer —** Have you looked at other things like training opportunities, or pay, or location?

**Subject —** Er, I’ve looked at location cos for a while I didn’t know what I wanted to do, I didn’t know whether I was going for a placement or not (yeah) cos I didn’t really want to go for a placement and have to live somewhere by myself, cos I know of someone from around the University who’s gone on an engineering placement, he’s in Yorkshire, by himself, with no-one around and they’re all old people. He’s got really bored and I didn’t like the sound of that, like it turns me off it, but quite a lot, so I was thinking if I was looking for a placement I’d look for ones like round here, cos I live only an hour away from [university town] so either in [university town], I could stay here, or like round home and I could still be like near [this university] where my friends are. So erm I’m thinking OK, maybe I’ll do that and then, all my friends started to get houses and I’m like thinking OK, what if I don’t get a placement? I might be stuck for somewhere to live (yeah) and like have to finish the final year and not have anyone like my friends to live with, so I’m a little bit panicky. So I decided – I thought I’d live in [university town], so I’ve got a house now (OK) so when I’m looking for placements now, they’re definitely around [university area], like where I can commute to. So I’ve restricted myself to that. Erm, pay – I haven’t looked at like quite a bit. I mean obviously it’s nice to get paid but I’m thinking like, if – if you get paid that’s a bonus already like, because they’ll be paying you something and you’re getting like good experience out of work if you choose the right one, so I haven’t worried too much about pay – I haven’t looked for a high salary and just aimed for those, I haven’t looked at that too much.
