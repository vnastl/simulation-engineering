## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): interest, special, family, varied, mother, careers, discoura, whodisc, school*

**Interviewer —** OK. When and why then do you think that you decided to do design technology?

**Subject —** Erm, I always liked arty things, from my mum, and then at school I found – I mean I was an all rounder but I always enjoyed art and design technology and then it was just one of my best subjects at GCSE which I really – I really enjoyed it at GCSE so I did it for A level and then it was just the obvious choice really.

**Interviewer —** OK. Do you think that the fact that your mum is involved in the industry has influenced you at all?

**Subject —** Erm, I think it gave me an interest in it, but it was just the fact that I enjoy it and that I'm good at it, that made me choose it really.

**Interviewer —** Yeah. Did you have any careers advice or anything like that?

**Subject —** We had Careers Advice at school but I don't think it was that helpful. But I mean it always came up as one of the options that erm I was suited to. You know we went on computer programmes and spoke to advisors.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think it is that appeals to you about the subject?

**Subject —** The subject I'm doing here is so broad, compared to other degrees. You get to do engineering subjects like mechanics and electronics but you get to do – you get to be creative, but you also get to make things as well, which you don't – you don't in many other things so – it's just so broad and varied. I think it's a lot more exciting than sitting, just listening to lots of lectures.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you ever consider doing any other subjects?

**Subject —** Erm, I considered doing something more engineering based but I thought that I'd miss the graphics side of it (yes) and I also considered doing the same course but with a language on the side, like French because I did that at A level, but now I'm just doing that on the side here rather than as part of my degree.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that being in an all girls school affected your choice of subjects at all? Do you think you were encouraged more to do maths or science type subjects?

**Subject —** You're encouraged more to do maths and science and IT but – and they weren't going to run design technology for my 'A' levels – for our year, but we did a petition and – I mean I was going to swap schools if they didn't provide it for me because I'd decided that's what I wanted to do. But, it did make me want to be more academic in my choices in general.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (11): pleased, Mconfid, Fhelp, deadline, interper, supppers, groupwk, teachhrs, friends, coursewk, variety*

**Interviewer —** Ok. Does it ever bother you that there aren't many women on the course?

**Subject —** Erm, kind of. It's more the fact that it was quite intimidating and I felt – just things like men in the workshops woodwork and things, I just felt as though I would have less background in it because I know it's a stereotypical kind of view that, you know, boys will do that outside of school and I just thought that I'd be further behind and I think boys in general tend to be a lot more confident about their work and it's something that you need to be confident of your own ideas. But I mean, after the first – after the first few months, I realised that I was just on the same level as everyone else really – fine.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that any of those characteristics are more male or more female? I know you said earlier males are probably more confident.

**Subject —** Yeah, I think in general they're a lot more confident when they explain their ideas and show them, whereas when we have girls in the class, they'll explain it but they'll point out the bad bits or they'll apologise and in general the guys don't do that and er the – I think a lot of the girls tend to be neater with their work, rather than just, you know, drawing it and sharing – but on the other hand there's things that girls have, like we keep getting told that females have better appreciation of colour and they're more sensitive to things like shapes and things – which is also useful. I think it's just mainly the confidence thing – the guys tend to be a lot more confident than the girls in their work, and it shows.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think has been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Hmm, the worst things are the amount of work but then we were told to expect that and the fact that it's a lot of course work, so we have a lot of deadlines in, at the same time, and it's – you don't get much sleep at all. And it – a lot of the work is very time consuming. You can't just – the fact that you – you never actually hand it all in and have no work to do because there's always an on-going project. But then, I suppose that's something that I like anyway. Erm, and the best thing – hmm, I think our department in general, because we have a lot more seminars and workshops. I like the fact that we're a lot more bonded than other courses. We know everyone in the year and we see each other – we can chat during lesson time – it's like school, you can just sit there and work and talk to each other and I really like that.

**Interviewer —** OK, are you pleased that you decided to do this course?

**Subject —** Er, yes – still. I've had moments where I've just been overwhelmed by the amount of work, but other than that I do really enjoy it and it is what I want to carry on doing.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, or female students.

**Subject —** Erm, I think some of the male lecturers are more helpful to the girls than to the guys. Female lecturers I think tend to be the same to everyone. But then I think it might be because the girls come across as less confident that the teachers would want to you know, help them more.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (10): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, unigrade, finalyr, placothe*

**Interviewer —** OK, did you always want to do a sandwich course or did you just like the course and it happened to have…?

**Subject —** No, I didn't. I only changed to that this September (OK). At first I was a bit too scared to go out into industry, in the middle of my degree. I'm still a bit scared I think – it will be really strange but, after advice from my parents I decided to go for it, because I just thought that it adds to your degree and I've been told that you get better marks in the final year, generally and also it will just – in my field you always need a minimum of 1 year's experience, and it'll be an easy way to get that.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the university could do anything more to help prepare you?

**Subject —** Er, we weren't given much help – we haven't been given much help for applying for jobs (right) and I mean I think it's quite a competitive field. My friends, in other universities, on similar courses, have been told – they were told a lot earlier, about the whole process and erm told to start applying to lots of different companies early on. And I'm finding now that I'm ready – I've missed out on quite a few opportunities because a lot of the companies have stopped taking applicants, which is a shame but on the other hand they did tell us that it would be up to us to find a placement and they – they have given us a few emails about companies, but they just haven't given us that much yet.

**Interviewer —** Why did you decide to do the placement in the end?

**Subject —** Because well I'm not ready for third year because I need to have a great idea for a final project and gain confidence about my work. And I'd like to gain some extra knowledge in a year in between. I think it will be good experience, not just work wise but also personally, which is (?) another person and also, I mean there's the more serious things that my parents have told me about – you know – that you generally get higher marks, it kind of gives you the experience already that you'll need later on.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think will be the best and the worst things about going on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, the best thing will just be the experience – the new experience for a year in the company and you know, it's kind of a taster for what I want to do for a job, so it's just practice. I don't need to worry too much and just anything that I learn from it. The negative things will be I think if I'm away from university then I'll miss out on the final year of all my other friends and that's probably the only negative thing. Another good thing will be getting some money so I'll be more out of debt.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): carpath, workrole, salary, location, people, training*

**Interviewer —** OK, in terms of your career then, where do you see yourself in say 5 years time?

**Subject —** Erm, I don't really know. I'm hoping that my year in industry will help me decide, because there's so many things that we can go into. I mean there's a design consultancy which can be lots of different products or a company that does one specific product, or I mean you can go into packaging or something more engineering and yeah, I'm hoping next year, we'll focus more on the kind of path that I want to take.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think would be your ideal career then?

**Subject —** Hmm, I think working in a consultancy appeals to me the most. It's just – I mean eventually I'd like to – I live in London so I'd like to move back there and work in there you know, a fast-paced company with lots of things happening all the time – lots of exciting ideas coming out and getting some money.

**Interviewer —** OK, and would you like to go into management?

**Subject —** No. Management doesn't really appeal to me – managing other people. I just like being part of the group.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that you'll encounter any difficulties trying to achieve your ideal career?

**Subject —** Yes, I mean it's so competitive and I'm not – I don't know I'm particularly ambitious so I probably would be just happy working for a smaller company that's – does one product type. As long as I'm just – I'm enjoying my work and I'm comfortable money wise. So I mean there might be a lack of motivation.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that career prospects are good in design and technology?

**Subject —** I think once you've got a foot in the door, yeah and that's why I'm hoping that I'll get a good placement for next year. But, on the other hand, I think – I get the impression that it is very competitive, but coming from [this university] I think you've already got a head start – we've been told because [this university] and [university] are the highest universities for our course, so we should be more highly regarded, from the start.
