## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (0): none*

**Interviewer —** OK, how would you describe the company’s attitude to women?

**Subject —** They have never had a female flight manager before, so I’m the first. And – we’re known as brokers? – there are no female brokers within the Cargo Department but there are a few in the Passenger Department. Um, they’re very aware that it’s a male-dominated world actually, and to be quite honest they’re quite male-dominated as well. So they’re learning, they’re learning still, with women.

**Interviewer —** OK, we’ll talk a bit more about that later. Um, how useful do you think that your degree so far has been to the work you’re doing?

**Subject —** Really really useful, it’s really relevant. Um, because I’m working in the air industry, so I’m doing management for aircraft as well, so yeah, it’s been really good.

**Interviewer —** OK. Are there any particular skills that have been useful?

**Subject —** Just generally, um, part of my course is, you know, aircraft knowledge and what it takes to run an airline, so that’s been quite helpful. Maybe some of the theory parts as to, you know, the psychology of transport, isn’t so helpful to us, but you know, the real practical parts have been quite good.

**Interviewer —** … or do you think that the course could involve more practical…?

**Subject —** No, I think, I think it’s a good balance. You know, I think I’m quite lucky in this placement because I know a lot of students will be just doing the theory as part of their placement so, I’m pretty lucky.

**Interviewer —** How do you think doing teamwork in the workplace compares to doing teamwork at uni?

**Subject —** For us it’s a matter of (our job or not?), so we really do rely on each other, you know what each person is capable of doing within the job. Within the university, I don’t know, it still feels like you’re playing a bit, its still, oh XY and Z will do this and this, within work you just know straight away what person can achieve that and you tend to pick up on their strengths and weaknesses a bit better, I prefer it.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (1): placemen*

**Interviewer —** OK, how would you summarise the placement experience so far?

**Subject —** Um, it’s been absolutely fantastic. It’s been put down as the best placement you can do for my course, it’s been amazing – I’ve been places, seen things, that I wouldn’t have ever done. I’ve had the experiences and it’s making me grow up because I want to be in charge of a hell of a lot more than I did at university, so you know, one day I can be in the Sahara and the next day flying round England. It’s a great experience and (inaudible).

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think that you’ve gained from the placement so far?

**Subject —** A lot of experience of the airline industry, how it works, people’s attitudes, which you don’t get taught in the classroom. Um, (inaudible) responsibility, you know, it’s been good for me to grow up and have to organise myself, and get myself half way across the continent and back again. So it’s, you know, organisational abilities.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that there are any drawbacks at all to doing a placement?

**Subject —** To doing a placement? No. You can only really take positive things and it can help you decide what you want to do – like I don’t ever want to work in an office again, or, you know. I think it can be positive even if you don’t enjoy it that much.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that your career ambitions have changed at all as a result of the placement?

**Subject —** Um, yes. When I first got this placement I really felt quite lucky because we’re the highest paid placement, we were like yeah, look what we get to do. But now money doesn’t matter, it’s how I feel about the job. I couldn’t care if I got paid £8,000 a year but I was happy. £17,000 - you know, I don’t really get a social life apart from my workmates, but even then we’re never in the same place at the same time. Um, you certainly don’t get to form relationships. I mean my placement tutor said to me, how do you feel about doing this after (inaudible). I said well I sound a bit like a 15 year old but I want to like go out with someone and be in the same place at the same time for more than about 30 seconds. So I mean I want to have a life, eventually.

**Interviewer —** Yeah OK. Do you think that the placement experience will help you when you start work after you leave?

**Subject —** I think I’ve got pretty much, um, unique kind of skills through this one, so either I’ll go straight back into kind of brokering for airlines or I might go back and do this job again, but yeah, I think it’s been a pretty good experience for me to have on my CV.
