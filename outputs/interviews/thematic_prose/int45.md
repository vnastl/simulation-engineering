# Interview INT45 — thematic prose

## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): interest, challeng, special, mathsci, knowledg, family, varied, school*

**Interviewer —** Right, OK. When and why then do you think that you decided to study engineering?

**Subject —** I don’t really know what it was to make me actually do engineering. I think my grades sort of geared me towards doing those ‘A’ levels, and then having chosen them it seemed the logical step. I knew I didn’t want to do erm maths, as a degree subject. I thought it narrowed it down a bit too much, engineering was quite broad (yeah) and sort of where my strengths lie, so that was why I chose the subject.

**Interviewer —** So were there were any other influences on your decision, like careers advice, or were your family supportive?

**Subject —** They were supportive but it was really left to my decision. I sort of got the books out and chose what course I wanted to do. I did sort of think about joining the Navy, which erm may have sort of influenced it because it was more what did I want to do in the Navy? And I wanted to be an engineer. Erm, but that was sort of more before I chose my ‘A’ levels, really.

**Interviewer —** OK, what is it, do you think, that appeals to you about engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, I don’t know if it’s one particular thing. It’s certainly the kind of works – the mathematical side of it and sort of seeing how things work and how you can improve them and everything and sort of how my mind’s always worked. So I suppose it was that kind of thing attracted me to it.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you know any engineers to talk to?

**Subject —** Erm, my uncle, who I’m not sort of particularly close to. Erm but he’s an engineer – I couldn’t actually tell you what sort of engineering he does but he was always sort of saying oh engineering, it’s a good thing to do. Erm, I didn’t really know anyone my age or anything that was going into it.

**Interviewer —** Do you think it is just the subjects that you did, that meant it was a natural progression. Do you think if you’d have done other subjects you’d have known what engineering involved?

**Subject —** No, because although I didn’t really research it, I knew what engineering was and I knew pretty much what to expect, sort of once I got to university and things. And – but yeah, I suppose it was the subjects that sort of influenced that progression.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): pleased, Fhelp, interper, supplect, groupwk, variety*

**Interviewer —** OK Do you think that there are any advantages and disadvantages to being a woman engineer?

**Subject —** Erm, I certainly haven’t found it at Uni. I mean there’s obviously always the jokes of oh, the woman, you know, don’t let her touch anything or whatever. But, when it really comes down to it, if you’re working in a group, I think the men tend to be more helpful because you’re female (yeah). So I suppose yeah, it works both ways. I mean you get the disadvantages of the little comments but the advantage is that they actually do help when it comes down to them.

**Interviewer —** How do you deal with like getting little comments all the time?

**Subject —** I think to be honest, if you’ve chosen to do engineering as a woman, knowing that it’s male dominated, and you’ve got to pretty much the end of your second year, you sort of know what to expect and I wouldn’t really say it’s anything – it’s more sort of banter between people, which I’ve never really had a problem with.

**Interviewer —** OK. What do you think have been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Oh gosh. Erm, I think probably the worst so far has been a particular group project which has basically run across the whole of this year and you didn’t get to pick the groups you were in, and that was really difficult. I think that’s more like a personal experience as opposed to how everyone found it. The best thing – I’d say that there’s always the support there and the lecturers are really good at – if you’ve got a problem, you can always go and find them and get help. Yeah, that’s probably the best thing.

**Interviewer —** In what way?

**Subject —** Everything – sort of basically, I think I know what my strengths are from doing that project, and I’ll be able to sort of take those and know I’m confident in going off and doing say report writing or something. Erm, and I think also, I’ve probably learnt you know how to tolerate problems, because people aren’t always going to pull the same, so that’ll be a help.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (10): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, industry, perdevel, unigrade, apptheor, finalyr, placothe*

**Interviewer —** OK, why did you want to go on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, it’s kind of hard really because I didn’t want to do it at first and then as the information started coming through, I changed to being on a DIS course, so I had the option. Erm, I basically looked at the fact that you get an extra qualification out of it, but hopefully it’ll improve your grade at the end of it. The skills, obviously that we do take there and use, you’re gonna use so much further than you could learn in a lecture, so hopefully I’ll actually sort of understand applying it rather than well here’s another example, here’s another exam paper. Erm, and I think that side of it, and the fact that I would then be able to come back fresh, you know, finish my degree and then hopefully go and sort of find a job which my placement will sort of assist me in.

**Interviewer —** OK, why, when you first started did you think that you didn’t want to do the placement?

**Subject —** Erm, well when I first came to uni, it just seemed like an extra year and although I was aware that you got a qualification, I didn’t quite realise that the implications of that putting you so much more ahead of people that have just done the straight course. But I just sort of considered, oh it’s an extra year I don’t sort of want to spend any longer than I have to, getting the qualification, but now I’m sort of seeing it in a different light.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you apply to any other companies?

**Subject —** Erm, I didn’t apply as such to other companies, I more sort of approached them to see if they were interested at all, which most of them sort of weren’t big enough to take a student. Erm, and then I applied to [company] in [town], erm then I went for an interview there. But that seemed actually – quite a good point – it seemed like a really male orientated job and when I got there they were sort of quite shocked to sort of see a female, even to the point where the female toilets were locked and things like that. Erm, it was a lot of sort of hands on welding work and everything.

**Interviewer —** What are you most looking forward to and most worried about going on placement?

**Subject —** I wouldn’t say there’s anything I’m actually worried about – there’s a few things I’m apprehensive about, such as you know, who you’re gonna live with and sorting that out. And it seems like a relatively short time as well, because from when I finish here, I start there 5 weeks later but then I know other people who have only got a week, so. Erm, I’m quite excited about meeting new people, working with new people – hopefully learning quite a bit from the year. Erm, slightly apprehensive that they’re gonna expect too much but kind of hoping that when you get there, they’ll be able to assess it like.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): carpath, workrole, charship, workenv, people, training, promot*

**Interviewer —** OK, in terms of your career then, what do you see yourself doing in say 5 years time?

**Subject —** Erm, I don’t know. I would like to become a Chartered Engineer – it’s sort of what I’m aiming towards. I suppose I’d like to think that I was really happy with Xerox and they were really happy with me and I ended up there, and I’d probably still be there in 5 years, so.

**Interviewer —** Do you think you’ll have like climbed the career ladder?

**Subject —** I’d like to think so. I’m quite ambitious. I’d certainly like to do more than just have an engineering degree and go into basic engineering.

**Interviewer —** Do you think if you do progress upwards, have you got in mind now, whether you’d like to follow like a technical route or a managerial route?

**Subject —** I don’t really know, to be honest. I haven’t really considered it that far.

**Interviewer —** If there isn’t an actual job that you can say would be your ideal job, what sort of characteristics do you think your ideal job would have?

**Subject —** Erm, definitely some kind of design work. Erm, yeah possibly sort of managerial as well and from being able to basically improve on optimising, so I think those would be the main things.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that women are sometimes made to feel like that they have to choose between career and having a family?

**Subject —** No, because I know that I want both and I think, even more so now, for a woman to go back to work, after a family, and continue with her career is quite normal.
