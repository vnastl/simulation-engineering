## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (12): interest, special, mathsci, knowledg, family, varied, mother, father, careers, discoura, whodisc, school*

**Interviewer —** OK then, when and why do you think that you decided to do design & technology?

**Subject —** Probably first year – first year of secondary school. Year 7, because the teachers were nice to me. The teachers were nice and it was a cool place to go and there wasn’t much else to do at lunchtimes, so you could go there and just do whatever you wanted to and it builds your confidence, with the machinery and everything. And it just seemed to be something I was OK at but I understood the mechanics and the physics of it as well – so I just tied the two together.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think their views have sort of encouraged you really then?

**Subject —** Partly yes, but partly because – the reason – there was like a big group of us, all my friends did DT as well because it because it was compulsory then. And I chose it at GCSE because I didn’t like IT and at ‘A’ levels – I got my highest GCSE in DT and ‘cos I chose my ‘A’ levels through my highest subjects at GCSE, because I loved science but at the same time I liked English and the arts as well and if I did science all day I’d get bored, if I did art all day I’d get bored, so DT’s a nice crossover.

**Interviewer —** Did you have much careers advice, when you were at school?

**Subject —** Lots – they tried to persuade me against it.

**Interviewer —** What did they say to you?

**Subject —** They said that I had a good er I would say RSA qualification and I had good communication skills and I got an A at ‘A’ levels in my English, so I should do journalism or management and work in an office environment but I just didn’t want to do that. It’s not me at all because I’ve always got on better with lads really than girls. Still do but erm lads are very straightforward and a spade’s a spade basically. I’d rather have people being honest – I hate having a bit of back stabbing, so I decided to carry on with it because I felt comfortable with it.

**Interviewer —** Did you do much research career wise?

**Subject —** Lots and lots and lots because I’d got a couple of friends who were 4 or 5 years older, from school because at school I was involved in music a lot. So I thought it was a really good opportunity to build up my confidence and mix with the lads at the same time, but get to know people who were a bit older, because I always seemed to be a bit older than my year – I was always seemed to get on better with a bit – people who were a bit older than me and er one of the girls had done industrial design technology and had come out and found a really, really good job and she was a car designer now – it was wonderful but then I was aware that the people who did management, or journalism didn’t have jobs to go to. And, even though the careers advisor was wonderful, at school, and she was saying oh you know, it’s a good steady job and it’s been around for years so it’ll be around for many more years – industrial design’s always been a bit of a hazy one and – because we can fit into lots of different roles like design engineers, or car designers or graphic designers and even on the university campus here, if people ask you what you do – you say industrial design technology, they’re like ‘oh, that’s nice’ (‘what is it?’) Yeah, and er most of them tend to think it’s all Art Schooly BA but it’s not because we do a BSc and we have to do number crunchers like everybody else.

**Interviewer —** OK, going back to the characteristics – do you think that any of the characteristics are more male or female characteristics?

**Subject —** No, because, this is my mum’s upbringing now – this is dreadful – I sound like my mum, but – I’ll have to explain my family history - my mum and dad got divorced when I was 5, my mum suddenly became very feminist and she said you can do anything, don’t end up like your dad and so I’ve always had it drummed into me, confidence and things like that. But I don’t think that any characteristic has to be necessarily female or male.
