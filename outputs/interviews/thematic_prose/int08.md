# Interview INT08 — thematic prose

## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (11): interest, mathsci, knowledg, family, varied, mother, father, careers, teacher, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** OK, what do your parents do? Are either of them engineers?

**Subject —** Erm, my mum isn't – not at all. She's more into financial stuff, but she's retired now. She was like a clerical assistant. My dad works for the local council, but he's previously studied up to HNC level in electrical engineering and electronics. So…

**Interviewer —** OK, so when and why do you think you did decide to study engineering?

**Subject —** Er, I think I did it partly because no one else was really doing it at the time. I thought it might be quite interesting, and it's quite broad – I didn't want to limit my options, at the time. What else? I think I liked the fact that it had a design side to it, I thought it might be a different way of getting into design and understanding it from a different point of view, and hopefully become a better designer. That's my theory anyway.

**Interviewer —** Did you have any careers advice while you were at school?

**Subject —** Yeah, there was a careers service but they didn't really – they explained to me all the options but they didn't really have much influence on my choice at all.

**Interviewer —** Did they tell you much about doing engineering?

**Subject —** No, not really. It was one of those things I had to kind of look out for myself (yeah) and I think the reason – I got to sixth form, where I think my physics teacher, she's the one that really knew more about the wider organisations and stuff like that. But, previously, there hadn't been any information or promotion of it really.

**Interviewer —** Did you actually– consider all the others and rule them out before you did decide?

**Subject —** Yeah, I did look at all of them and like looking at the University prospectuses to see what you can learn in the course, what modules you covered and I just felt that – I knew I didn't want to specialise too soon, especially not having any experience in the industry, it's a bit too early to say. Yeah – what – I had a tendency towards – I thought I'd keep it to the mechanical because it seemed to be the broadest or at least cover different aspects of each sort of engineering, so yeah.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): practica, pleased, competit, interper, supppers, practwk*

**Interviewer —** OK, that's fine. I'm going to ask you a few things about the course that you're on now, so has the course been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Erm, to some extent no, I expected it to be a bit more practical (right). Erm, the theory isn't too bad, but there's so much to take in and to understand and sometimes to be – I don't know – be on the ball with it, all the time. Er, I think for me, I'd personally like a bit more of the practical in work so…

**Interviewer —** OK, so what do you think have been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Erm, OK. Erm, the best – I might have to come back to that. The worst – the only thing I can think of is just the time spent – it's just not going to lectures, that's it. It's more or less like a 24/7 thing. Erm, yeah, so much of it you have to balance – more or less always be thinking about the subjects. But, I suppose, that might be a good thing in a way because I suppose in real life, or when you've finished uni, there's so many things that you're gonna be thinking about and have to balance anyway, that you'll probably be better prepared for it.

**Interviewer —** One of the questions that I was going to ask you later actually, was how do you think your educational experience differs to other students?

**Subject —** Definitely. I think the Social Science students, they have the time to read up and things – really understand it for themselves. Prepare themselves and I suppose that extra time also – on a communicative level – they're able to – probably express themselves better because their minds are much clearer, whereas I suppose engineering is – I don't know – stereotype; not being able to express themselves that well. I suppose that might be the case, I haven't really spoken to everyone to know if that's the situation.

**Interviewer —** Are you happy about that? Would you like a female personal tutor or aren't you bothered?

**Subject —** Er, no he's fine. I had one, like in the foundation year beforehand and I had a male tutor then and it really wasn't a problem. I think, once again, it was basically that they'd been in industry and they'd got the experience if you have any questions. They're there like to reassure you or guide you kind of thing. I think if I'd had a male – sorry a female tutor, it'd probably have a bit of comparison. I think the only difference is maybe, character at the moment. Because we're all different – so otherwise, for what they're really meant to do, it really doesn't influence me at all.

**Interviewer —** OK, have you had to do much group work since you started the course?

**Subject —** It's very rare that you're working by yourself – it's got to be said. And a lot of our coursework, labs, our project work, it's all group work. We've had a lot of it this year. Er, even in our first year, when we were set tutorial questions, that was – for the dynamics part anyway – that was the emphasis was all placed on group work and submitting as a group, so I suppose they introduce it to you from your first year.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): placemen, experien, educatio, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, apptheor*

**Interviewer —** OK, we've talked about this a little bit already but why did you want to do the placement?

**Subject —** Why? Well yeah, I wasn't sure at first but then I suppose partly because changing college half way through, doing the foundation year and then getting into my course. There's so much theory that I've done that this is a good chance to get experience, in industry before I finish. 1) to make use of what I've learnt but 2) to really know that if engineering's for me – if I can – if what's involved in it, if actually doing it in industry is something totally different – and you'll be able to find out if you're actually good at it, if you're interested in it. Erm, yes, it's to become a bit more aware – more prepared when you've finished your degree, basically.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you know where you're going yet for a placement?

**Subject —** I've just managed to get the job at [company], which is [company], placement. So…I just got that recently.

**Interviewer —** OK, what was it attracted you to that company?

**Subject —** Er, I liked the fact that they do a bit of everything in their production cells, as well as the commercial side and how engineering is kind of like – you know – how it relates to the business side of things. Erm, yeah it's all – but I thought – yeah, I think if I probably thought it was heavy engineering I'd be quite put off. You know, not necessarily because of the factory, but really – it's the hard core stuff – it would have put me off. But this, it incorporated a bit of both and I think it might - it eases me into it.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think will be the worst things and the best things about going on placement or what are you most worried about and looking forward to?

**Subject —** Well, I'm looking forward to the break. Erm, to doing something new, something different, even though it'll be in the same area but you're not going sitting, for an hour, just listening to somebody. Whatever, it's totally different. Probably – well, yeah it's change from [this university]. Erm, one thing I'm really worried about which, well I worry like anything anyway, is the fact, about when I'm actually in there, if I'll actually be good at the job, if I'll actually be able to do what's required, I don't really want to mess things up. That's the only thing that I'm really worried about at the moment

**Subject —** I'd like it to involve travelling, because I like going to different places. Erm, even if it meant like learning a new language would be fine and something with a design element to it. Sometimes that can really hit me hard so that is the only thing but otherwise I would say nothing because I did want to do the course, I did want to study mechanical engineering, and even though maths and physics are my weakest subjects and I don't really have a strong background in engineering, I've managed to do it. So, on that front, nothing's going to stop me but myself and you know, if I just doubt myself so…

**Interviewer —** OK, that's good. Do you think that the career prospects are good in your field of engineering?

**Subject —** I'd say so. The fact that every article I read says they're still crying out for engineers and oh yeah, because there's so much that engineers can get involved in. Erm, I think yeah there – basically if one door closes there's going to be so many others open and you'll be in the best position to have a peep inside and see if you like it or move on to the next door. And, yeah it's definitely not one of those that I can see being restrictive.
