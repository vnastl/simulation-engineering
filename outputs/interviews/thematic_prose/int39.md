## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): interest, family, hobbies, mother, father, careers, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** Right, that’s fine. Are either of your parents engineers at all?

**Subject —** Yeah, my Dad is, he works for the Gas Board – it’s kind of engineering-ish.

**Interviewer —** When and why do you think you decided to do engineering?

**Subject —** Er, it’s funny because when I started my ‘A’ levels, I wanted to be an Optician – that’s what I was going to go for but somewhere along the line I just changed my mind and when I was looking through all the prospectuses, I just decided really – to do it, cos I’ve always been kind of interested in making things and things – like practical things. (Yeah) So I just – yeah, I just decided and so I applied for it and when I applied to University, I applied for a couple of engineering courses, and a maths course and an English course but then I just decided that I wanted to do the engineering (yeah) so it’s just – I dunno really – it just happened (laughs).

**Interviewer —** OK. Do you think the fact that your Dad was kind of involved in the industry played any role or..?

**Subject —** I think so, yeah. Not directly but I think subconsciously it must have, it definitely made me want to do something like that, so yeah.

**Interviewer —** Did you have any careers advice or anything like that, when you were at school?

**Subject —** We did, but erm nothing – nothing that helped really (laughs).

**Interviewer —** Do you think that’s all to do with how men and women are brought up?

**Subject —** I think so yeah, yeah, because I used to like building things like getting a kit and making – so I like to do things like that, rather than play with dolls too much, do you know what I mean? Yeah I think that was – it must influence you somehow, yeah.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (5): practica, pleased, interper, supppers, groupwk*

**Interviewer —** Do you think that there are advantages or disadvantages to being a woman engineer?

**Subject —** Er, it’s good. Erm, it depends on you. It’s not – I found it good because I’ve always had more boy friends than girl friends, and so I’m just used to it and it’s not an issue for me or anything but I think you could feel a bit awkward because you are singled out because you’re the girl, but it doesn’t really bother me too much. But if you were a bit insecure I think that could really affect you because in every lesson it’s like guys and lady – they make a point of it – only trying to be nice (yeah) but that could affect it.

**Interviewer —** OK, has the course been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Mostly. I think yeah – because I’m doing a BEng, I knew it wouldn’t be as practical as the BSc’s or something like that, but most of the subjects have been fine. There’s a few, which are just new I suppose, different to what you’ve done at school and things like but yeah, it mostly is – it’s mostly been …

**Interviewer —** That’s fine – what would you say have been the best and the worse things about the course, so far?

**Subject —** I think – mm, the best thing I’ve had loads of experiences, like I’m being interviewed by you now (yeah) and I’ve been in the paper and things like that – yes, so that’s quite – they’re probably the best things, yeah, because I’m a girl and engineer I get a lot of opportunities and things like that – that’s good. The worst thing though was this year we had all our assignments – they were all between Christmas and Easter and so it was horrible. We had loads and loads on, so that was, I think, probably the worst time trying to – because you’re still going to work in the day (yeah) and then you’ve got to come home and do your homework on the weekends – I think that’s the hardest bit.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the lecturers treat male and female students the same? You’ve said that you sort of get singled out sometimes.

**Subject —** Yeah, I think so – I’ve never felt otherwise, but I know some people have said that they think – cos where I work there’s another girl on the course, and she said she’s had some of her lecturers, she thinks have been a bit sexist but erm, to me personally, I haven’t had that. I don’t mind if they say lady – cos’ they say ‘lady and gents’, I think it’s quite funny, it doesn’t bother me because if they just said ‘gents’ or whatever it wouldn’t bother me, but er no, I’ve never felt awkward about it really. Erm, one of our lecturers – I mean he just makes like women jokes, which are alright, they’re not offensive, but it – you just get bored with them, every lesson, do you know what I mean? But erm yeah, no, most of them are really fine.

**Interviewer —** Why not?

**Subject —** It depends who you’re working with. If you’re working with a group you like and they’re all working hard as well, that’s great, but I don’t like it because you can’t make them all work and they don’t all work as hard as you’d like, and so, sometimes you feel a bit let down by them and you know, if you’d done it on your own you could have got a better mark. I don’t like that side of it.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, industry, apptheor, noplaoth*

**Interviewer —** OK, do you use the skills that you’re learning in classroom at work?

**Subject —** No, not really (laughs). None of them directly apply. Erm, I think by doing what you do in Uni – you appreciate what’s going on in work, but nothing links directly at all, no.

**Interviewer —** Did you have a choice of which company you could go and work for?

**Subject —** No. No, what they did – I signed, I filled in an application form to go on network 75, and then they just sent me a letter then saying I’ve got an interview with [company], where I work now. And then – so I went to the interview and I got it, so I accepted that because I liked it. I know other people had a couple of different interviews in other places and I think because I liked it straight away and they accepted, that didn’t matter to me.

**Interviewer —** Yeah – you just thought you’d go for it. (Yeah) OK, you’ve probably touched a bit on this already, but do you think there are any advantages or disadvantages to studying part-time and doing the network 75 then?

**Subject —** Erm, the major advantage is you get paid (yeah) and you’re doing work that does kind of relate really, do you know – not directly but it’s engineering isn’t it, so it goes well, otherwise I’d have to work in a shop or a pub or something like that. So I’d rather do it – do it linked to it. Erm, yeah I like that – it’s like stability I suppose, isn’t it, a bit? I don’t like it when you’ve got lots of homework on and things like that. I find it really hard then cos after a day’s work you’re tired and you can’t be bothered to do anything. To motivate yourself then is really hard – so I don’t like that side of it.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think are the best and the worst things about being on the placement?

**Subject —** Hmm – ooh, the best is the experience you get because you get to know working life, what that’s all about and then, yeah, you get involved in different things and so I think that’s probably the best aspect of it, just – you are actually doing proper work (yeah) and it’s long-term it’s what you want to be doing as well. And the disadvantages – oh, I think because you come in as a new person and you’re not there all the time, that – they find that quite hard to give you like big projects or things like that because yeah, I think that’s quite an issue, from the work perspective. Because you’re only there a few days a week, they think oh we can’t give them that to do because that needs to be done yeah – so I think that’s probably the worse aspect of it, it’s sometimes you miss out on things because you’re not there.

**Interviewer —** Do you think it might improve your career prospects at the end?

**Subject —** I think so, because you’ve got a degree and you’ve got 5 years experience already before you’ve even done anything really. So yeah I do think so. And I think because I’ve had to do – deal with lots of different things, just throughout mixing them both together, I think it’s put me in a good standing for a job.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): carpath, workrole, furstudy, studarea, location, flexible*

**Interviewer —** Will you be an Engineer?

**Subject —** Yeah, I think I would still but sometimes I think I’ll stay where I am. Sometimes I think I could do teaching or something like that (yeah) and then, I’ve no idea really (laughs).

**Interviewer —** Ok. What would be your ideal career then, do you think?

**Subject —** Erm, probably working 3 days a week. I would like that. Erm, yeah, because you’re used to being – you’re used to the weeks being split up. When you have to do the summer, it’s 5 days a week and it’s really boring.

**Interviewer —** It is quite interesting what you said about it being part-time as well, because some of the people that I’ve interviewed – students at [name] University as well, that are going on the placement and some of them have talked about more long-term, if they decided to have a family what happens (oh yeah?) and stuff like that, and especially people in construction side of things, because it’s a lot of site work and that, they’re saying you can’t really work part-time (that’s true) stuff like that, so I don’t know what we’d do if we had kids and things like that (yeah) whether we’d probably have to go into consultancy rather than site work, so it is interesting that they are actually – even though it is quite difficult for them, they are doing it.

**Subject —** Yeah – no, I do think that. I wouldn’t like to work full time if I had a family – I wouldn’t like that at all. But erm – yeah – or even if – just after I got married or whatever, I’d quite like to work 3 days a week (laughs)

**Interviewer —** I’d quite like that too.

**Subject —** I don’t know if I could get away with it really, that’s why I think teaching because you get a whole summer off don’t you? So yeah, yeah, even though I like – I do enjoy work but I’m not like a career, career person. If I had a family and things I would stop but I wouldn’t – I’d work, I wouldn’t have to think oh I have to be a Manager or what ever, as long as I’m doing something I enjoy, it wouldn’t matter too much really.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that the career prospects are quite good in mechanical engineering?

**Subject —** I think so, because there’s such a broad range of things you could do, like at the moment its aeroplane engines, there’s loads and loads of different things you could do yeah. And then, like lecturing, and things like that you could do as well, so I think – and if you were willing to move, not necessarily abroad, but definitely around Wales – England as well, I think, yeah, I think there’s a lot of different opportunities really.

**Interviewer —** OK, would you like to stay local to here or…?

**Subject —** I’m a – I don’t mind either really. I’m not opposed to moving. I think really – realistically I realise that I probably have to move away, because it’s not – there a lot of places in [town], but it’s not like loads. There’s probably more opportunities elsewhere (yeah) so I don’t know really. Probably, I’d probably like to go away say for 10 years or something like that, but I maybe move back eventually, something like that I think.
