## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (3): challeng, knowledg, varied*

**Interviewer —** I guess that’s what you would say has been the worst thing about the placement?

**Subject —** I personally, I like challenges. I might scream when I see them, but once I’ve done them I get – I do appreciate them very much so and a lot of the time I don’t feel particularly challenged intellectually. I mean I know there’s a time constraint but I’ve never actually had to use my brain. I mean I had a resit in the summer and that was like the first time I’d ever done anything that was remotely what I’d call using my brain and I was – I was excited, oh fantastic, do some revision, whoo-hoo, use my brain, fantastic. I mean it just felt so good and it’s like well it shouldn’t be like that, surely? Really shouldn’t be.

**Interviewer —** Is that because this experience has put you off or…?

**Subject —** It has. It has in some respects, I think, just because I, um, it’s not how – I'm undecided. I probably – I like – the whole reason why I chose engineering – one of the reasons why I chose engineering was it was quite nice to do a degree and then be able to just go into a job that was actually to do with the degree, instead of doing Business and not really knowing where you’ll end up with something like that. And – so I always imagined myself doing engineering. I really don’t know what I’d do if I didn’t do engineering because it’s been in my head for that long. Coming out of this placement, it’s a lot – there’s a lot less engineering in it than I thought there would be, but I think – I think the way it would work is I would actually stay in engineering but go into a business role, because I think that you do need the engineering knowledge for some jobs, but you can sort of not do any of the technical stuff.

**Interviewer —** Do you have to do a lot of work explaining the engineering stuff to people that aren’t engineers or…?

**Subject —** No, it’s mainly – it’s mainly engineer to engineer here. I don’t think I've ever had to explain anything to a non-engineer. It’s getting used to the jargon that they use here, you know, like, ‘Ah, that’s a 50-mili bore such and such. Why are you using a road tool which is only 20-mili with a circumferential something or other or such and blah di blah’, you know. I mean it’s just ‘What?’ (inaudible) or a PCND or something, I don’t know, all this stuff they just come out with, they just (inaudible) I suppose if you’re in this industry it’s fine, but yeah. I think I’ve got one more word to think of, um… one more word about engineering… don’t know… varied, most definitely varied. It’s not all about technical, it’s not all about drawing, it’s not all about managerial, it’s not all about marketing, which I didn’t even think it was about marketing before, but it is.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (2): interper, groupwk*

**Interviewer —** Okay, we’ll come back to that sort of stuff later. Most of the questions I’ve got are about how the placement’s going and how you think it relates to your course and stuff like that. So how useful do you think that your course has been to the work that you’re doing so far?

**Subject —** Not at all really it has to be said. Um, mainly the one thing that I’ve found useful from the course has been, um, the modelling that I’ve been doing, um, using computer packaging, but even that was so basic that I – as soon as I came here I was told, ‘Well, you know what, you can do it that way, but it’s not the best way to do it’ and you know, when you get into industry things are a bit more complicated and you have to really think more about what you’re doing and you don’t get these simple problems that you get at university; everything’s a lot more difficult and detailed and – because at the end of the day you’re going to be making tooling from this that’s got to make that product, so it has to be spot on and things from down to drawing etiquette and technique, we’re following British standards and to, um, just the technical ability to be able to model the sophistication of the parts, it’s just a bit extreme. But other than that I’ve not – not really use anything from my course. I’ve been mainly doing things, um, to do with the business management and project management and sort of brand awareness, so that’s more marketing and looking at that. So I’ve been doing – chopping and changing between all sorts of different things. (inaudible)

**Interviewer —** Do you think that there’s anything like the more generic skills from uni that you’ve used, like communication and organisation skills?

**Subject —** I would say that they pale in comparison to the type of communication and, um, patience you need in a working environment. I’ve never met such a diverse amount of people and had to treat each one of them completely differently, because some people take things a little bit more seriously than others and some people, um, you have to be quite careful because if you don’t, I mean some people in here there’s a big joke, don’t go to this certain person because they will talk to you for hours and hours and hours and you’ve just got to be very tactful about the way you kind of say, ‘Well that’s nice, thank you for your information, but I really need to get on and do my work right now’. Things like that and doing presentations to the directors of the company and the directors of marketing and all this kind of stuff, it’s just nerve wracking and you don’t realise how much pressure gets put on you when you’re put in that sort of situation and you don’t know how you’ll cope until you actually do it. So it’s all about preparation.

**Interviewer —** Why not?

**Subject —** Because – because I know what’s waiting for me. I know – I know how much harder the course is going to get. I know exactly what I'm going to be doing. I know I’m going to be staying up til three o’clock in the morning doing crazy amounts of work and sort of running around trying to get from lecture to lecture and being challenged and completely not knowing what I’m doing. Whereas here, it’s a bit more, you know, you get told what you’re doing in a way. I mean it’s all about finding your own way in the workplace, but I don’t know, like… it’s difficult to explain. In some respects the university tells you exactly what you’re supposed to be doing and yet they leave you alone to do it, whereas at work you don’t get told what you’re doing but you have help to help you understand –

**Interviewer —** Do you have to do lots of group work?

**Subject —** No, I don’t do any group work at all. Despite being told at university, ‘Well, you know, you’re always going to do group work.’

**Interviewer —** Do you think it would be better if group work at university was organised more like that?

**Subject —** The thing that annoys me about group work at university is the fact that it is really, really haphazard about who you get put with and you can get put in a group with someone who doesn’t do any work and that automatically puts the onus on everyone else and your grades suffer because of it and that is really annoying. Um, if it was in industry then that person would get fired and I don’t – I don’t see the point really.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): placemen, experien, jobdecid, industry*

**Interviewer —** So, do you think what you’ve learnt here will help you when you go back to uni?

**Subject —** Definitely, definitely. It was an experience worth having and I’ve still got a couple of months left.

**Interviewer —** Okay, do you think there’s anything else the university could have done to help prepare you for coming on placement?

**Subject —** I don’t know. I think that’s the point of coming on placement, to learn how it is in industry. Academia is very different to the real world, very much so. It was a big shock coming from [university] where you’re told it’s a certain, you know exactly what you’re products are. You know exactly what you want, what you’re told to do. You have a project, you have a deadline, you’re told what you have to do by when and, you know, your given your group and everything. It’s just not like that in the workplace, and coming here and having my work rate shrink was just – I was sitting there going, all right, I’ve done this work really quickly, come on, give me something else to do and it’s like well no, you know. And it was probably that I hadn’t done it properly or something like that, but I thought I’d done it properly and one thing my boss pointed out was – I was given, um, a project in – at the beginning of the my placement, so 21st of June, and that project’s still going on now, which tells you the depth and scale of this project, and my first reaction was right, solve this project, two weeks, you know. Get a solution, find a solution, do it and then it’s done. It’s not like that, it’s a gradual process. It’s finding out this, finding out that, see how people react to this and blah di blah. That was a big shock, having to do that, because everything was so much – the – looking into the future is looking three weeks ahead, not looking five years, as it is in a company.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that there are any drawbacks to doing a placement, or do you think everything about it’s beneficial?

**Subject —** I would like to be given more responsibility at some point earlier than I have been given it. I mean like I said, it’s only – I mean how many months have I been here now? Eight – eight and a half I think and it’s only about two days ago that I’ve been given an actual D&D project and that’s only happened because someone’s gone off sick. Other than that, I’ve been passed off with what I call monkey work again. Sometimes I’ve been given stuff, but it’s more like sort of assembling data from various people, which is good on the people skills, though, of course. So, kind of more responsibility would have been nice.

**Interviewer —** Would you recommend someone else to do a placement here then?

**Subject —** No. No. No, I wouldn’t. If they’re anything like me they’ll want to be challenged and this place just doesn’t push you enough. I don’t think I’ve ever been in a position where I’ve actually sat down and gone oh my God, I’ve got this deadline here, I’ve got that deadline there, there’s no way I’m every going to be finished and then actually got through to it and done it. It’s been more like ah, I’ve got four weeks to do that, I can just sit here and do nothing for five days and then I can do that work in a day. More like that kind of thing, especially when you know you’re not going to be checked up on.

**Interviewer —** My first job was a bit like that. I had two weeks off sick or something and I came back, nobody had been doing my job but it wasn’t like I had to catch up on stuff. It’s like nobody’s missed you.

**Subject —** Exactly. Yeah, you do feel like that and I think I don’t feel particularly valued at all. I feel like Sunday and Sunday night when I’m up visiting friends or something like that, I think well, you know what, I won’t go into work tomorrow because you know what, no one will notice and if they did notice, the only reason why they would notice is because my clocking in card hasn’t been used. And I think that’s a sad position to be in. A very sad position to be in and that’s what depresses me so much about this place, is the fact that no one would notice, no-one does care, so.
