## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): interest, special, mathsci, family, varied, father, careers, nobody, discoura*

**Interviewer —** OK, What do your parents do are either of them engineers at all?

**Subject —** My dad’s an engineer. He did production engineering. Erm, he originally did an apprenticeship but then he got asked – you know a couple of people were changing to a degree, you know and he went on to do production engineering.

**Interviewer —** OK so when and how did you decide to do engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, it was actually – I’ve always been into – interested in the engineering side of things but the architectural side of things interested me more but I knew that I’m not particularly arty and I knew that erm that I wouldn’t be good at all as a pure architect or something like that. And it’s actually – erm I’ve sort of found a course and it’s kind of pretty unique and it just looked something I really wanted to do. I think my dad probably influenced me a bit at school (yeah) and that type of thing is one of the few places with engineering basis but I’m not so technical to go into something like mechanical (yes) so I decided to sort of go for it.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think you would have gone into engineering if your dad wasn’t an engineer?

**Subject —** Probably. Because I just – I always enjoyed maths, physics and design technology and it was always seemed to point towards that so, I think probably I still would have gone into it.

**Interviewer —** OK, once you decided that you wanted to do this did you have much encouragement or did your dad put you off at all or was he happy that you wanted…?

**Subject —** Oh yeah, I think he was very happy because I’ve, of course I was looking at all the universities and my dad being an engineering background, as soon as I mentioned [this university] was like definitely go to [this university], so I think he was very happy in the end, that I ended up coming here. Yeah, I got lots of encouragement.

**Interviewer —** Do you think then that there needs to be more information about what engineering is?

**Subject —** Yeah because I mean even when into my interviews and did the Moreby test and things like that, there were loads of things like accounting for me, and I was just like – you can do something like that. Engineering never even just came up for me.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): pleased, Mconfid, Fhelp, deadline, interper, supppers, groupwk, friends, variety*

**Interviewer —** OK. We’ll talk a bit about the course then, has it been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Yeah, I think so. I – I tell you what I really enjoy with my course – I really – I mean of course there are some aspects that I just don’t get on with, its like there’s a couple of the modules that I find hard, but because it’s something I know I want to do, I’m prepared to work for it, sort of thing. And I do enjoy myself.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think have been the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Er, I hate exams – that the really worst thing and er that was, like we’ve got six exams this semester. Erm, I’ve had a couple of modules, by a particular lecturer that have been really disorganised and I think that’s the worst bit – is when you sort of – even, if you don’t do the work fair enough, but when you actually put in the work and you’re sort of let down by a lecturer or something like that, that can annoy me a bit, but overall it’s been such a positive experience, I’ve really enjoyed it.

**Interviewer —** OK, Have you noticed any differences in the subjects that the women teach or the styles they teach – or like how they treat you? the female and male lecturers?

**Subject —** Not particularly. Well – the thing – apart from the one that we’ve got the design group with, the other 2 lecturers I haven’t had a lot of contact with them – they’ve just been lecturing and maybe in tutorials we’ve been doing work and things like that. But those modules I’ve got on with OK. Erm, I think it can work for them, because I think there’s one I’ve had a bit of – a few problems with a bit of course work where we had to email the lecturer and he said don’t contact me personally ‘cos I’m acting as a client – and I went up to him and he said, ah, and I said I’m Natalie, you’ve been having a few problems with me and he’s like, oh it’s so great to put a face to the name – he was really nice to me but I couldn’t help thinking that maybe if it was a lad who’d done it, he would’ve been like, no, I told you not to contact me. So I think it can work for you.

**Interviewer —** Are you bothered about that at all, would you prefer a female?

**Subject —** Not at all. He’s brilliant, I get on with him really well because I speak to him about the fact that I’ll be going away and he helped me out with the fact that I might miss a few deadlines, I had to schedule it round my deadlines and he was no, no, - he was really good.

**Interviewer —** OK, have you had to do much group work?

**Subject —** Loads, loads. It’s kind of – this semester, it’s kind of doing my head in a little bit, because we’ve only got one module where we’re not doing group work and – although – yeah, I do think it is a good thing, to have team work but at the same time I think it – sometimes it can reflect badly on those who have put loads of work in and you always get the ones who just go along for the ride. And I know they do peer assessment but it’s very hard, although they say, do your marks, put them in – it just doesn’t matter. It’s very hard, when you’re sitting there, in a group and you’re having your peer assess each other and say, well actually everyone got 10 apart from you – you got 5. It’s not (hard?) especially when they’re people on your course. And you’re gonna have – you know you’re gonna have another year and a half with them, or whatever. So…

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): placemen, experien, money, educatio, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, apptheor*

**Interviewer —** Ok, did you always know you wanted to do a sandwich course?

**Subject —** Erm, yeah I did, ‘cos when I was in my first year I – erm you can’t help but think – it’s like you get to go on the fact that oh my God, it’s going to be really hard to get a placement and I always wanted to do it because er although I really enjoy my course, I’ve never had that much sort of focus, I mean my course is kind of orientated towards getting on site and having a role on site but also having the architectural basis behind it, so it’s going in 2 directions. And I was never quite sure – I always wanted my placement year to actually use it as – almost like an experiment to actually find out what I want to when I graduate.

**Interviewer —** OK, fine do you feel like your ready to go on placement now?

**Subject —** I’m really looking forward to it (yeah) in fact I’m really looking forward – particularly because, I don’t think I could’ve done 3 years straight studying because even now I’m thinking I really want to get out there and actually use what I’ve learnt, in a work situation, because you don’t really get to do that. Erm I’m really looking forward to that one.

**Interviewer —** OK, why did you decide that you wanted to go on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, I don’t know – is it bad that it’s money orientated? I er – no, I think it was nice because I think I might feel a little bit restless if I did the 3 year degree and I think it’s nice that I’ll actually be able to get – get out and actually experience it and I’ll be able to come back in my 3rd year and go well, I’ve got all this input now, I can reflect it in my work, in my dissertation – whatever. So er, yeah, I think I – I don’t know I think it’s good getting out there and actually using what I’ve learnt.

**Interviewer —** When you were looking at the companies you applied to – not just [placement company], did you look at like the type of work that you would be doing and things like the training opportunities and …?

**Subject —** I did – well er my big thing was opportunities after I graduate. Because I think there’s loads – certainly, it’s quite a scary the statistic - I don’t know what it is – however many graduates can’t get work now, so I mean I think luckily being in engineering and they’re so desperate for engineering students, but at the same time I think that was one of the benefits. But I actually er I did a – I looked generally into the consultancies but er it was also management roles, such as project management roles and things like that, that I went into. I actually went into the (?) that did the engineering recruitment like (lost words) and things like that, and I actually managed to speak to quite a few people personally so I had a chance to find out what sort of – because industrial placements is kind of a – it’s very varied what you might be doing. You might be working all hours on a site and then you might be just picking up and making tea. So I did look at the criteria for what I’ll be doing and things like that. Although I haven’t got a specific erm – a specific role at the moment, I will be – I know roughly what I will be doing.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think will be the best and the worst things about the placement? Or what are you mainly looking forward to and what are you worried about?

**Subject —** I’m mainly looking forward to actually getting into the design process, being able to sit there and have my input on a building that might be built – that’s really kind of exciting. The worst thing will be making tea. It would be dogsbody – it would be, I hate to say it but a glorified secretary – I’d hate that.
