## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): interest, family, varied, nobody, discoura, whodisc, school*

**Interviewer —** So, he is in the engineering industry but– not in your field. Why do you think that you decided to study Air Transport Management then?

**Subject —** It’s a long story (laughter). I’ve always wanted to work in like just planes and stuff – they just fascinate me. I think mostly it’s the airports that fascinate me, so I just wanted to work in an airport whatever happened, so I thought I’d just take this degree because I was gonna do air traffic control, but because I didn’t pass the exams, I had to come to uni. I only had 2 options after ‘A’ levels. I said either – ‘cos I only applied to one uni – this was the only one I applied to - I said, if I get in, I get in, if I don’t, I don’t. And er, I applied for air traffic control – I failed that and erm looking into this, afterwards, I got into uni. So I thought I might as well.

**Interviewer —** Did you consider doing aeronautical engineering?

**Subject —** No. No I don’t like physics.

**Interviewer —** How did you decide not to go with them things, in the end?

**Subject —** I got input from my personal tutor at my ‘A’ levels. He used to help me quite a bit and I – I used to sit there, after school, basically just slobbing around, going oh, I don’t know what to do and that, so that’s why I chose standard subjects in ‘A’ levels, ‘cos maths, English, geography, is like just general subjects. So I didn’t do anything specific except chemistry in case I would go into that but I didn’t think I’d ever go in that. (OK) And then I had my support from my family. That was – in our culture we’re not allowed – girls are not allowed to travel and it’s like, obviously after marriage – and it’s like a girl thing, isn’t it? Like ladies don’t travel, they get married and have (?) nice life – but er, I had the support from my parents and stuff and they said you can do whatever you want.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that like in secondary schools, people should be provided with more information about…?

**Subject —** That is definite because when I started doing this – like my school didn’t even know about this course, and they didn’t know this thing existed in the first place, I must admit. Because they said out of all that – I think they were established for about 100 years – no student came out with it. I was the only one, so he was struggling but – ‘cos he lived in [place] and he worked at [name] Airport, and his wife was a teacher too, so he had a connection with [name] Airport, that’s why he knew about it. But like other teachers have no – not a clue that this course was even available or anything. I reckon – yeah, there is a lack of like information like I suppose. Because you get accounting and you get maths and you get all these chemistry and medical science and I think, engineering – in terms of engineering, you probably get mechanical engineering and stuff like that – building, but you wouldn’t get in terms of transport, they wouldn’t – I think, I guess it’s just a new thing now that’s just come up now, but you don’t really get information like that. Because I struggled – because obviously it was my research, that I did, with the help from my tutor, that I did get to this stage, but I wouldn’t have got to this stage because school didn’t have any information. I did it all on my own.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): pleased, assess, supppers, groupwk, friends, variety*

**Interviewer —** OK, I’m just going to ask you a few questions about your actual course. Is the course what you expected it would be?

**Subject —** At first I didn’t – I was not happy with it. I did consider to change it. But not changing in a sense from transport, just changing in terms of air transport ‘cos it’s a – ‘cos I didn’t have any luck in finding placements and stuff like that and it’s a bit hard. I thought I might have problem in finding a job later, because I was doing a degree to find a job later and if I don’t get anything then there’s no point. But now, now that I’m well into it, I’m quite happy with the course – it’s not, it’s everything I expected now, whereas like before we were like – ‘cos you don’t expect anything other than airlines or airports or air traffic and we had to do like roads and we had to do the vehicles and I was like oh no, I don’t know anything about cars –even though I drive a car. And stuff like that. It’s just like – you didn’t – you didn’t expect stuff like that. I didn’t expect logistics. I didn’t expect stuff like that – like economics. ‘Cos I used to hate economics and I was like oh no, it’s come back to haunt me. But yeah – now I don’t think it is – I like the fact that they’ve done it like that. It’s really good.

**Interviewer —** What do you think has been the best and worst things about your course?

**Subject —** Er, I think the best bit is having 50% course work, 50% exams, I think I’d rather do more course work than exams. I like that. The worst thing is we have do 12 modules a year. ‘Cos it means that I – even though it’s not especially hard, we’re doing 10 different subjects a year and it’s just so much ‘cos like at [name] uni they do 8 a year and that’s 4 each semester, we do 2 more than them and it’s just so much. And it’s like sometimes with our six exams a term and it just gets too much after it. I think they’re trying to compact so much in a year and if they could increased the – I think people would be happy to do like a 4 year course, rather than a 3 year course and getting everything stuffed in there. But I guess it’s a fun part of learning, isn’t it? But I don’t like the fact that we do 12 modules – not too keen on that.

**Interviewer —** Are you pleased that you did choose to come on this course?

**Subject —** I am. I have no doubts. I was saying this to my Mum yesterday, I have no doubts as to why I did this course or anything. I am happy. I think it will be hard to get in, but once you get in you’re just laughing because working in (?) you know where you’re going. You can find your way through upwards, wherever you want to go so I think once you get in, well it’s hard for every industry – it’s hard getting in I suppose. I don’t think it’s that different to anything else like – people make it out that it’s so different but it’s not – I think it’s the same.

**Interviewer —** Is that like from your past experience? Do you think that men probably make better teachers?

**Subject —** Yeah – I think – well I don’t say better teachers or anything but I guess in terms of our industry I think I’d trust a male lecturer more than a female one. I guess it comes back to the fact that male are dominating again – do you get it? But I don’t know. No, I’ve never assumed I’d be taught by a – like a female, because you’ve always got this image that – from like ages that lecturers are all male. So you’ve always got to have – I’ve never had a female lecturer.

**Interviewer —** In terms of yeah – academically. Has there been a person or an event that’s happened, or anything like that?

**Subject —** I guess my personal tutor has really helped me greatly. That’s good. At first I was finding it really hard ‘cos it’s different to move all the way from home and stuff. And before I used – I was just like a – I don’t know what you’d call me. Probably one of those really quiet people, so I had to come out of that (yeah) and to be able to do like I say, to be really big step for me. I would say my personal – in terms of like lectures, or in terms of like Uni, I think my personal tutor. Then I also have so much support from my friends and stuff, so…

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): placemen, placloca, noaccept, noplaoth*

**Interviewer —** OK, how did you decide not to do the sandwich course? Why did you think that you didn’t want to do that?

**Subject —** Well, I’ve been applying – since I started Uni, I’ve been applying for placements since then, even that 3 day experience that I got from [airport], it took me 2 years, because I applied first and they said no to me, and I applied again. I wrote them a letter and said, I really want to do it (yeah) and its been the second time, I’ve been turned down, can you please give it to me. So that’s why I got it but I thought from my first experience I start from the beginning. So I started writing around and stuff, like looking around, seeing if I could find them and I had no luck. I had written about 60 letters so far and I didn’t get any replies. I think I’ve only got 8 replies out of that, and they’re all nos – so… I did – ‘cos I really wanted to do a placement, out of the UK – not the UK, but I wouldn’t mind abroad, in the EU, or like out of London, because that’s where I come from and I didn’t want to stay in London at all, so I thought Glasgow, Edinburgh, somewhere like that – anything like that but not London because I didn’t want to stay there. So erm, but I didn’t get any luck because I did apply abroad and none of them – they all said no to me. I don’t know why, but…I think it’s just September 11th thing – that is what affected it, because when I started my course, that’s when it happened, so that was a factor – but I thought, in 5 years time terminal 5 will come on so I thought that’s the time I’ll come out of uni and I’ll be ready, so I thought I’ve still got a chance.

**Interviewer —** So it’s more to do with personality than the fact they’re male or female?

**Subject —** Yeah, yeah, I think it is personality that dominates quite a bit and I think the other – I lied before – my other reason for not doing a placement is ‘cos all my mates that I hang around with at the moment, they’re not going on placement. It’s like – I don’t want to stick around and be left around with the other people, so that’s the other reason too – ‘cos I think the main – other than that – that wasn’t the main factor though, but…

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that your course or your university experience prepares you for going out into the workplace?

**Subject —** I think it does – definite. I guess if I did have a placement, it would be good. It would be really nice because I’d come out with one year’s experience and I would be more attractive than someone who hasn’t done it, but at the end of the day, it’s like getting it in the first place – and er – but yeah, definite – at least you’ve got that knowledge, like you’re not going into it – like if I was going into an airline – if I’d did a degree in airline and I was going into computing, then I don’t have no knowledge of that so like this way at least if I want – because I want to go into airlines, at least that will give me a basic to it. At least I’ve got the foundations, so I think it will help and like, obviously it will help me in terms of like where I want to go, what I want to do and stuff, ‘cos some of the things I didn’t even know existed in the first place so obviously you’ve got that.
