## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): highsal, interest, knowledg, family, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** Okay. When and why do you think that you decided to do music technology?

**Subject —** Um, I wasn’t going to do it for years and then I applied for loads of courses and then realised that the only thing I wanted to do was music, so I had to come here to do it because they can’t do it in Ireland. So I said yeah, because I knew if I did something else I’d regret not doing music.

**Interviewer —** What do you think it is about music that appeals to you?

**Subject —** Um, the critical side I like a lot and also the producing side, so I came in wanting to be a producer but that’s slightly changed a bit, I don’t really know what I want to do anymore. Um, yeah, I still don’t really know what I want to do.

**Interviewer —** Okay. I’ll ask you a bit more about that later on. Do you think that the fact that your parents are both musicians influenced you at all?

**Subject —** Yeah, because I was taught music from a young age anyway, so I didn’t really, well, I had a choice, but I was going to learn music whatever and then, yeah, so they’ve had – they have had an influence on it because they’re both musicians.

**Interviewer —** Okay. Did you do any career research at all before you started?

**Subject —** Um, I kind of had an idea of what you could go into, like sound engineering and producing and stuff like that, but I suppose you don’t realise, well, with music how little work there is really until you come to university. Not how little work there is but how you’re going to have to do a lot of other things before you can get to where you want to be.

**Interviewer —** Okay, how did you decide to come to [university] then?

**Subject —** Um, I just applied to a few universities and – for music technology because not that many places do it, um, and these were the first people to send me a letter to come for an open day, so I came here first and I couldn’t afford to fly every time I was offered an open day so, I liked it here so I said ‘Why not?’.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): pleased, relevanc, supplect, supppers, groupwk, practwk, variety*

**Interviewer —** Okay. Has the degree been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Um… yes and no. It was – there was a lot of stuff involved in the degree that we weren’t made aware of before we went in, like a lot of multimedia, um, stuff that we – that nobody really wanted to do in the course, but we’ve done it now and – I’m in the third year and you get to decide basically what you want to do in third year, so you can just specify what you want, which is a lot better. But first and second year there was a lot of things that we didn’t really like.

**Interviewer —** Okay. What do you think have been the best and worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Um, the best things are working in the studio with other musicians on the performance side of things. Um, I love critical studies as well, which is just talking about music and arguing about it and… that was one of the most enjoyable ones because, I mean you get to talk about music with people who love to talk about music, that kind of thing, whereas your friends get bored when you talk about music all the time. So that was – that’s probably one of the best bits.

**Interviewer —** Okay, and what about the worst?

**Subject —** The worst, um, doing a lot of multimedia that I knew I would never want to do again. That I’d never – I wouldn’t use it again and I would never think about a career in that field but yet I still had to do it to pass.

**Interviewer —** How often do you get to choose?

**Subject —** Rarely. You get to choose kind of who you work with musically. When we had to do our compositions and stuff we chose people who we worked with musically, but, um, for other things, like we’ve a group project this year which is, um, a third of our whole degree, which we think is far too much because you’re relying on a lot of other people for a third of your degree. So, I don’t mind doing it, but as long as you have a good group, because if you don’t have a good group then they could take down a lot of your marks, which is slightly unfair.

**Interviewer —** Would you do anything differently if you went through it all again?

**Subject —** Um, I think if I’d known how much multimedia was involved in this I might have chosen a different course, but I’m still glad that I did it. They’ve got new courses now, like pop music and stuff, which I probably would have chosen had I known how much multimedia was in this course, but I’m glad I did it.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): placemen, experien, placloca, noappeal*

**Interviewer —** Did you have the opportunity to go on a placement?

**Subject —** Yeah, last year they, um, I could have been doing it this year and then come back this year again, but the placements they were offering us were completely nothing to do with our side of things. It was, um, it was basically a factory work that had small relation to electronics somewhere, but nothing to do with music or anything like that. So everyone – a lot of people didn’t do it.

**Interviewer —** There just weren’t the opportunities. If there had been something –

**Subject —** If there had been something which really interests me I think probably yeah, but the other thing was the cost, that we still had to pay for being in the University even though we’d be out working, and that put a lot of people off. And the fact that they’d probably have to move to London or somewhere like that, so it’d be more expensive.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that’ll help you when you go to get a job afterwards?

**Subject —** Yeah, definitely. I think experience is probably more important than a degree when it comes to my – music technology, but I just wanted to do the degree anyway. But, um, the experience is very important.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (3): carpath, workrole, salary*

**Interviewer —** Okay. Do you know what type of company you’d like to work for when you finish?

**Subject —** Um, I don’t really know. Um, I’d like to get into the business – the business side of things somehow, the industry side of things. But where I’ll start I don’t really know. I haven’t got a clue.

**Interviewer —** What sort of things do you think will attract you to a company? Will it just be the type of work that they do when you’ve decided what you want to do? Will you look at things like training opportunities and salary and stuff like that?

**Subject —** I think yeah, things like that. It will be the type of work I want to do, I think. I think salary, I mean it is important but it’s not what I’m going to look for. That’ll just be a bonus if it happens to be good, but yeah.

**Interviewer —** Okay. What are you most looking forward to about working in industry?

**Subject —** Um, I suppose doing what I want to do really. Um, I’d like to run my own thing eventually, to be able to make my own music and I’d like to do my own business side of things as well, but mainly just being able to make music, yeah.

**Interviewer —** Is there anything that you’re apprehensive about or anything?

**Subject —** Um, the money side of things because it’s going to take a long time to start earning money and then paying back debts and stuff like that. It’s going to be for a couple of years really hard to cope with, money, but I’ll be all right.

**Interviewer —** And work at the same time, or just…?

**Subject —** Yeah, travel for a couple of months and then work. Um, and I don’t know what kind of work I’m looking for. I think I’ll probably just work wherever and then think about a career when I come back here, but yeah I’m planning to travel next year.
