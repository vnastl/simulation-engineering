## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (1): varied*

**Interviewer —** So do you think that’s the biggest factor when you decide where to work or who is offering you the best package as it were rather than, I definitely want to go to this country or I definitely want to go to that country?

**Subject —** Um, both of them are important reasons. While they are offering you something, it could be – it depends on, oh how shall I explain it? They might offer you less but the cost of living there and having a better standard of life could be cheaper, so it depends. And there are some countries that I wouldn’t want to go, and also I, I’m planning to go to a country and stay there for a few years and then change to another, so …

**Interviewer —** So you’d like to travel about?

**Subject —** Yeah, and that’s why I chose engineering.

**Interviewer —** Yes, because it’s something you can do everywhere?

**Subject —** Yeah, it’s not similar to law or something like that that you have to stay.

**Interviewer —** I’ve got some questions now that are questions that I asked in the last interview but it’s not a test to catch you out, it’s just to see if your opinion has changed with the experience that you’ve gained. I’m starting off with the hardest question as well. (OK) What five words would you use to describe the culture of your field of engineering? I mean that you’ve been working in?

**Subject —** Mm… it’s analytical, it’s hard core, it’s, um, not well paid, it’s tough, um, it’s still quite masculine, yeah, and you tend to be more – something, um, something that I didn’t change my opinion is, it’s very different – engineering courses and art courses – for art courses you can have your own opinion and you can try to persuade people why you think that way and so on. With engineering it’s more a way to analyse and to reach the – to solve a problem – and it’s more, um, based on thinking than feelings.

**Interviewer —** Is that what appeals to you about it?

**Subject —** Yeah, yeah, I’m glad that it’s not too, all about feelings, like artistic, because, yeah I don’t think I’d like to work something like that. It’s nice but, yeah.

*Coded variables informed by this theme (0): none*

**Subject —** I’d say 90% not relevant.

**Interviewer —** Not relevant?

**Subject —** Because I’m doing transport, which is something that I didn’t expect that I would do. (Right) Um, um, what is useful that I’ve done in [university], it’s the way to approach the work and the way to analyse things. Um, so far what I’ve done is not very technical and I didn’t do any transport modules in [university], that’s why it’s not relevant.

**Interviewer —** OK. Do you think you’ll use what you’re learning now when you go back to your final year?

**Subject —** Yeah, I will. Um, in – study-wise there’s a module about highways that I think I should be more, having advantage over other course-mates, and my Excel skills that I learned here and, yeah, the way to organise things.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, OK. Do you think that it’s worthwhile doing teamwork at university? Even though it’s different to teamwork in the workplace, do you think that you still learn things from it?

**Subject —** Yeah. One of the reasons is there will be too much work if you work on your own, and it’s inter-personal skills that you get out of it from group work and you get to know your course-mate better as well.

**Interviewer —** Are you looking forward to doing the work?

**Subject —** Yes and no. I forgot most of the things from what I learned in uni, like, um geotechnics and water, it’s something that I forgot straight away when I started.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (5): placemen, experien, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry*

**Interviewer —** OK. How would you summarise your placement experience so far?

**Subject —** Um, there are ups and downs. Um, you feel quite good when you see a report issued with your name on it, but then you feel, oh, will they come back to me? And it’s – sometimes clients and the Government give you quite a hard time and they don’t, they could be fed up with work so they don’t speak really nicely and, yeah, but it’s a good experience that I know what the real world is and if I want to stay and work in England after I’ve graduated.

**Interviewer —** Would you recommend that other people do a placement?

**Subject —** Yeah I would. Um, you will be more competitive when you graduate and you could have contacts with companies and people, and you are not – you would be more, um, close to the real world. You are prepared for what you’re going to do when you’ve graduated.

**Interviewer —** Yes, they’re all the positive things. Do you think there are many disadvantages to doing a placement?

**Subject —** At the moment not really. Sometimes they tend to give me some easier work but I think no matter – probably because I’m placement or because I’ve no experience, so I think even though you’re a graduate, when you have no experience they’ll do the same. (OK) Oh, the only drawback is the salary. (Yes) It’s really, really bad!

**Interviewer —** OK. Do you think that the placement experience will help you cope when you start work after graduation?

**Subject —** Definitely. It’s – they don’t treat me very different from permanent so sometimes I have to consider myself a new graduate, apart from I’ll go back to uni life next year.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (5): carpath, workrole, salary, location, travel*

**Interviewer —** Both.

**Subject —** Well, yes and no. I like the company, I like my team, but I’m not sure if transport is the right career for me. (OK) Sometimes I do still fancy working in structural, but on the other hand it seems quite boring in structural because for the whole, I don’t know, three, four or five months you’re calculating the same bridge or same project, but with transport you jump from here to there.

**Interviewer —** OK. Do you think that you have changed from doing the placement?

**Subject —** Lots. Um, when I was in uni I thought I would like to do the MEng and I would like to work in the UK, and I wouldn’t mind to go to work on site. And I was only going to be either site engineer or structural engineer, and I thought I would choose no other career other than engineering, but now I’m not, I’m not sure if I will stay, if I will be site engineering, if I will stick with engineering.

**Interviewer —** Have you thought about what you might do instead?

**Subject —** Accounting. Yeah, possible. Or go into Government.

**Interviewer —** OK. If you did stay in engineering, do you think that you’d like a more managerial role or would you like to go down the technical or specialist route?

**Subject —** I used to think that I would be working in management side, but from seeing what my managers do I don’t think that’s very interesting. He has meetings, actual, actually every day, or sometimes two or three meetings in a day, and when I say sometimes customers or clients shout at us, I think he got even more than we do because he’s dealing with all of them.

**Interviewer —** OK, if [company] offered you a job, would you come and work for them when you’ve finished uni?

**Subject —** Possibly, it depends on how much they are offering. I need to talk about money now! There was once – I went to a career fair, it was a few months ago in [airport], and I had a chat with the IStructE, the executive, and I can’t remember what he was but I think he’s the chief executive of IStructE, and I asked him about the salary, before and after chartered and approximate starting salary. And he said that depends on which company you are working with, but if I really want to have an indicator I should go – refer to the back of the magazines – of the engineering magazines where they have the salaries. And I said, oh that’s – the figures are too discouraging. And he said, I know, I don’t know what we did wrong, for all the professionals. So it’s a problem about salary in engineering and it’s – I like what I do but it’s quite a turn-off when I think of what I’m getting, what I will be getting, and what I could possibly get if I don’t work in engineering.
