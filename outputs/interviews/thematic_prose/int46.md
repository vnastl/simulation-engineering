## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (12): interest, special, knowledg, family, hobbies, gooddeg, mother, father, careers, teacher, discoura, whodisc*

**Interviewer —** OK, what about your parents, what do they do? Are either of them engineers?

**Subject —** No neither of my parents, my dad works for [company] but both my Grandfathers were engineers, which I learnt afterwards.

**Interviewer —** So, when and how do you think you decided to do engineering?

**Subject —** Er, it was kind of secondary school. I think it was just ‘cos I liked building things. I always liked taking stuff apart and building things but because, at secondary school, they were all kind of well, girls can’t build things, I thought well, I’m gonna show ‘em. So I thought right, I want to start building things, so it just kind of got on from there. But it’s completely different now I’m doing the degree ‘cos it’s more thought, rather than actually building, but yeah.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think there’s anything particular that influenced your decision? Were your family supportive, did you have any careers advice –anything like that?

**Subject —** Erm, I think that what persuaded me, ‘cos first of all I was debating about – between accountancy and engineering but I think what swayed me, that engineering is such a good basis for anything afterwards (yeah) to start off with if you’ve got an engineering degree, because that’s kind of communication, maths (yeah), physics you’ve got in there – so that’s what swayed me to – to start. So you can go wherever I want, you know.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you do much career research?

**Subject —** Erm, I didn’t do much because I was pretty hell bent on doing engineering from secondary school. I just went on that, is it Kudos? Where it asks you questions and it comes up with what your ideal - and it came up with A levels for engineering and I thought well I’m not gonna argue with that, so…

**Interviewer —** When you were at school were you told much about what engineering was –– did you have any careers advice suggesting engineering is an option?

**Subject —** There might have been but not in engineering – they weren’t really erm enthusiastic about engineering. They were just – they tried pushing me towards teacher and something like that – and I said no, I wanted to do engineering. And I want to study physics and maths and people are like well, OK. And, like my sister does a law degree now, so mum and dad, so mum and dad say oh yes, one’s a lawyer, one’s an engineer and they go, engineer, really? You know it’s just – if it’s kind of mm, that’s the way people see it like that but…

**Interviewer —** Do you think that’s because people don’t really know what engineering is?

**Subject —** Er, yes. I mean I didn’t really know about it till I got here – I just thought like building stuff but the amount of thought that goes into it, and design and it’s just the whole like – thermodynamics and fluids, well…

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (10): practica, pleased, Fhelp, interper, supplect, supppers, groupwk, teachhrs, practwk, variety*

**Interviewer —** Ok, has your degree been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** No, I thought there’d be more building on it, but it’s opened my eyes to like loads more subjects and made me think so, well we might whinge a lot about the amount of lectures we have compared to all the other courses, they’ve all got reading week this week, and it’s then lectures, but at the end of it I know I’ll – I know I’ll be disappointed if I don’t do well so…

**Interviewer —** What would you say are the best and the worst things, so far, about the course?

**Subject —** Oh one of the best things was last summer when we had to build a glider on the course. We all, we all whinged ‘cos we had like 2 weeks off our summer holidays at the start, but it was a fantastic 2 weeks cos we had just like the run of the uni, while we were building it and it just brought everything from the year together. And everyone’s got – it was the first time probably everyone’s been relaxed so, yeah, really enjoyed that, and worst, erm can I say exams? (yeah) So yeah, it’s just the amount of exams that we’re getting – 7 and 8 – most of the people have got like – have only got 2.

**Interviewer —** Ok, have you got a personal tutor?

**Subject —** Yes, but I haven’t seen them.

**Interviewer —** Is there anything or anyone that you would say has been the biggest influence on you since you started the degree?

**Subject —** Well, there’ve been 2 really good lecturers. I guess the ad – one advantage of being female, on the course, actually, is that they know your name. It’s more personal, because er there’s [lecturer 1] who took us mainly for building and stuff like that – he’s been great and [lecturer 2] – they’re really good. [Lecturer 1]’s really – you can talk to [lecturer 1] about anything, so those 2 I think, would be the best, because I’ve hardly seen [female lecturer] but everybody’s encouraging.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that group work is good in terms of when you do go out into work, the skills that you’ve gained from it?

**Subject —** Oh definitely, I’d rather do group work than individual work on engineering any day. It’s just – because everyone understands different bits (yeah) you can just help each other, so definitely.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): placemen, wkexp, breaked, finuni*

**Interviewer —** OK, how have you decided not to go on placement?

**Subject —** Er, it was a kind of – after – in between sixth form and uni I worked during the summer and I enjoy working so I thought – my problem is that I know if I take a year out, I won’t get back into the education side of it. I’d be too wanting to carry on, so I’m just thinking well I should get a degree over and then start working, that’s the problem.

**Interviewer —** Do you think there are any particular advantages or disadvantages to going on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, I think the advantage of going on placement is that erm you’re closer to getting a job when you actually do come out of the degree. That’s one thing I’ve been thinking about and thinking oh I’m a bit – ‘cos I’ll find myself a bit in the deep end when I do come out, but for me, the disadvantage of going on placement would be to not be used to doing the degree again (yeah) and actually wanting the money.

**Interviewer —** Ok, I think we’ve said a bit about this one as well but is there any way that you think the course or the university experience could better prepare you for going out into the workplace?

**Subject —** Erm, just a week’s placement I guess. I mean, I know they tell you that you can do a year, but to me that’s just too long a time to be out the degree. A week and just like the holidays like, so just probably a week or day or something like that.
