## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (5): pleased, competit, interper, groupwk, variety*

**Interviewer —** Okay, what about the less technical parts of your degree, like communication skills and things like that? Do you think anything like that has helped?

**Subject —** I think all the presentations we’ve done and just generally communicating an idea through drawing has been very helpful and it’s used all the time, and also the fact that on my course, because there are so many different modules, you’ve always got lots of different things going on, whereas on other courses it’s just, say, one essay to concentrate on and in my job I usually have quite a few different things going on, so I probably find it easier to keep my – to get my head around everything and remember everything.

**Interviewer —** Do you have to use a lot of CAD at uni?

**Subject —** We’re encouraged to, but we don’t have – we don’t really have enough lectures on it. You know, it’s a very difficult package, like it’s renowned for being difficult to use even in industry. I don’t know why they teach it, but yeah – so I was almost dreading that aspect and now I don’t even notice it, and it’s not – there’s not as much sketching work, but I suppose that just depends on me. I probably could introduce that if I wanted to.

**Interviewer —** How do you feel about doing team work at work as opposed to team work at uni?

**Subject —** Um, I haven’t had anything – any bad experiences like I have at uni. At university it’s a bit more personal because it’s your own grades that are affected and so you do stress a lot more and you feel a lot more responsible for other people’s work and your own, and it’s – I think you feel a lot angrier at people when they don’t put their own effort in. I think at work you’d still feel annoyed when people don’t put the effort in, but you can get away with just doing your exact amount and no more, if you know what I mean.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that when you’re at work you have clearer roles within your team?

**Subject —** Yeah, I think you have clearer roles and a clearer amount of work to do each, whereas at uni they just kind of leave you to sort it out amongst yourselves, which can end up being unfair and more difficult.

*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, experien, money, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, finalyr*

**Interviewer —** How do you summarise the experience so far?

**Subject —** So far, well I’ve learnt a lot about working just in an office in a kind of – well the way to act and different ways to deal with situations and people and deadlines. Um, I’ve learnt some new skills, computer skills. I’ve learnt knowledge about manufacture, but I’ve also – I’ve also learnt things that I enjoy and I don’t enjoy, like – that I might not have picked up on at university, like liaising with people and sorting out problems that crop up, I really enjoy that and going round and speaking to lots of different people. And I’ve also found that I do enjoy having lots of different projects on because otherwise I do get bored when I’ve only got one thing to do. So yeah, I’ve also – I’ve – it’s not a job that I’d want to do for years and years like some of the people there have been doing, but even bad things like that – that’s probably a negative comment, but it’s still a good thing to learn now, I think.

**Interviewer —** We’ll come back to that later. At the moment what would you say are the benefits of the placement?

**Subject —** Benefits, the money is probably the main one.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that you have changed personally as a result of the placement?

**Subject —** Hopefully, I'm growing more confident I think in, um, putting across my own ideas and sticking by my ideas and what I’m saying in general. I’ve been told my confidence has grown.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (0): none*

**Interviewer —** Would you work for them when you finish if they offered you a job?

**Subject —** I think I probably would, but not for more than a year or two. I think I probably would just to – to keep, well, to have a job in the first place, because I think it’s quite difficult to just get a job straight after uni anyway and also I think I could still learn more things but I would – I would run out soon, because it would get repetitive after a while.

**Interviewer —** Do you think you’ll use what you’re doing now for your final year project?

**Subject —** Probably not, because I want… I want to – at the end of the final year I want to have quite a wide portfolio, so I want to show as many different things I can do as possible. So this year is a whole year’s worth of doing a certain type of, a certain type of design and product, so I want to do something different next year.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that your career ambitions have changed at all from doing the placement, because you were talking earlier about there’s things that you’ve realised you do like doing and some things you don’t like doing.

**Subject —** Yeah, I think it – it has shown me the pros and cons of the things on my course and my job at the moment and it’s highlighted certain things I enjoy and certain things I don’t enjoy and I’ve also seen – I mean there are some people who have been there for years who love it, but there’s also some people who have been there for years and really want to stop and they don’t like their job any more. That’s motivated me not to be like that. I don’t want – I don’t want to be in a job and complain about it and not doing anything about it. You know, be bored at work and not try and change it. I think it’s probably made me more ambitious than I was before and it’s also reminded me that I really need to – I need to work hard next year, but I also need to make sure that I get the jobs that I enjoy because otherwise eight – well, nine to five is such a long time to work if you’re not enjoying it.
