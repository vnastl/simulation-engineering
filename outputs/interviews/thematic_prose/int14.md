## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (14): highsal, interest, challeng, mathsci, family, hobbies, gooddeg, varied, mother, father, careers, teacher, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** OK, I'm just going to ask you then a bit about why and how you decided to do engineering. So, what do you think made you decide to go into engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, I don't know. I wanted to join the air force and then applied to be a pilot (right) and I was too short and I thought well if you can't – if you can't fly them, why not learn how they work and how to design them or how to build them you know, and it ended up carrying on from that really.

**Interviewer —** Right, OK, so in terms of things that influenced your decision, it was probably the type of job that you wanted to do, or that you weren't able to do – ended up leading you into it?

**Subject —** Yeah, as a kid I always sort of played with technic lego and stuff like that. And my Design, Technology I enjoyed and I got good marks and – and it carried on from there really, along with a couple of other things.

**Interviewer —** OK, so what about things like your family and careers advice, anything like that? Did that help you or…

**Subject —** Careers Advice was awful at the school.

**Interviewer —** And what about your family, have they been supportive?

**Subject —** Yeah, they're supportive of whatever I chose to do.

**Interviewer —** Yeah? OK, did you know any engineers to talk to, to find out what it is all about?

**Subject —** I have a very close family friend who is just a retired – retired chartered engineer (right) and he kind of gave us some advice, told us a lot about the fact that our course is a lot of mathematical stuff, and in the end you just put it into a computer and it's part of what – of what – a lot of what you do here is not what you do in industry. (Yeah) But, not an awful lot of people really, just the odd one or two.

**Interviewer —** What was it that attracted you, particularly, to mechanical engineering and say not civil or electrical or aeronautical?

**Subject —** Do you know I'd applied for aero (uh huh) for my foundation year and I didn't get offered that – I got offered mech eng instead. But I think it's a bit diverse. Again, you can come into whatever discipline you choose afterwards. It's not as specific as say aero where you couldn't then move into civils, things like that.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (11): practica, pleased, Mconfid, interper, supplect, supppers, groupwk, teachhrs, theory, practwk, designwk*

**Interviewer —** OK, what did you expect that the degree would be – like in terms of the gender proportions, or the type of skills that you're learning, or the type of teaching? Is it what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Erm, the male to female ratio was what I was expecting but I thought by now I'd have some sort of more hands on, you know, I think. Like I went to the motor club the other week, to watch a couple of my friends taking out an engine. And I'm like oh, while I'm doing all these things about engines and systems, I couldn't tell you what these things were, and I think that probably affects what I can do, in terms of industry – like if you've got a part and it needs to be supervised, taken out or whatever, and you don't even know what it looks like, how it works, all you've got is a triangle on a piece of paper and the guys there, obviously knew, it'd probably be detrimental. So probably a bit more hands on, more examples – realistic examples.

**Interviewer —** OK what's been the best and worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** The best bits have probably been the design projects (yeah) – realistic problems that need solving, but, at the same time, working in the teams for these projects has probably been the worst part. You know, trying to get people, especially the boys, to listen to anything that you're saying is difficult, even though they're the best part of the course.

**Interviewer —** Like using real life examples?

**Subject —** Like all these forces we've discussed and talks and that, all you see is a line on an OHP and what – you don't actually see a beam with these weights and dampers and stuff, and I think being able to see what they're actually talking about – it would help.

**Interviewer —** And have you had to go to them for anything?

**Subject —** Er, well yeah, I've had to get a couple of references off him, but every time I go, he doesn't know who I am or what I'm here for. Nothing personal though.

**Interviewer —** Who or what do you think has been the biggest influence on you, since you started your degree?

**Subject —** Phew, er, ooh, well we've had encouragement from a couple of the staff in particular (yeah) when you walk through the department they say hello, how you're doing? What are you up to? That kind of makes you want to, oh yes, I'll keep going, this is not too hard, stuff like that. There's been 1 or 2 individuals.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the group work that you do helps that communication side of things?

**Subject —** Yeah, but – oh yeah, I suppose it does but not to the same extent that they can – I'm sure they must do discussion sessions and a lot more of that. I suppose, even in our tutorials it's sit there and listen. It's listening the whole time. Yet, like I've not heard say more than 50% of my course speak, so you know if they speak, I'm like, oh, that's what they sound like - I'm sure that doesn't happen down in Social Science but…

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, apptheor*

**Interviewer —** OK. And, how did you decide to do that and why did you decide?

**Subject —** I thought it would be a good opportunity to learn what it's about, whether it's what I want to do (yeah) or whether it isn't. Yeah, just to get a feel for it, I think.

**Interviewer —** OK, why did you decide to go on placement?

**Subject —** Well, like I said before, just the opportunity to get a feel of what – whether engineering was for me.

**Interviewer —** OK, did anyone particularly encourage you or discourage you from going on a placement?

**Subject —** I think, speaking to a lot of my friends, they all said it was worthwhile (yeah) and the year they always – they thought was a very good year when – where they sort of grew up and it also gave them links and people speak to you afterwards – potential employers and stuff like that.

**Interviewer —** OK. Have what you've applied for and what you've been offered been quite different really?

**Subject —** Yeah. I don't know whether you'll find this interesting or not but I've gone for engineering positions and they've offered like a safety role or a production management role and they all seem to be saying oh yeah, you're doing alright, we're interested in you but not for the engineering roles, why not do something – well, it sounds a bit bad – but more female orientated, you know.

**Interviewer —** At the start of your course – I think a few people – I don't know on the mechanical so much, but I know a few people, particularly doing civil engineering, are sponsored from the start of the course, and then they go on a placement year with them and they've probably got a job with them at the end.

**Subject —** Yeah. I suppose I thought – that I think that I'm not 100% sure that this is what I want to do and signing on the dotted line meant that you would end up feeling cornered.

**Interviewer —** OK, you've probably touched on this already, but what are the things that you're expecting to get out of your placement? Other than putting the theory into practice. You've said you've found something quite – it's helped you like grow up, so is that the type of thing that you're looking for as well?

**Subject —** Yeah. Er, a bit of confidence, a bit more – you know – people skills and opportunities after the year.
