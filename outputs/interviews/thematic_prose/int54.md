## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): pleased, competit, interper, groupwk*

**Interviewer —** Ok What do you think are the main ways that work and university differ?

**Subject —** In a way the deadlines, like something - whether you’ve have a long time to work on it or something’s come up like when units have gone through (forced altitude?) you need to, that’s like when they have had to shut them down unexpectedly, and certain information is needed on the spot. Well obviously like at uni you do have coursework and things but you kind of know how long you've got to do it. Yes like I said the fact that with work you can kind of leave work at work. You can switch off, you can actually leave. You feel as though you can detach from the situation and come back to work refreshed. I think your focus is better then as well because you're not thinking about it all time. Also I think in the workplace it's a lot to do with how you interact with people. We do get the opportunity to do a lot of teamwork or group work at uni, but it's just not the same. I can appreciate what they were trying to do and I’d still encourage them to do it, but in the workplace it's a totally different system.

**Interviewer —** Why do you think that is?

**Subject —** I've thought about it, I think at university it seems like a real chore, a real hassle to work in a group, and at work it just seems like you've got this common goal, you are just trying to get each others ideas and obviously some people might know certain information and you need to retrieve that from them. And it does seem like you're working together like really with your aims in the same kind of framework. Yes the only way I can describe it in uni is like a chore. You think 'oh not group work again’ you think that can do it better in your own time by yourself, it would be great kind of thing. You don't have to be bothered with meetings and things like that whereas at work you've got to say (inaudible) in the same places, you do have other commitments, and it might be workplace, it might be outside but at work you can appreciate meetings more, getting together churning out your ideas, going away. Yeah.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that there is anyway that group work at uni could be redesigned to reflect industry?

**Subject —** I don't know. At uni there seems to be more of a competitive edge, everybody, even though you are working in a group, the competitiveness is there more than in the workplace and there's always someone trying to be the leader to lead the group, instead of how about everyone just taking even responsibility, so I can't think how you could implement that but from the group work that I have done, I think that’s probably one of the things that could make team work more effective.

**Interviewer —** Do you think you will get on better working in a group when you go back?

**Subject —** Yes. Well last year I found myself getting really frustrated at things, like trying to get your point across and then obviously if you've got someone that's really strong about their view about something. Whereas yeah definitely here you feel you can give your idea, it can be reviewed and it's a bit fairer. I still think there is competitiveness here, because you've got your production people and your commercial people and there's a bit of a battle between them 'Oh you haven't passed me the right information on’ and that kind of thing.

**Interviewer —** Ok. How would you summarise the experience so far then?

**Subject —** So far? I'm definitely glad that I've done it. I think I wouldn't have been able to take from it if I'd just stayed at university. Now I realise, I wasn’t of the idea of doing a placement anyway, but I think it's just as valuable as doing your degree and it's one thing knowing all the stuff, which is great, but being able to apply it and communicate that across to people is so important and even how you deal with things in certain environments where maybe previously you would have like got annoyed so it's a lot to do with how you respond to things, how you go about - yes personal things. And also it gives you more confidence, because a lot of the time like particularly at uni, well anywhere really, you always feel as though there's always going to be someone doing a better job than you are, and at uni I think the pressure really is there obviously because you really think that you're trying your hardest and maybe it's just the way that you're applying yourself to it. Yes you can really put the pressure on yourself a lot, and being here, in a way uses all your skills, not just what you've learned out of a text book or at a lecture, but how you are as a person, it's like you're using one hundred per cent of you more or less. The confidence that you can take from that when you're going back into your final year I think it will help a lot.
