# Interview INT06 — thematic prose

*The full answer to each recurring interview question, after probing — shown as the verbatim exchange and linked to the variables it informs.*

## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): interest, family, gooddeg, varied, careers, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** Ok, so when did you decide that you were interested in air transport?

**Subject —** Well, being an RAF cadet, I was kind of looking for a degree course and I actually found it through my boyfriend at the time, who was doing it, and it was a bit different from what I was looking at – I was looking at something geography based. It kind of really grabbed my attention actually, 'cos I was looking at all the engineering stuff he was doing actually, like how planes are designed and things like that, it was really interesting.

**Interviewer —** Did you ever consider doing aeronautical engineering?

**Subject —** No, I just, I'd rather do management, not really engineering.

**Interviewer —** Ok, what other factors do you think influenced your decision? Were your family supportive?

**Subject —** Yes they were actually, I want to join the RAF so an air related degree is quite useful for them. Yeah, no-one in my family's done anything like it. I've got a sister who's a lawyer, and my brother's a sailor.

**Interviewer —** Did you have any careers advice or anything like that?

**Subject —** Only from the RAF.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that more women should be encouraged or enabled into…

**Subject —** Yes, definitely. Yes, 'cos coming from a girl's school, most of the kind of leaflets we seem to get are like politics and English, and you don't really get that many engineering ones. Whereas the boys school next to us, were always going on about being engineers and aeronautical, so there's a big difference between the girls and boys schools.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): pleased, quality, supplect, supppers, groupwk, friends, theory, variety*

**Interviewer —** Has the course been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Yes, I think so. I knew it was going to be a management course. Erm, I expected it to be male-dominated. I probably expected a little bit more emphasis on aeronautical, planes and stuff, I'm quite surprised by er, we're doing a psychology module at the moment and marketing. You know, its like why do people want to travel? So I'm quite surprised by some of that. But I don't know really what I expected, that's pretty much it.

**Interviewer —** What would you say have been the best and worse things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Oh we've got this really cracking lecturer called [name], and he's been so enthusiastic, it's just like really good. I think it's the lecturers who make or break the course. We've got another one whose pretty boring. But yes, meeting everyone, you kind of get to know everyone and what they do. Most of the guys work in like the airports round here, so its cool to meet people who like what you do.

**Interviewer —** What about the worse things?

**Subject —** I can't really – I think – mmm, maybe the focus of the course. I think some people didn't actually expect it to be so management related because its in civil and building, they expected it more like, how a plane is built, because there's so much theory, they're a bit put off by it. But I think in the long run, its more helpful for a job.

**Interviewer —** You said you've got a personal tutor, a female personal tutor, have you been to see her at all?

**Subject —** No, I've seen her once. She's not actually anything to do with my course, which makes it hard. I'm not sure if she actually knows what my name is, she just calls me [shortened version of surname] (laughs). I normally go to the course tutor – if I've got a problem, he's really good about it.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the group work will help you in like a work environment?

**Subject —** Yes because the whole like team work and its like using everyone's skills, 'cos when you all come together, we all know different bits and pieces, so you can do it like that. Erm, yes I find it easier to work as a group actually, than on my own. I enjoy it more, just talking to people and finding out what they know, and what they know, and getting it altogether.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, finalyr*

**Interviewer —** Do you feel ready and prepared to go on placement at like this point in time?

**Subject —** Yes, it's just finding the placement to do it in. That's been the biggest problem for everyone on my course.

**Interviewer —** Have you applied to anything like that?

**Subject —** I've applied to [company] in [USA] I'm still waiting to hear on that one. I've applied to [company] in marketing and sales, and I applied to a company called [company] on Friday and then I found out that they've shed 540 jobs out of 600, so I don't think I'll be working there. But yes, most of the airlines, it's really difficult to get the placements. It's not what you know and how good you are, it's who you know and who can get you the placement.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that will help prepare you for the industry, or do you think they should be doing anything else to help prepare you for when you do go?

**Subject —** Yes, I could do with a little bit more help. I feel that, I do feel a bit daunted by the placement year. I don't know what to expect. I don't know if I'm going to turn up and make a complete mess out of it (laughs).

**Interviewer —** Ok, why do you think that you do want to go on placement?

**Subject —** For the experience it will bring. I think that if I decide to go into the RAF, and then come out maybe in 6 or12 years, I can at least go back and say well I've worked doing this, I'm not just RAF, so can you take me on that. And plus, if things don't go well, it's still the experience, and it will (show?) what I don't want to do whether, whether I've enjoyed it or not.

**Interviewer —** Ok, what are you really expecting to get out of a placement? In terms of say skills and what you're going to learn?

**Subject —** I think working in an office environment is going to be the biggest thing that, it's going to show me how to do it. Because, no experience I've done so far has been in an office, so I don't know what to expect, and you know, how people kind of work together. Team working and probably just getting used to working as part of a small team would be cool.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): carpath, workrole, travel, training*

**Interviewer —** Has anyone got a placement yet?

**Subject —** Mmm, my best friend applied for one and she got one with [budget airline], but its baggage check-in (laughs), which isn't much to do with our course. You know, its stuff like that. A few of the guys have got jobs already, which I think they'll carry on. But it's getting one that's really relevant. I want to try erm, consultancy, but I don't know if there'd be any placements.

**Interviewer —** After that have you looked at training opportunities and things like that, or salary or career prospects or anything like that?

**Subject —** Yes. Erm, the training programme, I'd actually like to travel, so world-wide would be nice, so I've had a look at that. Erm, other than that, not really.

**Interviewer —** In terms of your career then, where do you see yourself in 5 years time?

**Subject —** Hopefully in the RAF, somewhere in Britain or around the world, managing planes.

**Interviewer —** Is that your ideal career then?

**Subject —** Yes. I'd like to do a bit of management consultancy afterwards, but yes.

**Interviewer —** Ok, you've said that you'd like to travel, do you want to do that long term or?

**Subject —** Erm, I'm not sure. I think its more kind of while I'm younger, I'd like to see the world before I've got too many responsibilities.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the career prospects are good in what you're doing?

**Subject —** Yes, I think that for my course, you can do so much with it. Erm, there isn't just one thing that I can do either. I can manage, do logistics, supply chain management, sales and marketing. There's just so much I could do with it.
