## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): interest, challeng, family, gooddeg, mother, father, nobody, discoura*

**Interviewer —** Right, so I’m going to ask you a bit about why you decided to do engineering now. Is there any particular things, that stand out in your mind, why you chose engineering?

**Subject —** Er, cos I don’t feel that I can do business. When I see my father running a business, I find it quite hard er there’s not really a regular schedule. When anything goes wrong you have to go straight away. Probably it’s the same with engineering but I – I’d rather take the responsibility of engineering. And I found that more – erm satisfying things in engineering because I’d like to say I did this bridge, or something. I find it – yes.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you ever consider doing anything else like maybe social sciences or something like that?

**Subject —** I’d love to do politics and literature, but since I don’t apply to Hong Kong, after I’ve graduated I don’t think it’s easy for me to have a career on that path.

**Interviewer —** Ok did any other factors influence your decision to take up engineering – has your family been supportive?

**Subject —** Mm, no. Actually it was a friend of mine. I was thinking to do science subjects and he said he’s studying about engineering and that idea came across my mind and I started to think it could be a good idea. My family, they are neutral – my mother actually said it could be quite tough for a girl to do civil engineering, but, I think, or yeah, I’d like – yeah, I think I’d like the course so…and engineering discipline can go to business sector as well.

**Interviewer —** So you like that side of it?

**Subject —** I like the infrastructure, I think. I was thinking to do materials as well but I decided to do civil, that’s all.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): pleased, interper, quality, supplect, supppers, groupwk, friends*

**Interviewer —** OK, I’d like to ask you a few things about the course now. Has the course at [this university] been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Yeah, I really like it.

**Interviewer —** OK. What do you think have been the best and worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** It’s just I think I’ve learned a lot more but at first I didn’t know what to expect from the course. So everything’s quite a surprise and new for me. But I like it. Some modules they are quite boring and er, coursework they are quite hard but most of all I find it quite interesting and the lecturers they are willing to – to teach and they are patient.

**Interviewer —** Just one. Do you think that there’s any difference in the style of teaching between the male and the female lecturers?

**Subject —** I think the male lecturers tend to go faster you know – at a faster pace. Erm, and I think my female lecturer got a special way to lecture, so it’s quite hard to compare.

**Interviewer —** Has that helped you?

**Subject —** Yes, I think I’ve become a different person, or a stronger person.

**Interviewer —** Have you had any particularly bad experiences of group work?

**Subject —** Erm, sometimes my group mate will not turn up on time or they just disappeared. It’s quite annoying sometimes.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, when you’re working in the groups, with people you don’t know, do the men treat the women differently at all, to how they treat each other, do you think?

**Subject —** Er, I think they are quite fair on the mental work. On the physical work they might help you.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, experien, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, finuni*

**Interviewer —** OK, why did you decide that you wanted to go on a placement?

**Subject —** Because I want some experiences in the civil industry and hopefully I can get the job easier after I graduate.

**Interviewer —** OK, did anyone particularly encourage you or discourage you from going on placement?

**Subject —** My Dad told me not to go on placement. Yeah, he thinks it’s better to finish university earlier but I believe it’s better to have some experience.

**Interviewer —** How did you decide on the types of companies that you’ve applied to?

**Subject —** Erm, I’m applying to big companies (yes) because I like to travel around but I want to have something with company so that it looks better on my CV. And I will have experience working in the big company.

**Interviewer —** OK, what – what sort of skills and things like that do you think that you’ll get out of going on placement?

**Subject —** First of all I know what civil engineering is really like. Erm, some technical skills because being on site for a year will be – probably will learn more than staying on at university and yeah.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think – what will be the best and worst of like going on placement?

**Subject —** The best thing is to get (?). To know more people and to larger your network and know what to expect after you graduate, when you go into work. The bad side is it’s very hard to handle the situation at times, I think, especially on site because I really have no idea what will happen there.

**Interviewer —** Do you think you do want to be an engineer or are you hoping that the placement might help you make up your mind about what you want to do?

**Subject —** No, I do think I want to be an engineer, but I don’t know if I want to be consultant or contractor. And I think my placement experience will help me make the decision.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): carpath, workrole, furstudy, studarea, charship, salary, travel*

**Interviewer —** So you aren’t too bothered, at the minute, which of those that you’re going to or do you have a preference?

**Subject —** I would prefer to go into contracting. But then I am thinking, I don’t know if I can handle the situations on site and it’s – it would be a more relaxing environment, in consultancy. So I applied to contractors in the first place and then I applied to consultants.

**Interviewer —** OK, in terms of your career then, where do you think that you see yourself in 5 years time?

**Subject —** Er, I hope that I can be Chartered, or I’ll carry on and an MSc programme after the undergraduate.

**Interviewer —** OK, what about more long term, say in 10 years time, what do you think you’ll be doing?

**Subject —** Definitely being chartered. Hopefully I can be senior engineer or be responsible for quite large project and I can travel around.

**Interviewer —** OK, that’s good. Do you think that the career prospects are good in your field of engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, I wouldn’t imagine it is hard to have a job but I really think that the salary is not very competitive (OK) because engineers are – they put in a lot of hard work. Erm, the starting salary’s good but after being a senior engineer, it’s not going very far.

**Interviewer —** OK, well that’s everything I have to ask you. Thank you very much for taking part.

**Subject —** And being a civil engineer, you might travel quite a bit and it’s very hard work but you might have to concentrate in your career totally, well having a family you have responsibility for your family and then I guess every female does – you start to think which is more important. And if you think your career is taking up too much time, then probably you might leave it. Or probably more simple, if you’re in contractor and if you’re pregnant, you just won’t go on site, and then after you leave for a year, then you probably just let go.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, when you’ve been trying to decide whether to do the contracting or the consultancy have you been thinking about things like that or is it not an issue at the minute?

**Subject —** Not at the moment but eventually I think I’ll go into consulting because I wouldn’t imagine myself, at age 40, climbing up and down (laughter)
