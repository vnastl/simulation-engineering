## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): interest, challeng, knowledg, varied*

**Interviewer —** OK, when and why do you think you decided to do engineering?

**Subject —** I really don't know. Loads of people ask me and I let – they say when did you decide you liked it – and I just say, I don't know. Because I can't actually remember sitting down and thinking, oh I wouldn't mind doing that. (Yeah) But I can't remember when and I can't remember why. I just got into it – I just did, that's all. It's a bit different I think and not what people expect of a little girl.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, what do you think it is that appeals to you about engineering?

**Subject —** I don't know. I think it is because it's – for a girl it's very different and I think there's always something different you know, like there's never two days the same or anything like that. I think it's just challenging all the time, I think that's why I'm enjoying it, because I don't get bored.

**Interviewer —** OK, did you do any careers research or did you know any engineers to talk to or anything like that?

**Subject —** My step-dad was –worked at [company] and his brother, but other than that I didn't really know anyone and I sort of spoke to them and I think it was because they (?) so exciting, because they were on the engine of a plane, and I think I just got excited about that. (Yeah) But other than that I didn't really know anyone or do any research. I sort of researched into apprenticeships in the Army and Corus and things like that, but other than that I never really looked into it that much, I just thought yeah, I want to do that, I'll have a go at that.

**Interviewer —** OK, why did you think that that company would be good for you?

**Subject —** Erm, I think because they were quite – well I thought, at the time, they were quite small (yeah) and they were local cos' I can't drive, and I just thought it would be interesting because I had never thought of – I had always thought, I think, of engineering as cars or big aeroplane engines and – and then I went down there and seen it's not all like that, there's other spectrums.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): practica, pleased, relevanc, interper, supplect, groupwk, friends*

**Interviewer —** Yeah, are there any other girls on your course?

**Subject —** There's one other girl, there is one. I was quite surprised. She was quite surprised to see me as well but – and we get on quite well. We sort of stick together when they're all ganging up on us.

**Interviewer —** OK, that's fine. Has the course been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** I dunno because some of it, yeah, and then other times I just think no, because I thought it would be – the course itself would be a lot more practical but it's not that – there's only one subject that we do that we do any practical work in. And there's like two modules that I wouldn't generally associate with engineering, which is management and communication and I wouldn't have thought of doing them as part of an engineering course but they've turned out to be alright – I think I did quite well in them, so…

**Interviewer —** OK. Are you pleased that you chose this course then?

**Subject —** Yeah, I am quite pleased that I did.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the lecturers treat the female and the male students the same?

**Subject —** Yeah, I think they do pretty much. I think – yeah, I think all our lecturers this year wouldn't have cared – they just saw the person (yeah) and they didn't – it was like well you know, you're not having an extra couple of days because you're a girl, or – and I think that was really good because they just got on with it. They didn't see you as a boy or a girl, they'd seen you as just who you are.

**Interviewer —** OK, have the group projects that you've done gone quite well?

**Subject —** Yeah, I think the video we had about 70/80% in and the management project, I think we had about 60/65 in. So yeah, we've been quite good.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, industry, apptheor, wkexp, noplaoth*

**Interviewer —** OK, what appealed to you about doing the Network 75 programme?

**Subject —** I think it was – I can remember last year struggling for money a bit. I thought the money would be handy, plus I'd have 5 years experience that someone sitting next to me in my exam might never get (yeah) and I just thought it would be different – it's not just sitting in a Uni doing nothing (yeah) and I just thought, yeah, the experience is going to be handy, because even if I don't necessarily get on with that company, I've still got the experience – they've taught me to move somewhere better, or bigger or whatever.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that if the Network 75 thing hadn't been available you'd have done the course full time or…?

**Subject —** Yeah, definitely I would have done it full time. Yeah, I think I definitely would have – I wouldn't have gone and looked for someone to employ me while I was work – er, studying. I don't think I even would have done a sandwich year or anything like that, so yeah, I probably would have just got my head down and done it.

**Interviewer —** Yeah. That's interesting. How do you think that being at work differs to being at University?

**Subject —** I can't stay in bed all day (laughs). It's just a lot more – I think it's prepared me for like getting out of bed a lot earlier, because I was – last year I think I was proper into staying in bed as late as possible, and now I've started to wake up about – if I'm going to work by about 9 O'clock anyway. So now I should be active. And I think it's just prepared me for the real world – I've seen, taken off my rose tinted glasses and seen things for how it is. So yeah, it's been quite good.

**Interviewer —** Do you spend half the week in Uni and half at work?

**Subject —** Yeah, I do two days in Uni and three days at work.

**Interviewer —** OK, are you happy with all like the career choices that you've made so far? (yeah) Would you do anything differently if you went through it all again?

**Subject —** Sometimes I do, when it's a cold, rainy morning and I'm out of bed and at 7 O'clock I do think, oh I wish I was just going to Uni today, but I don't ever seriously sit down and think oh why have I done this? Why am I doing this to myself? Because I think, at the end of the day, it's all going to be worth it, because I can walk into an interview and even if somebody next to me has got a better degree I've still got 5 years experience that they haven't got. So, yeah, I'm quite happy with everything I've done really.

*Coded variables informed by this theme (2): carpath, travel*

**Interviewer —** OK. In terms of your career then, what do you see yourself doing when you finish the course?

**Subject —** Erm, I'm not really sure. I think I'd still like to go into the aerospace industry (yeah) because that's what I started with but I'm not sure how I want to go into it yet. Like I said, I'd like to set up my own business and build planes and have my own airline and service them, or – but I need money for that (yeah). **Subject —** I dunno, it seems to be getting better now, but it can just rise and fall so quickly (yeah). Erm, it is quite good, especially if you get a qualification to go with it but if you don't have that, you've just got to work really hard to get to it. So – I dunno, I think they are quite good.
