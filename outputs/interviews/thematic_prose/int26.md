## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): interest, knowledg, family, mother, father, careers, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** So when and why do you think you decided to design and technology?

**Subject —** Well, I wasn’t even going to pick it as an ‘A’ level, because we were the first ones to go through the new ‘AS’ levels, so we had to pick 4 instead of 3, so I had to pick a 4th one, so I picked design ‘cos I liked drawing and stuff and I liked doing design, and I’ve got a an A at GSCE, so I thought I’d do that one as an extra one, and I can drop it after a year, but I ended up dropping biology instead, ‘cos I really like design. And then erm, I was looking for a universities that was something I could do, and I was thinking about doing sports science, but then I didn’t realise that you could do design tech, ‘cos we hadn’t been told anything, and I looked it up and found that you could and I don’t live far from here, and I come here a lot ‘cos I used to do running, so I used the track a lot, and this was, I think, one of the best unis for doing industrial design so I decided to do that instead because I thought it was what I enjoyed more than, ‘cos I enjoy it more than doing like sciencey things.

**Interviewer —** Did you have much careers guidance or anything?

**Subject —** No, nothing really at school.

**Interviewer —** What about your family, have they been supportive?

**Subject —** Yeah, my Dad’s really into it because he likes all drawing and making things and stuff, with him being an engineer as well, he always helps me do it.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): curricul, pleased, competit, Fhelp, interper, supppers, friends*

**Interviewer —** Has the course been what you expected it to be?

**Subject —** Its been a lot harder. Its been a lot more hours than I thought it’d be, its like 24-7, just working. Like I’ve got lectures most of the day, and then I’m working at night to do the stuff that they’ve set us in our lectures, its just non-stop, and it’s a bit more electronics based than I thought it would be, but apart from that there’s not much else really.

**Interviewer —** Well it started as a bad thing, then went to a good thing.

**Subject —** Good things are its very interactive. We don’t usually have just lectures in a lecture room, there’s usually about 20 of us and we’re all talking and that, so you get a lot more friends from it, and it’s a lot more like interactive, you get a lot more out of it. And, I like that at the end of it we’ve got something to show for it, we don’t just have an essay, we’ve got like a portfolio of work, and then products or whatever, so we’ve got something to show for what we’ve been doing.

**Interviewer —** Do the male lecturers treat the male and female students the same?

**Subject —** No, erm, not like really badly. Some are fine, but there’s some that always try and help you if you’re a girl which is a bit annoying.

**Interviewer —** Not approachable then?

**Subject —** No, no I wouldn’t ever approach him for anything. There’s a few of them who are really nice, who I’d go to before I go to him about anything.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, experien, money, educatio, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry*

**Interviewer —** Do you feel like ready to go on placement?

**Subject —** No, not at all, I’m really scared.

**Interviewer —** Ok, did anyone particularly encourage you or discourage you from going on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, I wasn’t gonna go, but I decided to go and then last year when I was in my first year, I thought oh I dunno if I want to go anymore, because our course is so competitive and I just thought I’m not gonna get on, as they’re all so competitive, and they kept saying there’s only like 20 out of a 100 people that usually get placements and things, and I just thought, oh I don’t know if I even want to bother, but my mum and dad were just like do what you want to do, or they kept saying that I could just come home for a year and just get a job if I wanted a rest from the course as it is really, but they were just saying do what you wanna do really. My boyfriend wanted me to do it, but that’s ‘cos he failed his first year, and wanted me here for his last year.

**Interviewer —** What do you think are the advantages of doing a placement then?

**Subject —** I think it will just help you get a job more and it will help, I’ll know what its like to be in a full-time job and what sort of work places do and things. I think it’ll help – look good on my CV.

**Interviewer —** Did you apply to anywhere else?

**Subject —** No that was the only one. It’s the best paid one out of all the placements as well, so I was like yes I’ll take that.

**Interviewer —** What sort of things are you hoping to get out of the placement?

**Subject —** Erm, money? Er, Just some experience, because I think that’s what I’d rather do, something like, but I don’t think I want to do the full on design, doing the design consultancy thing, I think I’d rather do the research, or like the marketing side of it, so I’m quite happy that I got this one ‘cos its not just all designing, so I think it’ll give me a lot of experience, and I’ll see if that is what I really want to do.

**Interviewer —** Ok, what do you think will be the best and worst things about going on placement?

**Subject —** Erm, the best would be the money, cos I’ve got none and I really need to pay off my overdraft, and that I can still stay in [university town]. Worst – at first its going to be horrible meeting everyone, and not knowing anyone. Travelling’s not going to be nice, ‘cos its ¾ of an hour everyday there and back. But I think I’ll enjoy the placement, and just doing the job will be quite fun – its just something different, isn’t it?

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (4): carpath, workrole, salary, travel*

**Interviewer —** In terms of your career then, where do you see yourself in say five years time?

**Subject —** Erm, travelling. I want to travel after uni for a few years. I don’t want to get a job! After uni, I want a break. I think I’ll get a job for a bit to get some money, and then I wanna go travelling, and then I want like, I’d like to do a marketing job after that and be the head of a big marketing firm, I don’t know.

**Interviewer —** Is that your ideal career then?

**Subject —** I don’t think I could go that far up, I don’t think I can tell people what to do very well, but no I’m not that strong-willed I don’t think.

**Interviewer —** Would you like a job that involves travelling then?

**Subject —** Erm, yes I would, erm I don’t know, yeah I think I would yeah. I don’t want to stay in England, it bores me staying in one place for too long – I’m really bored of [university town] already.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that the career prospects are good in the design and technology industry?

**Subject —** No, there’s not many jobs I don’t think, and they’re really like competitive and really short-lived if you do get one I think ‘cos they’re that competitive, they’re just looking for new people all the time, who can do better so, I think it’ll be quite hard to get a job, but I want to go more down the marketing route I think, so I don’t think – its not as worrying.
