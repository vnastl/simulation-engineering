## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): challeng, mathsci, knowledg, family, varied, father, careers, discoura, whodisc*

**Interviewer —** Everything’s experience. What do your parents do, are they engineers?

**Subject —** My dad used to be an engineer, my mum does various things. She’s got her own company but she’s also a school inspector.

**Interviewer —** OK when and why do you think that you decided to study engineering?

**Subject —** Erm, when I was at school, the careers people said what are your favourite subjects? I said maths and physics, and they said, OK look at engineering and I said, no. I’d never looked at it and I’d – it just didn’t interest me, but one day I looked at a prospectus and it looked it really good. I also did EMTA engineering course for girls, that kind of confirmed that mechanical was the better one for me.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that you ruled engineering out, to begin with, because you didn’t really know what it was?

**Subject —** Definitely. I’d never looked at it before – I just for some reason, I thought no. I just didn’t know enough about it.

**Interviewer —** OK, what factors do you think did influence your decision so was it like careers advisors and careers literature, that was obviously quite a big influence – what about your family, were they supportive?

**Subject —** Yeah, they supported me whatever I wanted to do because even when I wanted to be a vet.

**Interviewer —** OK.

**Subject —** Erm, I quite like the design side of things but I don’t actually enjoy drawing things, I just like to be able to design it, if I was good at drawing (OK) I’d like that side of it. Problem solving as well, I guess.

**Interviewer —** Do you think they are going to say that?

**Subject —** I have known engineers say that to me, yeah. On the job with the dashboards – they’re saying you’re stupid, don’t do engineering.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (6): practica, relevanc, interper, quality, supppers, groupwk*

**Interviewer —** And what have been the best and worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** Erm, the worst is having lecturers that can’t speak proper English. When you can’t understand. Like the Russians – they have to be the worst understand and listen to. They can put you to sleep in less than 5 minutes.

**Interviewer —** There must be something.

**Subject —** Erm, I suppose there are some good lecturers around that you can just sit and listen to and just take it on board. There are some good ones so – Group work tends to be quite good as well, if you get the right group. I’ve had mostly good groups actually.

**Interviewer —** And what was the sort of mix of that group – male and female?

**Subject —** I was the only girl. I think that helped actually.

**Interviewer —** Oh right, OK.

**Subject —** I kind of know her in a different light. She’s a really good tutor – she’s probably a better tutor than a lecturer. (OK) But I’ve also got another tutor, ‘cos she’s off on maternity leave at the moment, which is why I didn’t have her last year because she was having the first child.

**Interviewer —** Yeah.

**Subject —** No, not at all. Er, the whole atmosphere is a kind of step between school and work but it’s neither one or the other.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, do you think that you’ll get an opportunity to put what you’ve learnt at university, into practice more?

**Subject —** I think so. I don’t know quite how ‘cos what we do at university doesn’t seem like engineering to me, or actually worthwhile at all but apparently it does actually come in useful, so…

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (8): placemen, experien, money, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, apptheor, placothe*

**Interviewer —** Why do you think that you decided to do a sandwich course then?

**Subject —** Again, the experience. I’ve got a friend who’s gone straight to her 3rd year and she’s really struggling to find a place – (really) all companies are saying that she hasn’t got that year of experience, so she is struggling. So I’m glad I’m actually going to do it.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you feel prepared and ready to go on placement at this time? Like you said, after the first year people were probably still a bit immature and it wouldn’t have been right to go on placement at the end of the first year probably, but do you feel like you’re ready now?

**Subject —** I am now but I don’t know whether I was last year – for the first time in Part B, I don’t think I was quite there then but I – I suppose I’ve got more spare time now, so I do feel more ready now.

**Interviewer —** And have you had any interviews yet?

**Subject —** I’ve had 3.

**Interviewer —** OK and you’re waiting to hear from them at the minute?

**Subject —** 2 of them have turned me down, 1 I’m waiting.

**Interviewer —** OK, what are you really expecting to get out of your placement?

**Subject —** Erm, money and a bit of experience in the working atmosphere.

**Interviewer —** Is that – is that one of the reasons then why you are doing this year out, to sort of confirm in your own mind whether engineering is or isn’t for you?

**Subject —** No, I’m probably doing the year out – partly for the money, but also it gives me a bit more creditation, sort of on my degree. That’s probably the only reasons (OK) because it’s actually a scary thought going out to work for a year.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (1): carpath*

**Interviewer —** OK, that’s fine. In terms of your career then, where do you see yourself in 5 years time?

**Subject —** Probably not doing engineering.

**Interviewer —** Really?

**Subject —** I don’t know it depends how the year out goes. If that inspires me then yeah, I’ll stay, but other than that, it’s a degree at the end of the day.

**Interviewer —** OK, what do you think then would be your ideal career?

**Subject —** I don’t know I keep changing, so I haven’t really got a definite anymore. It used to be designing cars so that erm I’d sort of walk down the street and someone drives past in a car you’ve designed and somebody’s bought, and you’ve liked something I designed, it’d be cool. Erm, I suppose that’d still be the thing, but I don’t think I’ll actually get there.

**Interviewer —** Why?

**Subject —** Partly it means design work – which I’m no good at – but I just think it’s too competitive and I don’t think I’ll actually get there.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that the career prospects are good in engineering?

**Subject —** No. a lot of people struggle to get a job after – when they finish, no matter whether they’ve had a year out or not and there’s now a lot of people that can’t find a job.

**Interviewer —** Maybe in the fact that it is quite male dominated. Do you think that’ll have any impact upon you, in choosing what you want to do, if you do want to go into the industry?

**Subject —** The companies that I tend to go for don’t really have an issue with male domination. For instance [company] it was – it seemed to be half and half. There was no bias one way or the other so it didn’t become an issue. So I can’t imagine that I’ll stay in the company very long if it is an issue but at most companies it’s not.
