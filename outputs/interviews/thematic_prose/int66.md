## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (3): pleased, interper, groupwk*

**Interviewer —** Yeah, OK. Do you think that university and work have different goals and priorities?

**Subject —** Mm. I don’t know. I think that the thing I’ve found a little bit tricky with work is that I don’t have set targets – you know with uni how you’re just working to your final exams or you’re just working to get the piece of coursework done. Work, it’s like a day by day basis and so I mean if I’ve got stuff to do I have to write it down, I have to have like my little targets for the day, because otherwise I don’t really have a focus, and I end up going off at a tangent doing completely the wrong job or something like that. But I think, um, yeah, I think uni’s better in that respect because you’re working, you’re always working towards the goal of exams and things like that. But then it’s easier on yourself at work because at the end of the day you go home and you’re home, really, whereas at uni you’ve permanently got your exams on your mind and stuff like that. So, yeah, I think, I think working’s nicer because you don’t have that permanent pressure and stuff like that, so.

**Interviewer —** OK. What does your job entail doing?

**Subject —** I don’t think I have a specific role. I do bits and pieces, like, um, I just sort of – because we’re working on a major project at the moment, I’m just given little bits and pieces to do. Um, it’s varied from drawing issue, management side of things, right through to sort of more technical stuff. I’ve been working on some sub-stations at the moment. But there’s no particular focus or role at the moment. But then to be honest at the start of it I said to my boss, you know, who unfortunately isn’t here, I said to him, look, this year is for actually like discovering what I want to do when I actually graduate, because I love all aspects of my course, I didn’t have a particular focus. And I mean I’ve been here six months now and I’m already focussing towards the management side of things. But it’s nice because I’m getting lots of different tastes of lots of different things, so I don’t have a particular role.

**Interviewer —** Do you think that it is worthwhile doing group work at uni then?

**Subject —** I think so, yeah, definitely, because – I mean a few people that I work with, especially when we do, um, coursework with other courses, I think that really helps. Because I had to do some coursework with a couple of civil engineers and the technical stuff I was absolutely useless on but they were really good on. Whereas the management side of things, the organised time side of things, I was better on. So it’s great because you actually bring in all these different elements and so, um, yeah I think it’s definitely beneficial. The only problem I have is the way they put the teams together. Because they just stick you in alphabetical order and stuff so you end up working with the same people over and over, so. But it’s definitely beneficial.
