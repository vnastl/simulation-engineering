## Route into engineering — why they chose it, and who influenced them
*Coded variables informed by this theme (13): interest, special, mathsci, knowledg, family, hobbies, gooddeg, mother, father, careers, nobody, discoura, school*

**Interviewer —** Yes, that’s OK. So when and why did you decide to do this?

**Subject —** It was completely random. Erm, no actually I was thinking of doing engineering, of some description anyway, because of my ‘A’ levels. Something scientific but I didn’t want to do chemistry, so I thought right, what can I do? You know. First of all I thought like doing – like chemical engineering, but when I looked into it, it’s not actually anything to do with chemistry much at all. It’s like sort of architecture really – so it sounded more like building plants and I just thought no that sounds horrendous. And then I happened to be wandering past the stalls for materials engineering, when I came round to look round Loughborough – to look at like the chemical engineering. And I picked one of them up and I thought oh I could do this actually. It looks quite interesting, so I ended up doing it.

**Interviewer —** Did you do much research into careers then?

**Subject —** Yeah, well one of the reasons I chose materials, was because it said that they need more people in it and I thought, well if they need more people, then I’m more likely to get a job at the end of it. There’s no point spending 3 years of my life learning about something you find is useless.

**Interviewer —** Yes. Have your family been quite supportive of you?

**Subject —** Yeah, well my mum just thinks it’s hilarious because like, in my house I’m really like known for not being the brightest of sparks. I’m a bit – I’m a bit busy. Like I’ve been – I had to go on a random trip for [company] once, and I had to sit on a bus for like an hour because I’d left my (?) like – I had to go to the end of line to pick it up (oh no). My mum says – I was on the phone one day telling her what I’d been doing, she said, what are you doing girl, because I’m not – I don’t think I’m really an engineer, to be honest.

**Interviewer —** Yeah, do you think that the reason why women don’t do more engineering type subjects is based on false perceptions of what engineering is?

**Subject —** Er, I don’t know – most people I know just chose their course because they – it just appealed to them because of the subjects they’d done previously. It’s only an extension of them, the one’s that they enjoyed. So it just depends, but I suppose as well, a lot of it – it probably – it’s the physics because well, in my course I didn’t need to have physics, I got in on the fact that I did chemistry and maths but a lot of the other engineers – because you need physics and not that many girls do physics at ‘A’ level (yeah) so they haven’t the right ‘A’ levels to start with.

## Experience of the engineering degree and learning environment
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): pleased, relevanc, interper, quality, supppers, groupwk, friends, theory, practwk*

**Interviewer —** OK, what, what is it do you think that appeals to about the course that you’re doing?

**Subject —** Er, it doesn’t – a lot of the time, but now I’m doing it, I’m sort of stuck with it. ‘Cos talking to other people, they don’t like their courses either. Too much learning for the sake of learning and they’re just rubbish, but in reality you just look it up in a book, if you wanted to learn certain facts and figures but I like – I do like some of the modules. A lot of it depends on the lecturer. If you’ve got a good lecturer, anything can be interesting can’t it? But the actual - I do quite like the actual materials, but you’re learning about different packages and stuff.

**Interviewer —** What would you say are some of the best and the worst things about the course so far?

**Subject —** I like – I quite like the labs actually. It’s a lot of work for what they’re worth – the lab and parts and stuff. So I do quite like actually doing them.

**Interviewer —** So you don’t regret doing the course that you’ve done?

**Subject —** Not really. I mean it’s – as far as course go it’s alright – it has it’s moments of utter boredom but then at other times it’s pretty good, so.

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that your educational experiences differ from other students, like in Social sciences or Humanities?

**Subject —** Yeah, in – because a lot of ours is still like being in school a lot of the time. Especially, in first year, with having such a small group, it was sitting in a classroom and being taught, and you could ask questions, whereas a lot of the social sciences – they’re such huge groups that are just in a lecture theatre and there’s no contact involved between the lecturer and he’s just – my business modules were like that. Well, actually, I used to talk – (that was the difference between the rest of the engineers?) - they all hated it – what was it? - marketing and management of human resources and they were like we’re all engineers, why do we have to be? But I quite liked it, obviously, choosing to have done management anyway. You know, so they’d all just laugh when I used to answer questions and got involved – I quite liked it.

## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): placemen, experien, money, educatio, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, finalyr, placloca*

**Interviewer —** OK, do you think that at this time now – you feel ready to go on placement now?

**Subject —** Erm, I don’t know whether I’m ready for my placement. I’m just I don’t want to do my – I don’t want to do my final year, next year. That is more how I’m viewing it. I want to do something different. And as well if – ‘cos you have to do a final year project, ‘cos if I’ve done some time working in industry, I’m hoping that would help – give me some connections maybe and just give me more of an idea how the industry does work, then I’ll be able to help – er maybe it will make it easier to do my project. If you want information out of people, you’ll know more how to get it.

**Interviewer —** So you’re hoping that the industrial experience will help when you come back for your final year?

**Subject —** Yeah. (Ok) Just gives me a bit of time away from exams, because you get a bit fed up of them when you’ve being doing them since you were 12.

**Interviewer —** Do you think there’s any other reasons that the work placement can be a good idea?

**Subject —** Because I don’t really know what I want to do – because like our course is materials and management, I suppose, in theory, I could go and manage but – but I don’t know. You think when you’ve had a – when you’ve done the course that maybe – that maybe you should look at going into industry first and like I say, there’s loads of people at the moment doing actual management courses and (lost words) anyway. So I don’t know. I’m hoping it will give me more idea and I mean industry as well, its such as vague thing to say, because within materials I could go into copper bits or metals or ceramics or polymers, then within them you can go in like – there’s so many different branches, I’m hoping it’ll at least allow me to cross something off and it’ll mean I definitely don’t want to – give me some more idea of what it is that I actually do want to do because at the moment, I just don’t know.

**Interviewer —** Ok, that makes sense.

**Subject —** And the money will be useful as well.

**Interviewer —** What do you think will be the best and the worst thing about going on placement?

**Subject —** The work will – it’ll just be the fact that it’s going to be 9-5 everyday. But, at least you’ve got your weekends again. You don’t have anything hanging over your head can go home every weekend and it’ll just – it’ll be nice to just be in a completely different environment and just the fact that everything’s gonna be new. It’ll be learning about the whole different way of – how it all works and meeting different people earning some money, and being one step closer to actually being independent. Because at the minute you kind of play at being independent because well, I’m – I’m probably am more than the other people, because my parents just – they stick the money in the bank for me, for my rent and everything else so I get all the freedom without any of the responsibilities. So, next year if I’m on placement, I’ll have to be doing it all myself.

## Future career intentions and what matters in a job
*Coded variables informed by this theme (9): carpath, workrole, furstudy, location, workenv, people, travel, training, promot*

**Interviewer —** Do you see her as a role model at all?

**Subject —** Well no, well yes, sort of, in that I suppose she’s good for proving what you can achieve if you set your mind to it, but I don’t want to do that anyway – I don’t want to stay in academia, because I just don’t fancy that. I want to go into actual industry maybe or I might even not do engineering at all – I might manage something. I don’t know, I haven’t decided.

**Interviewer —** In terms of your career then, where do you see yourself in 5 years time?

**Subject —** I don’t know. I really don’t know. I could be, you know, in a little lab somewhere breaking things. Er, I don’t know, I could be travelling by then. One of my cunning plans is that I can, if I do my year in industry, decide what it is I do want to do - do my final year, finish university, get a job in whatever it is I’ve decided I want to do it in, in one of these graduate scheme things, which are usually about 2 years. Once I’ve finished all the training, and earned some money along the way, say right, I’m going to go travelling now. Go travelling for, I don’t know, a year at the most, but I’d probably do a bit less.

**Interviewer —** Would you like a job that involves travel?

**Subject —** Yeah, I wouldn’t mind working in different countries actually. In fact I might even just do that and not actually travel and just sort of have little sort of holidays – proper travel travelling. But yeah, the cunning plan was that we’re then travelling a bit, because once you’ve got the training, then, they’re more likely to say, well you know, we’ve invested all this money in you, then we’ll have you back sort of thing.

**Interviewer —** Erm, what would be the ideal career, do you think?

**Subject —** Well, I think it’ll have to have people in it somewhere because I generally like being around people, a lot of the time. Erm, I’m not very sure doing what – I wouldn’t just want to be – I don’t – it needs to be a job that’s got a lot of variety in it. I couldn’t do the same thing every day. I couldn’t – I couldn’t just be in an office but by the same token I wouldn’t just want to be, you know, going round all the time because although it might be different sites, they’re gonna be essentially the same. So, whatever it is it needs to be fairly varied. (Yeah) And not something where you get stuck. It has to be something where I could like, progress a lot. I fancy getting – you know – a relatively good job by the end of it.
