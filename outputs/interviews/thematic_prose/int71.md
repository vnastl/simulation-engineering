## The industrial placement — whether and why to go (or not)
*Coded variables informed by this theme (7): placemen, experien, jobprosp, jobdecid, industry, perdevel, apptheor*

**Interviewer —** Do you feel like you’ve been given an appropriate level of responsibility?

**Subject —** Mmm, I mean my jobs, they’ve been doing quite industrial placements in my department for I think it’s 7,8,9 years, so it’s quite a long time. Each year it changes slightly and you start off with very little responsibility and then it grows and grows and grows depending on what you feel like, depending on what’s going on. So I mean the first, up till Christmas I was doing, I always had someone with me when I was out on the site and then after Christmas it was, it got to the point where I was doing monitoring on my own, well for the easier things and where I got to know what I was doing, so I had those sort of responsibilities. And like the sites’ Jamboree, which we have just done now, that was solely my responsibility to, I was just put into a team of three and all of us IT’s [industrial trainees], we were just told to do a stand for the Jamboree. So my responsibilities are growing all the time and different things like that. And I have got a job on at the moment looking at (inaudible) depleting compound chemicals around the site and that information that I am gathering will report directly to senior level management, so things like that, my core responsibilities are growing and I think it does develop with the time and if I feel that I am being sort of buried or feel it’s too much, they, [name] will take it off at any time.

**Interviewer —** It’s quite good knowing that you’re not the only new person

**Subject —** Yes, so that’s quite good. And if you ever have any problems, there’s housing people on site that, and you’ve got HR that are you can always fall back on, so it’s got the structure and everything to help you as much as possible, so it’s not too daunting. Erm, I also think that you need the experience to get a job nowadays. You need to have at least a year’s experience and if people, if you just go through uni and through school, and don’t actually go and see the real world, you live on cloud cuckoo land half the time. Erm, and sort of getting that experience I think is a very good base. Especially for the future ‘cos you learn so much which you wouldn’t learn at uni, but you can just then take back to uni.

**Interviewer —** Even if you don’t come back to work at [company]?

**Subject —** I think it’ll, one it will help me get a job, having such a big reputable company on your CV, can’t be a negative thing at all. Erm, and also I think it’s also, the knowledge and sort of hands on experience that I’ve got in the different fields, that I wouldn’t normally have got at uni, erm had I just gone straight through, erm, I just would not have got the sort of experience and sort of getting to know the different places where you can send your waste, sort of different companies, getting to know them. Erm, its just a general knowledge.

**Interviewer —** Do you think it will help just in terms of settling into a new job, having been in a work environment?

**Subject —** Yeah. You know how things roughly work. I mean, yeah different organisations have different structures and obviously you’ve got to get used to them but at the end of the day, if you’ve worked in a big company with lots of different management structures, most places aren’t going to be as big, there’s only a few companies which are as big as [company]. So, erm, if you can survive in a place like this, you can survive anywhere, that’s my thinking anyway.
